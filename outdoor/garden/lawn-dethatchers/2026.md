## Summary

The 2026 dethatcher market splits cleanly into four product categories. **Corded electric walk-behinds** (Sun Joe AJ801E, Greenworks 27022, Worx WG850, Earthwise DT71613) dominate suburban yards under 10,000 sq ft — Reviewed.com, Family Handyman, and BestReviews all converge on the **Sun Joe AJ801E** (~$160) as the consensus pick for affordability and effectiveness, with over 15,000 Amazon reviews (4.4-star average) making it the most-purchased model in the category [src2, src6, src7]. **Cordless 40V models** like the **Greenworks DHF410** (15 in., ~$250 tool only) are gaining ground but reviewers still warn they lack torque for heavy thatch loads — Bob Vila lists Greenworks as an "Upgrade Pick" rather than the overall winner, and Reviewed.com still recommends sticking with corded for serious work [src1, src2]. **Tow-behind dethatchers** are the only sane choice above 1/2 acre: the **Agri-Fab 45-0295** (48 in., 24 tines, ~$111 on sale) wins on durability and value while the **Brinly DT-480BH-A** (48 in., 24 tines, inline-wheel design) is the only top-tier tow-behind compatible with zero-turn mowers [src3, src5]. **Manual thatch rakes** (Ames 2915100, Walensee 15-inch) remain the right answer for spot work and lawns under ~1,500 sq ft [src1, src4].

The single most important spec is working width — 13–16 in. for walk-behind electric, 40–48 in. for tow-behind — because dethatching is a 3-pass job (two perpendicular passes plus a cleanup pass) and time scales linearly with width. Tine count (typically 18–24 spring-steel tines for tow-behinds, ~45 zinc-plated tines for the widest electric Earthwise DT71613) and depth adjustment (3 positions on most models, 5 on the Greenworks DHF410) are secondary [src1, src3]. **Dethatching is seasonal**: cool-season grasses in early spring or early fall, warm-season in late spring after green-up. Dethatching during dormancy or summer heat damages turf [src3, src8].

## Head-to-Head Comparisons

### Sun Joe AJ801E vs Greenworks 27022
The two default corded walk-behinds. The Sun Joe (12-amp, 12.6-in. path, 5-position depth, 8-gal bag) is the consensus best-overall and the most-purchased model in the category; the Greenworks 27022 (10-amp, 14-in. path, 18 stainless tines) is wider but lower-powered and is frequently out of stock in 2026. [src1, src2, src7]

**Pick Sun Joe AJ801E if:** you want the proven default, the included collection bag, and 5-position depth — the safe pick for any lawn under 10,000 sq ft.
**Pick Greenworks 27022 if:** you specifically want the 14-in. width and can find it in stock at a lower price.

### Sun Joe AJ801E vs Greenworks 40V DHF410
Corded vs cordless. The Sun Joe (~$160) is cheaper, always-on, and never runs out of charge; the Greenworks 40V DHF410 (~$250 tool-only) trades cord-tether for battery freedom but costs ~55% more and tops out around 5,000–8,000 sq ft per charge. Reviewers still rate corded torque higher for heavy thatch. [src1, src2]

**Pick Sun Joe AJ801E if:** you have an outdoor outlet, want the lowest price, and don't want to manage batteries.
**Pick Greenworks 40V DHF410 if:** you already own Greenworks 40V batteries and value cordless mobility over price and runtime.

### Agri-Fab 45-0295 vs Brinly DT-480BH-A
The two 48-in. tow-behind heavyweights. The Agri-Fab 45-0295 (~$111 on sale, 24 spring-steel tines, 70-lb weight tray) is the durability/value winner for traditional lawn tractors; the Brinly DT-480BH-A (~$202, inline transport wheels) is the only top-tier 48-in. tow-behind that handles zero-turn turn radii without binding. [src3, src5]

**Pick Agri-Fab 45-0295 if:** you tow with a lawn tractor or garden tractor and want the best price-per-inch.
**Pick Brinly DT-480BH-A if:** you mow with a zero-turn (ZTR) — Agri-Fab does not warrant ZTR use.

### Earthwise DT71613 vs Sun Joe AJ801E
Both corded electric, but the Earthwise (13-amp, 16-in. path, 45 wire tines, ~$190) is the widest corded model here and digs deeper, while the Sun Joe (12-amp, 12.6-in., ~$160) is cheaper and the consensus all-rounder. [src1, src3]

**Pick Earthwise DT71613 if:** thatch is heavy (over 3/4 in.) and you want maximum corded width and depth.
**Pick Sun Joe AJ801E if:** thatch is light-to-moderate and you want the lower price and the bundled bag.

## Decision Logic

### If lawn is under 1,500 sq ft or thatch is patchy
--> **Ames 2915100** (~$45) or **Walensee 15-Inch** (~$35). A powered dethatcher's setup, storage, and noise overhead aren't worth it for occasional spot work. [src1, src4]

### If lawn is 1,500–10,000 sq ft and you have an outdoor outlet
--> **Sun Joe AJ801E** (~$160). The category default — most-purchased, consensus pick across Reviewed.com, Family Handyman, and BestReviews. Step up to the **Earthwise DT71613** (~$190, 16-in. width) only if thatch is severe (over 3/4 in.). [src1, src2, src6, src7]

### If lawn is 10,000 sq ft – 1/2 acre and you want cordless freedom
--> **Greenworks 40V 15-Inch DHF410** (~$250 tool only). Bob Vila's upgrade pick. Caveat: the tool-only price is ~$250 (≈1.6× corded equivalents), and battery runtime tops out around 5,000–8,000 sq ft per charge — buy a second 40V battery if covering more. [src1]

### If lawn is over 1/2 acre and you own a riding mower / lawn tractor
--> **Agri-Fab 45-0295 48-Inch** (~$111 on sale). Top tow-behind pick across 3 of 4 reviewed sources, and currently the best value in the roundup at 35% off list. If you mow with a zero-turn, swap to the **Brinly DT-480BH-A** instead — Agri-Fab's tow-behinds are not designed for ZTR turn radii. [src2, src3, src5]

### If you mow with a zero-turn mower
--> **Brinly DT-480BH-A** (48 in.) or **Brinly DT-40BH** (40 in.). The inline transport-wheel design is the only top-tier tow-behind that handles ZTR turns without binding. Agri-Fab explicitly does not warrant their dethatchers for ZTR use. [src5]

### If thatch layer is under 1/2 in.
--> Skip a dedicated dethatcher entirely. Routine mowing at the right height + a vigorous pass with a regular leaf rake is sufficient. Dethatching unnecessarily damages healthy turf. [src3, src8]

### Default recommendation (unknown lawn size)
--> **Sun Joe AJ801E** (~$160). Safest pick when you don't know the user's lawn size — covers ~80% of suburban use cases (under 10,000 sq ft, walk-behind, corded). [src2, src7]

Reviewed.com still explicitly recommends sticking to corded models for serious thatch removal because battery torque drops under heavy load. Expect this gap to close by 2027 as 60V/80V platforms (e.g., Greenworks Pro, EGO) move into the category. [src1, src2]
- **16-inch corded class establishes itself**: The Earthwise DT71613 (16 in., 13 amp, 45 wire tines) and similar 16-in. models are pushing past the long-standing 13–14 in. corded standard set by the Sun Joe AJ801E and Greenworks 27022. Wider cuts cut a 5,000-sq-ft job from ~3 hours to ~2.25 hours. [src3]
- **Brinly's inline-wheel design owns the ZTR niche**: With zero-turn mowers now ~40% of new ride-on sales, Brinly's tow-behind line — designed around inline transport wheels for tight turns — is the category's only ZTR-compatible top-tier option. Agri-Fab still leads 48-in. tow-behinds for traditional lawn tractors but explicitly does not warrant ZTR use. [src5]
- **Combo lawn sweeper + dethatcher attachments expanding**: Brinly's STS-42BHDK-A (42-in. lawn sweeper with integrated dethatcher) and the new 54-in. LSDT-54BH combine two passes into one — appealing for autumn fescue/Kentucky-bluegrass overseeding workflows where you dethatch, sweep, then seed. [src8]
- **Manual thatch rakes still hold the under-1,500-sq-ft segment**: Despite the rise of $130 electrics, Bob Vila's small-lawn pick is still a $45 Ames manual rake. Setup/storage/noise overhead of a powered tool isn't worth it for spot work. [src1, src4]

## Important Caveats

- Prices are approximate U.S. street prices as of July 2026 (Amazon verified). Sun Joe and Greenworks corded models in particular fluctuate $20–$40 around major lawn-care holidays (Memorial Day, July 4, Labor Day) on Amazon. As of this update, the Greenworks 27022, Brinly DT-40BH, and Ames 2915100 show as currently unavailable on Amazon, the Agri-Fab 45-0294 lists no consumer buy-box price, and the Greenworks 40V DHF410 is back in stock after intermittent availability in spring — availability on these rotates frequently.
- Tow-behind dethatchers (Agri-Fab, Brinly, Craftsman) are only useful if you already own a riding mower, lawn tractor, or ZTR with a hitch. They have no value for push-mower-only households.
- The "scarifier" function on combo dethatcher/scarifier units (Sun Joe AJ801E-SCRF blade, Greenworks DHF410, Worx WG850) cuts deeper into living turf than a tine reel and should only be used on heavily compacted lawns at the start of an overseeding cycle. Misused, it kills healthy grass [src8].
- Manufacturer "amp" ratings vs. real cutting torque don't always correlate — the 12-amp Sun Joe AJ801E outperforms several 13-amp competitors in side-by-side testing per Reviewed.com and Family Handyman, mostly because of better tine geometry and tine count [src2, src7].
- Dethatching season is narrow: cool-season grasses (Kentucky bluegrass, fescue, ryegrass) in early spring or early fall; warm-season grasses (Bermuda, Zoysia) in late spring after green-up. Dethatching during dormancy or summer heat causes lasting turf damage [src3].
