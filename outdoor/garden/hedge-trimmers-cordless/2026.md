## What are the best cordless hedge trimmers in 2026?

## Summary

Cordless hedge trimmers have reached a performance inflection point in 2026, with battery-powered models now matching or exceeding the cutting ability of gas and corded electric trimmers across most residential and light commercial applications [src1, src2]. The EGO Power+ HT2601 leads the field with its 56V platform delivering 3,400 SPM and a 1.2-inch cutting capacity through a 26-inch carbon fiber rail blade, making it the best overall choice for homeowners with medium to large hedges [src1, src3]. The Husqvarna Hedge Master 320iHD60 edges it out on ergonomics with a 3,800 SPM brushless motor and three-position grip, though at a higher price point around $299 [src2].

For budget-conscious buyers, the WORX WG261 delivers reliable 20V performance with a 22-inch blade and 3/4-inch cutting capacity at around $69, while the BLACK+DECKER LHT2436 (40V, 24" blade) is the cheapest live-stocked 40V kit at roughly $159 [src1, src4]. The DeWalt DCHT820P1 stands out for users already invested in the DeWalt 20V MAX ecosystem, offering 75 minutes of runtime with a 5.0Ah battery — the longest in our comparison [src1, src7]. Meanwhile, the Stihl HSA 45 is the lightweight champion at just 5.1 pounds, ideal for users who prioritize maneuverability over raw power [src1, src5].

Battery platform compatibility remains a key purchase consideration in 2026, as most major brands now offer 30-60+ tools on their respective voltage platforms. Choosing a hedge trimmer that fits an existing battery ecosystem can save $50-$100 on the initial purchase and simplify long-term tool management [src3, src4].

## Head-to-Head Comparisons

### EGO HT2601 vs Husqvarna 320iHD60
At similar price ranges ($229 vs $299), the EGO wins on raw blade length and ecosystem breadth (26" carbon-fiber rail, 50+ EGO 56V tools), while the Husqvarna wins on cutting speed (3,800 SPM vs 3,400) and ergonomics (three-position grip, side-mounted battery). Both weigh ~11 lbs; both handle 1.0-1.2" branches without bogging down. [src1, src2]

**Pick EGO HT2601 if:** you want the longest blade for big formal hedges and the broader battery ecosystem.
**Pick Husqvarna 320iHD60 if:** you trim daily, value pro-grade build quality, and prefer the side-mounted battery balance.

### EGO HT2601 vs Greenworks 40V 24"
The EGO HT2601 ($229) outperforms the Greenworks 40V 24" ($200) on cutting capacity (1.2" vs 1.0"), blade length (26" vs 24"), and lifetime carbon-fiber rail warranty. With the two now priced within ~$30 of each other, the EGO is the stronger overall buy — the Greenworks mainly wins on weight (7.1 lbs vs 11.1 lbs). [src3, src4]

**Pick EGO HT2601 if:** you want the longer blade, thicker cut capacity, and the broader EGO 56V ecosystem.
**Pick Greenworks 40V 24" if:** you want a much lighter trimmer (7.1 lbs) for comparable everyday residential cutting.

### WORX WG261 vs BLACK+DECKER LHT2436
The WORX WG261 ($69) wins on weight (5.5 lbs vs 7.9 lbs) and price, while the BLACK+DECKER LHT2436 ($159) wins on voltage (40V vs 20V), blade length (24" vs 22"), and runtime (up to 6,000 sq ft per charge). Both include battery and charger. [src1, src4]

**Pick WORX WG261 if:** you have small hedges, prioritize light weight, and want the cheapest complete kit.
**Pick BLACK+DECKER LHT2436 if:** you have medium-size hedges and need a 40V powerplant for longer sessions.

### Stihl HSA 45 vs WORX WG261
Both are lightweight (5.1 lbs vs 5.5 lbs) options for small-yard trimming. The Stihl HSA 45 carries Stihl's pro-grade build quality and dealer service network — but Amazon US prices it as a premium import (~$322). The WORX WG261 delivers comparable everyday performance for a fraction of the Amazon price. [src1, src5]

**Pick Stihl HSA 45 if:** you have a local Stihl dealer (often ~$150-$180) and value the brand's service network.
**Pick WORX WG261 if:** you're buying on Amazon and want the best value.

### EGO HT2601 vs Craftsman CMCHTS820D1
The Craftsman CMCHTS820D1 with its POWERSAW tip cuts thicker branches (1.5" vs 1.2"), but the EGO HT2601 delivers higher SPM (3,400 vs 3,000), a longer blade (26" vs 22"), and stronger ecosystem support. The Craftsman's kit SKU has been scarce on Amazon US in mid-2026. [src3, src4]

**Pick EGO HT2601 if:** you want a balanced premium pick for medium-to-large hedges with broad ecosystem support.
**Pick Craftsman CMCHTS820D1 if:** you specifically need to cut branches thicker than 1.2" without grabbing a separate lopper.

## Decision Logic

### If budget < $100
--> The WORX WG261 (~$69) is the cheapest live-stocked complete kit on Amazon in July 2026 — 22" blade, 20V battery and charger included. Expect limited runtime (~50 min) and 0.75" cutting capacity. [src1, src4]

### If budget is $100-$200
--> Choose the BLACK+DECKER LHT2436 (~$159, 40V 24") for the most blade for your dollar, or the Greenworks 40V 24" (~$200) for slightly better cutting capacity (1.0") and ecosystem expansion potential. [src3, src4]

### If primary use is thick, overgrown hedges
--> Prioritize cutting capacity over blade length. The Craftsman CMCHTS820D1 with its 1.5-inch POWERSAW tip handles the thickest branches (when in stock), followed by the EGO HT2601 at 1.2 inches and the Husqvarna 320iHD60 at 1.0 inch. Standard 0.75-inch models will jam on anything thicker than a pencil. [src4]

### If user already owns tools in a battery platform
--> Buy within your existing ecosystem. The savings from reusing batteries ($50-$100 per tool) outweigh marginal performance differences between comparable models. DeWalt 20V MAX, EGO 56V, Greenworks 40V, and BLACK+DECKER 40V all offer strong hedge trimmer options. [src3, src4]

### If user prioritizes low fatigue and ease of use
--> The Stihl HSA 45 at 5.1 lbs or the WORX WG261 at 5.5 lbs minimize arm fatigue during extended sessions. Avoid the EGO HT2601 (11.1 lbs) and Husqvarna 320iHD60 (11.0 lbs) for users with strength or endurance concerns. [src1, src2]

### Default recommendation
--> The EGO Power+ HT2601 (~$229) offers the best balance of power, blade length, cutting capacity, and ecosystem compatibility for the typical homeowner with medium-to-large hedges. [src1, src3]

## Key Market Trends (2026)

- **Battery platform dominance**: Cordless hedge trimmers now outsell gas models across all residential segments, with 56V and 40V platforms delivering performance parity with gas for all but the most demanding commercial applications. [src1, src4]
- **Carbon fiber and brushless motors**: Premium models like the EGO HT2601 use carbon fiber rails for weight reduction and rigidity, while brushless motors (Husqvarna, Greenworks, EGO) extend both runtime and motor lifespan by 30-50% vs. brushed alternatives. [src1, src2]
- **Ecosystem lock-in accelerating**: Major brands now offer 30-60+ tools on shared battery platforms; hedge trimmer purchases increasingly function as gateway tools into broader ecosystems covering mowers, blowers, chainsaws, and string trimmers. [src3, src4]
- **POWERSAW hybrid designs**: Craftsman's integrated branch-cutting saw tip represents an emerging category of hybrid trimmers designed to eliminate the need for a separate lopper or pruning saw on thick growth. [src4]
