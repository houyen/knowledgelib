# Best Electric Chainsaws (2026)

## What are the best electric chainsaws in 2026?

## TL;DR

**Top pick: Husqvarna Power Axe 350i (~$474)** — only large-bar saw that holds full power to battery cutoff (24 cuts on a 14" log). **Best value: Greenworks 80V 18" (~$247)** — gas-rivaling power at a price now below most 16" kits after a 40% street-price drop. **Best budget: DEWALT 20V MAX 12" (DCCS620P1) (~$349)** — lightest pick at 9 lb 2 oz, shares battery with 300+ DeWalt 20V MAX tools. [src1, src2, src5]

## Summary

Battery-powered chainsaws have reached a tipping point in 2026, with top models matching or exceeding the cutting speed of comparable gas saws while delivering instant starts, zero emissions, and significantly less noise. Testing across multiple review outlets confirms that 56V-80V battery saws can make 100+ cuts through 4x4 lumber on a single charge and cut through 14-inch hardwood logs in under 30 seconds. [src1, src4]

The EGO Power+ CS1804 and Husqvarna Power Axe 350i dominate the 18-inch class, with the Husqvarna maintaining full power until battery depletion and delivering 24 cuts on a 14-inch log. The Milwaukee M18 FUEL 16-inch offers gas-equivalent power with up to 150 cuts per charge, making it the runtime champion. After a 40% street-price drop in mid-2026, the Greenworks 80V 18" kit at ~$247 has displaced the DeWalt 20V 12" (~$349 after a 2026 price climb) as the value pick for budget-conscious buyers. [src1, src2, src3, src5]

The market has clearly shifted toward battery power: Consumer Reports now rates over 50 chainsaw models, with the vast majority being battery-operated. Corded electric models have been largely displaced by battery models offering comparable power without the cord limitation. [src4]

## Best for Each Use Case

### Best Overall: Husqvarna Power Axe 350i (~$474) — [Check price](https://knowledgelib.io/go/husqvarna-power-axe-350i)
The Husqvarna Power Axe 350i earned the top spot for maintaining full power until battery depletion, a trait uncommon among battery chainsaws. In Outdoor Life testing, it cut a 14-inch willow log in 26.6 seconds and delivered 24 cuts before the 7.5Ah battery ran out — the best endurance of any large-bar saw tested. Its well-balanced design and brushless motor make it feel lighter than its 14-pound weight suggests. [src1, src2]

### Best Value (Large Bar): EGO Power+ CS1804 (~$350) — [Check price](https://knowledgelib.io/go/ego-cs1804-chainsaw)
The EGO CS1804 delivers 18-inch cutting capability with a 56V system at a price roughly $124 below the Husqvarna. It includes a 5.0Ah battery and charger in the kit, and the 56V platform is compatible with EGO's extensive lineup of outdoor power tools. For users on the 56V platform who already own batteries, the bare tool is even cheaper. The EGO Power+ CS2005 20-inch model (~$539) is the fastest battery chainsaw ever tested, surpassing even 20-inch gas saws, for those needing maximum bar length. [src2, src6]

### Best Runtime: Milwaukee M18 FUEL 16" (~$399) — [Check price](https://knowledgelib.io/go/milwaukee-m18-fuel-chainsaw)
With up to 150 cuts per charge using the HD 12.0Ah battery, the Milwaukee M18 FUEL delivers unmatched runtime among battery chainsaws. Bob Vila testing confirmed it is "just about as close to gas power as a battery chainsaw can get," with the M18 REDLITHIUM High Output battery operating 50% cooler than standard packs. At 17.3 pounds with battery, it is the heaviest saw on the list, but its 33-35 minutes of sustained runtime rivals gas saws. [src3, src7]

### Best Mid-Size: DeWalt FLEXVOLT 60V 16" DCCS670T1 (~$335) — [Check price](https://knowledgelib.io/go/dewalt-dccs670x1-chainsaw)
The DeWalt FLEXVOLT 60V system delivers strong mid-range performance with a brushless motor, electronic chain brake, and auto-oiling. Bob Vila testing found it "easily spit out wood chips" with well-balanced handling and approximately 30 minutes of runtime per charge. The tool-free chain adjustment and auto-oiling reduce maintenance hassle. The FLEXVOLT 60V batteries are backward-compatible with all DeWalt 20V MAX tools (running at 9Ah equivalent in 20V mode). As of mid-2026, DeWalt's standalone listing on Amazon is the DCCS670T1 (2.0Ah) kit at ~$335; the 3.0Ah DCCS670X1 is sold primarily as a string-trimmer combo kit. [src3, src5]

### Best Value (Large Bar): Greenworks 80V 18" (~$247) — [Check price](https://knowledgelib.io/go/greenworks-80v-18-chainsaw)
After a ~40% street-price drop in 2026, the Greenworks 80V 18" is now the price leader for 18-inch battery chainsaws — cheaper than most 16-inch kits. It delivers the highest voltage in this comparison, translating to power that rivals gas chainsaws in real-world cutting. GearJunkie testing confirmed it handles professional-grade tasks like felling mature hardwoods. The brushless motor, steel bucking spikes, and electronic chain brake are standard. Battery life (~20 minutes continuous) is shorter than competitors due to the higher power draw, but a 30-minute rapid charger mitigates downtime. The 80V platform now spans 75+ tools, making this a strong platform anchor. [src3, src5]

### Fastest Large-Bar Cutting: EGO Power+ CS2005 20" (~$539) — [Check price](https://knowledgelib.io/go/ego-cs2005-chainsaw)
The EGO Power+ CS2005 is the fastest battery chainsaw GearJunkie and Family Handyman have ever tested — surpassing even 20-inch gas saws in standardized cutting trials. The 20-inch bar lets you complete single-pass cuts on logs up to ~18 inches diameter, eliminating the rolling/wedge work needed on shorter saws. The included 6.0Ah 56V battery delivers roughly 25-30 cuts on a 14-inch hardwood log before recharge. At ~$539 it costs ~$65 more than the Husqvarna for an extra 2 inches of bar; only buy this if you regularly cut logs over 14 inches diameter. [src5, src6]

### Best Build Quality: Stihl MSA 220 C-B (~$420) — Stihl dealers only
The Stihl MSA 220 C-B is the first Stihl battery chainsaw with a 16-inch bar option, built with the brand's signature Austrian manufacturing quality. Outdoor Life testing praised its superior construction and dependable performance, cutting a 12-inch log in 22.3 seconds. Runtime reaches up to 40 minutes with the AP 300 S battery. The trade-off is Stihl's dealer-only sales model — no online purchasing. [src1, src7]

### Best Lightweight / DeWalt Platform: DeWalt 20V 12" DCCS620P1 (~$349) — [Check price](https://knowledgelib.io/go/dewalt-20v-12inch-chainsaw)
At 9 lb 2 oz with battery, the DeWalt 20V 12-inch (DCCS620P1) is the lightest pick on this list — ideal for overhead pruning, ladder work, and storm-cleanup tasks where weight fatigue is the bottleneck. Outdoor Life testing recorded 45 cuts on a 6-inch log and a 9.7-second cut time. Note: the street price has climbed from ~$219 in early 2026 to ~$349 in mid-2026 as inventory tightened, so it is no longer the absolute "best budget" pick. Buy it primarily for the weight advantage or because you already own DeWalt 20V MAX batteries (300+ compatible tools). For pure budget shoppers, the Greenworks 80V 18" at ~$247 is now the better value. [src1, src2]

## Head-to-Head Comparisons

### Husqvarna Power Axe 350i vs EGO Power+ CS1804
Both are 18-inch large-bar battery saws within ~$124 of each other (Husqvarna ~$474, EGO ~$350). The Husqvarna wins on testing: it cut a 14-inch willow log in 26.6 seconds and was the only large-bar saw to maintain full power until battery cutoff. The EGO is lighter on the wallet and plugs into a much larger consumer ecosystem (75+ EGO 56V outdoor tools vs Husqvarna's ~20 40V tools). [src1, src2]

**Pick Husqvarna if:** you cut hardwood firewood weekly and want the most consistent power curve; you value Husqvarna's pro-grade build and dealer service network.
**Pick EGO if:** you already own or plan to own other EGO 56V tools (mower, blower, trimmer); $124 savings matters; you cut softwood or storm-cleanup logs.

### Greenworks 80V 18" vs EGO Power+ CS1804
At ~$247 (Greenworks) vs ~$350 (EGO) the Greenworks is now ~$103 cheaper for the same 18-inch bar after a 2026 price drop. The Greenworks 80V delivers more raw cutting power per cut, but its 2.0Ah battery yields shorter runtime (~20 minutes continuous) vs the EGO's 5.0Ah. EGO's 56V platform is broader for consumer outdoor tools; Greenworks 80V has ~75 tools and trends more pro-grade. [src3, src5]

**Pick Greenworks if:** you prioritize price; you cut occasionally rather than daily; you want max cutting power per cut and don't mind a spare battery.
**Pick EGO if:** you need longer single-charge runtime; you want a more refined trigger/balance; you're invested in or attracted to EGO's larger outdoor-tool platform.

### Milwaukee M18 FUEL 16" vs DeWalt FLEXVOLT 60V 16" (DCCS670T1)
Both are 16-inch pro-platform saws at similar price (Milwaukee ~$399, DeWalt ~$335). Milwaukee's 12.0Ah HD pack delivers up to 150 cuts per charge — best-in-class runtime — but the kit weighs 17+ lb. DeWalt's FLEXVOLT 60V cuts well and weighs 13 lb 2 oz, but the standalone DCCS670T1 kit ships with a 2.0Ah battery so total runtime is roughly half the Milwaukee's. Both batteries cross-compatible with massive tool ecosystems (Milwaukee M18: 200+, DeWalt 20V/60V: 300+). [src3, src7]

**Pick Milwaukee if:** runtime matters more than weight; you already own Milwaukee M18 tools; you do extended firewood sessions.
**Pick DeWalt if:** weight matters; you already own DeWalt 20V or 60V tools; you want a lower entry price and can buy a spare battery.

### Greenworks 80V 18" vs Husqvarna Power Axe 350i
After Greenworks' 2026 price drop, the Greenworks is now ~$227 cheaper than the Husqvarna for the same 18-inch bar. The Husqvarna still wins on build quality, power consistency to battery cutoff, and runtime per charge. The Greenworks wins on price and raw power-per-cut. [src1, src3, src5]

**Pick Greenworks if:** budget is the primary constraint; you cut occasionally; you want to dip into battery chainsaws before committing $400+.
**Pick Husqvarna if:** this saw will be your primary firewood/storm-cleanup tool for years; you value the Husqvarna brand's dealer network for chains, bars, and warranty work.

- Prices are approximate US street prices as of July 2026; MSRP is often 20-40% higher and sales events can reduce prices further
