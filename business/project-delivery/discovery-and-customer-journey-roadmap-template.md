# Discovery and Customer Journey Roadmap — Template

## TL;DR
Discovery for a multi-entity implementation runs in five tasks: **requirements → stakeholders and partners → business environment → solution mapping → training strategy**. Each produces a table that feeds the solution design.

## Task 1 — Business and technical requirements
| Activity | Output |
|---|---|
| Describe what each business entity does | Services per entity, in the customer's words |
| Identify improvement areas per entity | Pain points mapped to candidate solution areas |
| List regulations that differ by country | Tax, e-invoicing, labour, data residency, environmental reporting, per jurisdiction |
| Prepare clarification questions | Closed questions with an owner on the customer side |

Good discovery questions are specific and answerable: volumes per month, number of legal entities, systems to retire, integration partners, reporting deadlines, who approves what.

## Task 2 — Stakeholders and partners
| Stakeholder | Interest | Influence | Participation strategy |
|---|---|---|---|
| Chief executive / sponsor | High | High | Steering committee; approves scope, budget, governance |
| Operations director | High | High | Process owner; attends fit-to-standard workshops |
| Fleet / logistics manager | High | Medium | Core team; tests workflows |
| Chief technology officer | High | High | Architecture sign-off; integration testing |

Interest × influence decides the strategy: high/high — manage closely; low/high — keep satisfied; high/low — keep informed; low/low — monitor. For implementation partners, record the selection criteria (industry experience, local presence, certified skills) and the justification.

## Task 3 — Business environment
Use one or two analysis tools and apply them per entity:
| Tool | Use |
|---|---|
| PESTLE | External factors: political, economic, social, technological, legal, environmental |
| SWOT | Internal strengths/weaknesses against external opportunities/threats |

Carry findings forward as constraints (e.g. a legal factor becomes a compliance requirement; a technological factor becomes an integration constraint).

## Task 4 — Map solutions to needs
| Need | Capability | Product/module | Standard or custom |
|---|---|---|---|
| … | … | … | Prefer standard; justify each custom item |

## Task 5 — Training strategy
| Audience | Content | Format | Timing |
|---|---|---|---|
| Project team | Product and method fundamentals | Courses, certification paths | Before design |
| Key users | Process in the new system | Workshops, sandbox practice | During build and test |
| End users | Daily tasks | Hands-on simulation, short guides | Shortly before go-live |

## Roadmap view
Lay the outputs on a timeline by phase (discover → prepare → explore → realize → deploy → run), with milestones and quality gates, so the customer sees what is decided when.

## Common mistakes
- Requirements collected per department with no end-to-end process view.
- Country differences discovered after the template is built.
- Stakeholder list without a participation strategy.
- Training planned last, when dates and budget are already fixed.
