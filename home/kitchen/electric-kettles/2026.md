The 3-year warranty is best-in-class, and a memory feature allows the kettle to be lifted off its base for up to 2 minutes without losing its place in the heating cycle. ### Cuisinart PerfecTemp CPK-17P1 vs Ninja KT200
At nearly identical street prices ($110 vs $100), the Cuisinart wins on warranty (3 years vs 1 year) and continues to top Wirecutter, Reviewed, and Food Network. The Ninja KT200 wins on preset count (7 vs 6), single-cup boil speed (90 seconds), live temperature display, and is Tom's Guide's outright Best for Tea. Pick by ecosystem fit: Cuisinart is the safer multi-purpose pick; Ninja is the dedicated tea drinker's kettle. [src1, src2, src4]

**Pick Cuisinart if:** You want the longest warranty and the broadest expert consensus for a general-purpose kettle.
**Pick Ninja KT200 if:** Your primary use is tea and you want one-touch presets per varietal plus the fastest single-cup boil.

### Fellow Stagg EKG Pro vs Cosori Gooseneck CO108-NK
The Stagg EKG Pro ($180) delivers altitude calibration, brew scheduling, a built-in brew stopwatch, to-the-degree temperature control, and Wi-Fi firmware updates — features serious pour-over enthusiasts will use daily. The Cosori CO108-NK ($70) gives you the same precise gooseneck spout, 5 temperature presets, and a 60-minute hold for roughly one-third the price. For most home brewers, the Cosori covers 90% of the workflow. [src1, src5, src6]

**Pick Fellow Stagg EKG Pro if:** You brew pour-over daily, dial in by the degree, or want scheduling and stopwatch features.
**Pick Cosori CO108-NK if:** You want a real gooseneck and presets without paying premium-tier pricing.

### Cuisinart PerfecTemp CPK-17P1 vs Cosori Digital Glass Kettle CO117-DK
The Cuisinart ($110) has a longer warranty (3 years), a 360° base, and steel construction many users prefer for durability. The Cosori CO117-DK ($54) is half the price, adds a borosilicate glass body (no plastic OR steel contact with water), and offers a 60-minute keep warm versus Cuisinart's 30 minutes. Cosori is the value champion; Cuisinart is the long-haul pick. [src1, src3, src8]

**Pick Cuisinart if:** You want the safest long-term pick with the best warranty and steel construction.
**Pick Cosori CO117-DK if:** You want temperature presets and a glass body for under $55.

### Secura SWK-1701DR vs Hamilton Beach 40880
Both are sub-$40 boil-only kettles. The Secura ($36) wins on material safety (100% stainless interior, no plastic-water contact) and heat retention (double-wall keeps water warm ~75 min). The Hamilton Beach ($27) is cheaper, has a faster cold-to-boil time on 1L, and is a more compact countertop footprint, but uses single-wall construction. [src3, src5]

**Pick Secura if:** Material safety and heat retention matter most.
**Pick Hamilton Beach if:** You want the absolute cheapest credible kettle and don't care about keep-warm performance.

### Fellow Stagg EKG Pro vs Breville IQ Kettle BKE820XL
At similar street prices (~$180), these solve different problems. The Stagg EKG Pro is a 0.9L gooseneck for pour-over with degree-precise temperature, scheduling, and a brew stopwatch. The Breville BKE820XL is a 1.7L general-purpose kettle with 5 varietal presets and a soft-top lid for household tea/coffee. Pick by primary use, not feature count. [src1, src2, src5]

**Pick Fellow Stagg EKG Pro if:** Pour-over coffee is your primary daily ritual.
**Pick Breville BKE820XL if:** You want premium build quality for everyday tea/coffee at a household 1.7L capacity.

## Important Caveats

- Prices shown are approximate U.S. street prices verified July 1, 2026. Electric kettle prices fluctuate, especially during Amazon sales events. Models under $60 frequently drop an additional 20-30% during Prime Day and Black Friday.
- Boil times vary significantly based on starting water temperature and altitude. Manufacturer claims are measured under ideal conditions (typically 1L of 60F water). At higher elevations, water boils at lower temperatures, which affects brewing.
- Most 1500W kettles require a dedicated 15A circuit. Running a kettle on the same circuit as other high-draw appliances (toasters, microwaves) may trip the breaker.
- Kettles without variable temperature control only boil to 212F. For delicate green or white teas that require 160-185F water, a variable temperature model is strongly recommended.
