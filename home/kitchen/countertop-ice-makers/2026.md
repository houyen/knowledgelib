# Best Countertop Ice Makers (2026)

## What are the best countertop ice makers in 2026?

## TL;DR

**Top pick: GE Profile Opal 2.0 XL (~$549)** — Reviewed's 2026 Best Overall; 38 lbs/day nugget, 1-gal side tank, SmartHQ Wi-Fi. **Best value: Frigidaire Gallery EFIC255 (~$220)** — 44 lbs/day nugget at ~40% of Opal price. **Best budget: EUHOMY Luna Pro (~$139)** — sub-$140 crescent ice, restaurant-style slow-melt. [src1, src2, src3]

## Summary

The countertop ice maker market in 2026 splits into four distinct ice formats: nugget (chewable pellet), bullet (cylindrical hollow), clear square cube, and — newly mainstream — crescent (restaurant-style, slow-melting). Nugget ice makers command a price premium ($130-$550) over bullet models ($35-$130), but demand for Sonic-style chewable ice continues to drive the category. The **GE Profile Opal 2.0 XL** (~$549 as of mid-2026, up from ~$429 earlier in the year) remains Reviewed's "Best Overall" pick — its 1-gallon side tank triples ice production vs. the standard Opal 2.0's 0.75-gallon tank, hitting 38 lbs/day. Note the Opal XL has crept back toward list price in 2026, so watch for Prime Day / Black Friday drops. The **GoveeLife Smart Nugget Ice Maker Pro** (~$425, down from its $499 launch price) pushes capacity to 60 lbs/day with first ice in 6 minutes and patented AI NoiseGuard at 40dB — and at its current price it now undercuts the Opal XL by ~$125 while out-producing it, making it the value play for high-volume households. For budget buyers, the **Frigidaire EFIC255** (~$220) delivers solid nugget ice at ~40% of the Opal XL's price, and the **Igloo ICEB26** (~$80) is the best portable bullet option when in stock. [src1, src2, src3]

The biggest shifts in 2026 are (1) the **EUHOMY Luna Pro** crescent ice maker (~$139, launched Q1 2026), which won Reviewed's "Best for Crescent Ice" — bringing restaurant-style slow-melting ice well below the $200 price floor; and (2) Whirlpool's announcement of the **first refrigerator with a built-in nugget ice maker**, signaling that the countertop nugget category may be at peak in 2026 before migrating into integrated appliances. EUHOMY nugget models ($130-$170) continue to bring chewable ice down-market, though ice quality and noise remain noticeably below the GE and GoveeLife flagships. For clear ice enthusiasts, the **Frigidaire EFIC452** (~$107) remains the standout — producing transparent square cubes without special water prep, and at ~$107 it is now the cheapest path to bar-quality clear ice. [src1, src7, src8]

## Head-to-Head Comparisons

### GE Profile Opal 2.0 XL vs GoveeLife Smart Nugget Pro
The price gap has flipped in 2026: the Opal XL is now ~$549 while the GoveeLife has dropped to ~$425, so the GoveeLife is both cheaper AND higher-output. It wins on raw output (60 vs 38 lbs/day) and rated noise (40dB AI NoiseGuard vs ~52dB on the Opal). The Opal still wins on ice quality (drier, crunchier nuggets), real-world quietness (Reviewed measured the GoveeLife as louder than its 40dB rating), and ecosystem maturity (SmartHQ has been shipping firmware updates for 4+ years). At a ~$125 premium for the Opal, the value case for the GoveeLife is now much stronger than at launch. [src1, src2, src7]

**Pick the Opal 2.0 XL if:** ice quality and a proven app matter more than peak capacity or price, and you'll happily pay ~$125 more for the drier nugget and mature SmartHQ ecosystem.
**Pick the GoveeLife Pro if:** you want the most ice for the least money — it now undercuts the Opal XL while producing ~58% more ice — or you entertain 10+ people regularly, or you're already a Govee smart-home household.

### GE Profile Opal 2.0 XL vs GE Profile Opal 2.0 (standard)
Same 38 lbs/day, same compressor, same SmartHQ app. The XL ships with a 1-gallon detachable side tank (the standard ships with 0.75-gallon). The XL is ~$549 in mid-2026; the standard Opal 2.0 is frequently out of stock on Amazon (currently unavailable as of this update), so the XL is the version you can actually buy right now. When the standard is in stock it typically runs $50-100 less and buys a smaller footprint at the cost of a smaller tank. [src1, src5]

**Pick the Opal 2.0 XL if:** you run ice for parties or want fewer refills — and, in 2026, because it is the one consistently in stock.
**Pick the standard Opal 2.0 if:** counter space is tight, you're filling daily anyway, and you find it back in stock at a discount to the XL.

### Frigidaire EFIC255 vs EUHOMY Nugget Ice Maker
Both target the budget nugget tier. The EFIC255 (~$220, 44 lbs/day, built-in water filter) produces denser, drier nuggets closer to Opal-quality. The EUHOMY (~$152, 34 lbs/day) is ~$70 cheaper and faster to first ice (6-8 min vs ~15 min) but produces softer, wetter pellets and lacks a water filter. The price gap between them has narrowed in 2026 (the EFIC255 has dropped to ~$220), making the better-ice EFIC255 the easier call. [src1, src3, src6]

**Pick the EFIC255 if:** you've tasted Opal nuggets before and would notice the quality drop — for only ~$70 more you get higher output, a water filter, and denser ice.
**Pick the EUHOMY if:** you've never had nugget ice or your kids are just chewing it — the cheapest entry into nugget still buys a lot of forgiveness.

### EUHOMY Luna Pro vs Frigidaire EFIC452
The two sub-$200 picks for cocktail/whiskey use. Luna Pro (~$139, crescent) melts ~30 minutes slower than nugget or bullet — best for slow-sipping spirits where dilution control matters. EFIC452 (~$107, clear square) looks the most professional and is the only countertop maker producing transparent square cubes without distilled water — and at ~$107 it is now the cheapest of the two. [src1, src3]

**Pick the Luna Pro if:** dilution control is the priority (whiskey, single-spirit cocktails, slow sipping).
**Pick the EFIC452 if:** presentation matters more (cocktail parties, instagrammable drinks) and you'll consume the cubes within ~30 minutes.

### GE Profile Opal 2.0 XL vs GE Profile Opal 2.0 Ultra
Same 38 lbs/day, same SmartHQ. The Ultra (~$449) adds a scale-inhibiting filter and a tighter side-tank seal. In a notable 2026 twist, the Ultra (~$449) is now actually ~$100 *cheaper* than the XL (~$549), which has crept back to list — so the scale-filtered Ultra is the better buy for most people right now, not just hard-water households. [src5]

**Pick the Ultra if:** your tap water is hard (>120 ppm) or you're in a scale-prone area — or simply because at ~$449 it currently undercuts the XL while adding the filter.
**Pick the XL if:** you specifically want the 1-gallon tank and find it on a deep promotional discount that brings it below the Ultra.
