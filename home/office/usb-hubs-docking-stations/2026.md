# Best USB Hubs and Docking Stations (2026)

## What are the best USB hubs and docking stations in 2026?

## TL;DR

**Top pick: CalDigit TS4 (~$380)** — 18 ports, 98W charging, universal Mac/Windows/Chrome support; gold-standard Thunderbolt 4 dock. [src3, src6]
**Best value: Razer USB4 Dock (~$230)** — 14 ports and dual 4K@120Hz over USB4 at a Thunderbolt-dock-killer price. [src3, src5]
**Best budget: Anker 555 8-in-1 Hub (~$50)** — 4K@60Hz HDMI, Gigabit Ethernet, 85W PD in a compact dongle. [src1, src7]
Thunderbolt 5 docks have gotten cheaper — the Plugable TBT-UDT3 is now ~$255, undercutting the TS4 if you have a TB5 laptop. [src2, src8]

## Summary

The USB-C hub and docking station market in 2026 splits into three tiers: budget hubs under $50 for basic port expansion, mid-range USB-C/USB4 docks ($100-$250) for dual-monitor desk setups, and premium Thunderbolt 4/5 docks ($250-$500) for professionals who need maximum bandwidth and display support. Thunderbolt 5 docks have arrived in force and prices have fallen — the Plugable TBT-UDT3 is now ~$255 (down from $300), with the CalDigit TS5 ($400) and TS5 Plus ($500, 20 ports and 330W) topping every reviewer's chart at 80-120 Gbps speeds and 140-330W power delivery. [src2, src3, src8]

For most users, the CalDigit TS4 remains the best overall docking station in 2026 — its 18 ports, 98W charging, and flawless Mac/Windows/Chrome compatibility make it the gold standard, though its street price has climbed to ~$380. The Razer USB4 Dock (~$230) is the best value for users without Thunderbolt, delivering 14 ports and dual 4K@120Hz over USB4. Budget buyers should grab the Anker 555 8-in-1 hub (~$50) or the Hiearcool 7-in-1 (~$17) for basic port expansion. [src1, src3, src5, src7]

The biggest shift in 2026 is Thunderbolt 5 entering the mainstream, though TB4 docks still dominate the value segment. M1/M2 base MacBook users face persistent multi-monitor limitations — only DisplayLink-based docks (like the MOKiN 13-in-1) can drive more than one external display without native hardware support. [src2, src6]

## Best for Each Use Case

### Best Overall: CalDigit TS4 (~$380) — [Check price](https://knowledgelib.io/go/caldigit-ts4-usb-hubs-docking-stations)
The CalDigit TS4 packs 18 ports into a compact vertical dock — three Thunderbolt 4 downstream, three USB-C 10 Gbps, five USB-A 10 Gbps, DisplayPort 1.4, 2.5 GbE, SD/microSD UHS-II, and front/rear audio. It delivers 98W to your laptop and 20W from the front USB-C port for phone charging. Windows Central still calls it "the best docking station on the market" for users not yet on Thunderbolt 5 in 2026 — though at ~$380 it now commands a premium over the newer TB5 Plugable dock. [src3, src6]

### Best Future-Proof: Plugable TBT-UDT3 (~$255) — [Check price](https://knowledgelib.io/go/plugable-tbt5-dock)
The first widely available Thunderbolt 5 dock, offering 80-120 Gbps bandwidth, 140W power delivery, and support for dual 8K@60Hz displays on Windows (dual 6K@60Hz on Mac). Named PCWorld's Best Thunderbolt 5 Dock of 2026 at this price. Now down to ~$255, it actually undercuts the TB4 CalDigit TS4 — if you have a TB5-equipped laptop, this is the cheapest way to take advantage of it. Falls back gracefully to TB4 speeds on older hardware. [src2, src3]

### Best Value Dock: Razer USB4 Dock (~$230) — [Check price](https://knowledgelib.io/go/razer-usb4-dock)
At ~$230, the Razer USB4 Dock delivers 14 ports including HDMI 2.1, DisplayPort, four USB-A, three USB-C, Gigabit Ethernet, SD/microSD, and 100W PD — all over USB4 without requiring Thunderbolt certification. Windows Central called it "practically flawless" and the best value for users who do not have a Thunderbolt-equipped PC. [src5, src3]

### Best for Enterprise/IT: Kensington SD5780T (~$244) — [Check price](https://knowledgelib.io/go/kensington-sd5780t)
The SD5780T combines dual downstream Thunderbolt 4, HDMI 2.1, four USB-A 3.2 Gen 2, 2.5 GbE, and UHS-II SD into a mountable aluminum chassis with a three-year warranty. Now discounted to ~$244 from $300 MSRP. Its broad compatibility (Mac, Windows, Chrome) and TAA compliance make it a top pick for enterprise deployments. [src3, src2]

### Best Multi-Monitor (Windows): Anker 778 (~$300) — [Check price](https://knowledgelib.io/go/anker-778-tb4-dock)
The Anker 778 supports up to four 4K external displays on Windows via HDMI 2.1 and dual DisplayPort 1.4, plus 12 total ports and 100W PD. However, Mac users should avoid it — base M1/M2 Macs are not supported, and Pro/Max MacBooks are limited to mirrored rather than extended multi-display. Note: as of July 2026 this dock is showing as temporarily out of stock on Amazon — the CalDigit TS4 or Anker Prime TB4 dock are the in-stock alternatives. [src4]

### Best Budget Multi-Monitor: MOKiN 13-in-1 (~$87) — [Check price](https://knowledgelib.io/go/mokin-13in1-dock)
The MOKiN 13-in-1 is a PCWorld Editor's Choice pick that uses DisplayLink drivers to enable dual 4K@60Hz displays from any USB-C laptop — including M1/M2 base MacBooks that normally cap at one external monitor. It adds 160W GaN charging, 10 Gbps data ports, Gigabit Ethernet, and a unique 2.26" LCD smart display. Note: this model is showing as temporarily out of stock on Amazon as of July 2026. [src1]

### Best Budget Hub: Anker 555 8-in-1 (~$50) — [Check price](https://knowledgelib.io/go/anker-555-usbc-hub)
The Anker 555 adds 4K@60Hz HDMI, Gigabit Ethernet, SD/microSD UHS-II, 10 Gbps USB-C and dual USB-A, and 85W pass-through charging — now back to ~$50 at MSRP after earlier discounts. It is the best compact hub for users who need one monitor, a wired network connection, and card readers without a bulky dock. [src1, src7]

### Best Ultra-Budget: Hiearcool 7-in-1 (~$17) — [Check price](https://knowledgelib.io/go/hiearcool-7in1-hub)
Under $20 with 4K HDMI, dual USB-A 3.0 (5 Gbps), SD/microSD, and 100W charge-only USB-C pass-through. The HDMI maxes out at 4K@30Hz and there is no Ethernet, but for basic presentations and travel it is hard to beat at the price. [src1, src7]

## Head-to-Head Comparisons

### CalDigit TS4 vs Plugable TBT-UDT3 (Thunderbolt 5)
The Plugable TBT-UDT3 (~$255) now costs less than the TS4 (~$380), flipping the value calculus. The TS4 wins on port count (18 vs 11) and is the safer pick if you do not yet have a TB5 host — bandwidth-wise you cannot exceed TB4 anyway. The Plugable TBT-UDT3 is future-proofed at 80-120 Gbps, pushes 140W versus the TS4's 98W, and is the cheaper dock. [src2, src3]

**Pick TS4 if:** you need maximum ports today and your laptop is TB3/TB4/USB4.
**Pick TBT-UDT3 if:** you have (or are buying) a TB5 laptop or need 140W charging for a high-wattage workstation.

### CalDigit TS4 vs Razer USB4 Dock
The TS4 has more ports (18 vs 14), 2.5 GbE versus 1 GbE, and three downstream Thunderbolt 4 ports (the Razer has none). But the Razer USB4 Dock (~$230) costs about $150 less than the TS4 (~$380) and matches it on display output (dual 4K@120Hz vs dual 6K@60Hz) and PD (100W vs 98W). For non-creative users without TB peripherals, the Razer's value is hard to beat. [src3, src5]

**Pick TS4 if:** you have Thunderbolt SSDs/audio interfaces or need 2.5 GbE.
**Pick Razer USB4 Dock if:** you have a USB-C-only laptop and want the best dock under $250.

### Anker 778 vs CalDigit TS4 (Windows multi-monitor)
Both are ~$300 TB4 docks, but the Anker 778 supports up to four 4K external displays on Windows via dual DisplayPort + HDMI, while the TS4 maxes at dual 6K through one DP and one TB4. The TS4 wins on port count and Mac compatibility; the 778 wins specifically for Windows triple/quad-monitor setups. [src3, src4]

**Pick TS4 if:** you use a Mac or need maximum total ports.
**Pick Anker 778 if:** you are on Windows and need 3 or 4 external displays.

### Anker 555 Hub vs Hiearcool 7-in-1 Hub (budget tier)
At $50 vs $17, the Anker 555 roughly triples the Hiearcool's port capability: Gigabit Ethernet (Hiearcool has none), 4K@60Hz HDMI (vs 4K@30Hz), 10 Gbps USB-C (vs 5 Gbps USB-A), and pass-through PD that also powers downstream devices. The Hiearcool wins only on price and ultra-compact travel form factor. [src1, src7]

**Pick Anker 555 if:** you need a desk hub with wired Ethernet and smooth 4K.
**Pick Hiearcool 7-in-1 if:** you need a $17 travel dongle for presentations.

### MOKiN 13-in-1 vs Razer USB4 Dock (no-Thunderbolt dock)
The Razer USB4 Dock is the premium pick at ~$230 with native USB4 dual 4K@120Hz; the MOKiN 13-in-1 ($87) is the only sub-$100 dock that drives dual 4K@60Hz from a base M1/M2 MacBook (via DisplayLink). DisplayLink adds CPU overhead and minor latency, so the Razer wins for performance-sensitive work. [src1, src5]

**Pick Razer USB4 Dock if:** your laptop has USB4 or TB4 and you want native low-latency output.
**Pick MOKiN 13-in-1 if:** you have a base M1/M2 Mac (no native multi-display) or need GaN charging built in.

## Key Market Trends (2026)

- **Thunderbolt 5 enters the mainstream**: After Plugable broke the $300 TB5 barrier in late 2025, CalDigit shipped the TS5 ($400) and TS5 Plus ($500) in early 2026, plus Anker Prime TB5 and Razer's TB5 dock. Tom's Hardware's May 2026 round-up tested five mainstream TB5 docks. [src2, src3, src8]
- **USB4 narrows the gap and drops prices**: USB4 docks like the Razer USB4 Dock (~$230) match TB4 performance at lower prices, eroding Thunderbolt's premium positioning — especially as the CalDigit TS4's street price has climbed toward $380. [src5]
- **DisplayLink enables M-series multi-monitor**: Budget docks with DisplayLink/SiliconMotion drivers now reliably drive 2-3 external displays from base M1-M4 MacBooks, solving a longstanding pain point. [src1, src6]
- **160W GaN power supplies**: Integrated GaN chargers (MOKiN 13-in-1) shrink power bricks and increase total wattage, reducing desk clutter. [src1]
- **Price compression at the low end**: Budget USB-C hubs with 4K@60Hz and 10 Gbps ports are now under $35, down from $50+ in 2024, with the Hiearcool 7-in-1 dropping under $20 in 2026. [src7]

## Important Caveats

- Prices shown are approximate US street prices as of July 2026 — Amazon pricing fluctuates 20-40% and sale events can cut prices significantly; the MOKiN 13-in-1 and Anker 778 were showing as temporarily out of stock at time of verification
- Thunderbolt 5 docks fall back to Thunderbolt 4 speeds when connected to TB4/TB3 hosts — no performance penalty but no speed gain either
- DisplayLink-based multi-monitor (MOKiN, Baseus) adds CPU overhead and slight latency — not suitable for gaming or video editing on the secondary displays
- Mac multi-monitor support varies by exact chip: base M1/M2 = 1 display native, M1/M2 Pro = 2 displays, M1/M2 Max = 3-4 displays, M3/M4 base = 2 displays native
- USB4 certification is less strict than Thunderbolt — some USB4 docks may not support all features (like PCIe tunneling) that Thunderbolt guarantees
- Power delivery wattage is always measured at the dock input — expect 10-15W less reaching your laptop due to conversion losses
