# Best Travel Routers (2026)

## What are the best travel routers in 2026?

## TL;DR

**Top pick: GL.iNet Beryl AX (GL-MT3000) (~$99) -- best all-rounder for WiFi 6 speed, VPN performance, and value.**
**Best WiFi 7: GL.iNet Beryl 7 (GL-MT3600BE) (~$107) -- 1,100 Mbps WireGuard VPN, dual 2.5GbE, now cheaper than the Slate 7.**
**Best budget: GL.iNet Mango (GL-MT300N-V2) (~$30) -- pocket-sized, VPN-capable, around $30.**
WiFi 7 travel routers arrived in force in 2025-2026, but WiFi 6 models remain the best value since hotel/airport upstream speeds rarely exceed 100 Mbps.
[src1, src3]

## Summary

The travel router market in 2026 is dominated by GL.iNet, which offers models at every price point from the $30 Mango to the ~$143 Slate 7. TP-Link entered the WiFi 7 travel router space with the Roam 7 (TL-WR3602BE) in late 2025, and ASUS continues to offer the RT-AX57 Go with its unique AiMesh home-network integration. For travelers who need built-in cellular, the Netgear Nighthawk M7 (MH7150) is the first WiFi 7 mobile hotspot with global eSIM support, though at ~$470 it serves a different audience. [src1, src7]

The **GL.iNet Beryl AX** remains the consensus best travel router for most people in 2026. It delivers WiFi 6 AX3000 speeds (up to 2402 Mbps on 5 GHz), WireGuard VPN at up to 300 Mbps, a 2.5 GbE WAN port, and USB-C power -- all at ~$99. For those wanting the latest WiFi 7 standard, the **GL.iNet Beryl 7** (~$107) is now the best value: it matches the Slate 7's dual 2.5GbE ports and WiFi 7 BE3600 while adding far faster VPN (1,100 Mbps WireGuard) -- and after a 2026 price increase the touchscreen **GL.iNet Slate 7** sells for ~$143 (list $170), making it the premium pick for buyers who specifically want its display. The real-world difference between WiFi 6 and WiFi 7 travel routers is minimal when connected to typical hotel WiFi speeds of 10-50 Mbps. [src2, src3, src6]

All recommended models run OpenWRT (or OpenWRT-based firmware), support both OpenVPN and WireGuard, and can handle captive portal login pages (the hotel/airport splash screens). Every model is USB-C powered except the Mango (micro-USB) and the Nighthawk M7 (built-in battery). [src1, src3]

## Best for Each Use Case

### Best Overall: GL.iNet Beryl AX (GL-MT3000) (~$99) -- [Check price](https://knowledgelib.io/go/glinet-beryl-ax)
The Beryl AX is the travel router that Independent Travel Cats, Dong Knows Tech, and The Broke Backpacker all recommend for most travelers. WiFi 6 AX3000 with a dual-core 1.3 GHz MediaTek processor handles up to 70 simultaneous device connections. The 2.5 GbE WAN port future-proofs wired connections. WireGuard VPN tops out at 300 Mbps -- fast enough to stream 4K over VPN in most scenarios. USB 3.0 port supports phone tethering and external storage. At ~$99, it delivers 80-90% of the WiFi 7 models' real-world performance at 60-70% of their price. [src1, src3, src8]

### Best WiFi 7 (Touchscreen): GL.iNet Slate 7 (GL-BE3600) (~$143) -- [Check price](https://knowledgelib.io/go/glinet-slate-7)
The only travel router with dual 2.5 GbE ports (WAN and LAN) plus a touchscreen, making it the top choice if you want multi-gigabit wired connections and an on-device display. The touchscreen lets you scan WiFi QR codes, toggle VPN, and monitor speed without opening a browser. WiFi 7 BE3600 delivers up to 2,882 Mbps on 5 GHz. WireGuard VPN reaches ~540 Mbps. Runs OpenWRT 23.05 and draws 8-9W, so it requires a more powerful USB-C charger (18W PD minimum). At ~$143 (16% off its $169.99 list) it is the premium touchscreen option -- the cheaper Beryl 7 below matches its WiFi 7 specs and beats its VPN speed, so buy the Slate 7 mainly for the touchscreen. [src1, src5]

### Best WiFi 7 / VPN Performance: GL.iNet Beryl 7 (GL-MT3600BE) (~$107) -- [Check price](https://knowledgelib.io/go/glinet-beryl-7)
The newest GL.iNet travel router, launched Q1 2026, with a quad-core 2.0 GHz MediaTek CPU that delivers class-leading VPN throughput: 1,100 Mbps on WireGuard and 1,000 Mbps on OpenVPN-DCO. Despite these faster speeds, it draws only ~4.5W idle vs the Slate 7's 8-9W. Supports 120+ simultaneous devices. Dual 2.5 GbE ports. No touchscreen (unlike the Slate 7), keeping it lighter at 7.2 oz. The best choice for users who run VPN full-time while traveling. [src6]

### Best Brand-Name WiFi 7: TP-Link Roam 7 (TL-WR3602BE) (~$90) -- [Check price](https://knowledgelib.io/go/tp-link-roam-7)
TP-Link's first WiFi 7 travel router supports seven operating modes (Router, Hotspot, USB Tethering, 3G/4G/5G USB Modem, Access Point, Range Extender, Client). Dual-band BE3600, 1x 2.5 GbE WAN + 1x 1 GbE LAN, USB 3.0, and support for 35+ VPN providers including OpenVPN and WireGuard. Compact foldable design fits in a laptop bag. Connects up to 90 devices. At ~$90, it now undercuts the Beryl AX on price while offering WiFi 7. Tom's Hardware notes "compact and packed with features, but average performance" in benchmarks vs GL.iNet competitors. [src4, src2]

### Best for ASUS Home Network Users: ASUS RT-AX57 Go (~$96) -- [Check price](https://knowledgelib.io/go/asus-rt-ax57-go)
The standout feature is AiMesh: if you already have an ASUS router at home, this travel router integrates into your existing mesh network and doubles as a mesh node when you are not traveling. AiProtection security suite powered by Trend Micro adds malware blocking and network scanning -- subscription-free. WiFi 6 AX3000, two gigabit Ethernet ports, USB 3.0, USB-C power. One-click VPN setup for NordVPN and Surfshark. Currently ~$96, and still dips toward $70 on sale from its $130 list price. [src3, src2]

### Best Budget / Backup: GL.iNet Mango (GL-MT300N-V2) (~$30) -- [Check price](https://knowledgelib.io/go/glinet-mango)
At 1.4 oz and roughly matchbox-sized, the Mango slips into a pocket. WiFi 4 (2.4 GHz only, 300 Mbps) and 100 Mbps Ethernet are slow by 2026 standards, but adequate for connecting multiple devices to hotel WiFi through a single captive portal login. Supports OpenVPN (~11 Mbps) and WireGuard (~45 Mbps). Micro-USB powered by any phone charger or power bank. The best throwaway/backup device at this price. [src3, src8]

### Best with Built-In Cellular: Netgear Nighthawk M7 (MH7150) (~$470) -- [Check price](https://knowledgelib.io/go/netgear-nighthawk-m7)
The only WiFi 7 mobile hotspot with global eSIM support (140+ countries via NETGEAR eSIM Marketplace). Built-in 5G/4G LTE (Qualcomm X72 modem), 10-hour battery, 2.8-inch touchscreen, and 2.5 GbE Ethernet port. Supports 32 simultaneous WiFi devices. No need for hotel WiFi -- insert a SIM or download an eSIM and you have your own cellular internet. At ~$470, it is a fundamentally different product from the $30-150 travel routers, aimed at business travelers and digital nomads who need independent cellular connectivity. [src7, src1]

## Head-to-Head Comparisons

### GL.iNet Beryl AX vs GL.iNet Slate 7
The Beryl AX (~$99, WiFi 6) is the better value for most travelers because hotel WiFi rarely exceeds 100 Mbps, making the Slate 7's WiFi 7 advantage academic in practice. The Slate 7 (~$143) wins on wired speed (dual 2.5 GbE vs one 2.5 GbE + one 1 GbE), VPN throughput (540 vs 300 Mbps WireGuard), and the touchscreen for QR-code WiFi setup. But the Slate 7 draws nearly twice the power (8-9W vs ~5W), weighs more, and costs ~44% more. [src1, src3]

**Pick Beryl AX if:** you want the best value and your upstream connection is under 1 Gbps (i.e., any hotel/airport WiFi).
**Pick Slate 7 if:** you need dual 2.5 GbE ports plus an on-device touchscreen (otherwise the cheaper Beryl 7 covers WiFi 7).

### GL.iNet Beryl 7 vs GL.iNet Slate 7
Both are WiFi 7 BE3600 travel routers with dual 2.5 GbE ports. The Beryl 7 (~$107) is now the better buy for most: it more than doubles the Slate 7's VPN throughput (1,100 vs 540 Mbps WireGuard), draws far less power (~4.5W idle vs 8-9W), and weighs less (7.2 vs 10.2 oz). The Slate 7 (~$143) justifies its premium only with its built-in touchscreen for QR-code WiFi setup and VPN toggling. Even at its discounted ~$143, the Beryl 7 is both cheaper and faster. [src6, src5]

**Pick Beryl 7 if:** you want the best WiFi 7 value, top VPN speed, and lower power draw.
**Pick Slate 7 if:** the on-device touchscreen is worth paying ~$36 more for.

### GL.iNet Beryl 7 vs TP-Link Roam 7
Both are WiFi 7 BE3600 travel routers at similar price points (~$90-107). The Beryl 7 crushes the Roam 7 on VPN performance (1,100 Mbps WireGuard vs undisclosed) and draws less power. The Roam 7 wins on operating mode count (7 modes including USB modem), brand recognition, and ease-of-setup for non-technical users. The Beryl 7 runs OpenWRT for advanced customization; the Roam 7 uses TP-Link's simpler interface. [src6, src4]

**Pick Beryl 7 if:** VPN speed matters and you are comfortable with OpenWRT.
**Pick Roam 7 if:** you want TP-Link's polished UI, wide compatibility modes, and strong after-sale support.

### GL.iNet Beryl AX vs ASUS RT-AX57 Go
Both are WiFi 6 AX3000 travel routers with USB-C power and VPN support. The Beryl AX has a 2.5 GbE WAN port (vs ASUS's two 1 GbE ports) and runs OpenWRT with deeper customization. The ASUS wins on security features (subscription-free AiProtection by Trend Micro) and the AiMesh integration that lets the router double as a home mesh node. The ASUS frequently drops to ~$70 on sale, making it cheaper than the Beryl AX. [src3, src2]

**Pick Beryl AX if:** you want a 2.5 GbE port and OpenWRT flexibility.
**Pick ASUS RT-AX57 Go if:** you already own an ASUS router at home or want built-in security features.

### GL.iNet Mango vs GL.iNet Beryl AX
The Mango ($25-30) is a fraction of the Beryl AX's price but runs WiFi 4 at 300 Mbps with 100 Mbps Ethernet. For basic hotel WiFi sharing and VPN at ~45 Mbps WireGuard, the Mango is sufficient. The Beryl AX ($99) is vastly faster and more capable but costs 3-4x more. The Mango makes sense as a disposable backup or for budget travelers who only need basic connectivity. [src3, src8]

**Pick Mango if:** budget is under $50, or you need a tiny backup device.
**Pick Beryl AX if:** you can spend $99 and want real WiFi 6 speeds and robust VPN.

## Key Market Trends (2026)

- **WiFi 7 travel routers arrived but offer marginal real-world benefit**: GL.iNet Slate 7, Beryl 7, and TP-Link Roam 7 all support WiFi 7 BE3600, but none include 6 GHz band support. Since hotel/airport upstream speeds are the bottleneck (typically 10-100 Mbps), WiFi 6 models still deliver equivalent real-world performance for most users. [src1, src6]
- **VPN throughput has crossed 1 Gbps**: The GL.iNet Beryl 7 reaches 1,100 Mbps on WireGuard -- fast enough that VPN is no longer a bottleneck on any realistic travel connection. This was unthinkable in travel routers two years ago. [src6]
- **GL.iNet dominates the category**: GL.iNet holds 5 of the top 7 positions across major review sites. TP-Link and ASUS are the only major brand competitors with dedicated travel router products. [src1, src2]
- **Touchscreens on travel routers**: The GL.iNet Slate 7 introduced a touchscreen for QR-code WiFi scanning and VPN toggling. Expect this feature to appear in more models by late 2026. [src1, src5]
- **Global eSIM integration in mobile hotspots**: The Netgear Nighthawk M7's eSIM Marketplace (140+ countries) makes buying local data plans effortless. Tom's Hardware called it "a game-changer" for international travelers. [src7]

## Important Caveats

- Prices are approximate US street prices as of July 2026 (verified on Amazon). GL.iNet models fluctuate 10-20% on Amazon; the Slate 7 lists at ~$170 but recently sells around ~$143, and the ASUS RT-AX57 Go (~$96) still drops toward $70 on sale.
- WiFi 7 travel routers (Slate 7, Beryl 7, Roam 7) do not support 6 GHz band. Real WiFi 7 benefits (MLO, 4K QAM) require compatible client devices.
- Travel routers rebroadcast an existing connection (hotel WiFi, phone tether, Ethernet). They are not standalone internet sources -- you need a connection to share.
- VPN speed figures are manufacturer-stated maximums under lab conditions. Real-world VPN throughput depends on the VPN server, upstream speed, and encryption overhead.
- The Netgear Nighthawk M7 is a mobile hotspot with built-in cellular, which is a fundamentally different product category. It is included because travelers frequently compare it against traditional travel routers.
- Power bank compatibility: most USB-C travel routers draw 5-9W. The Slate 7 requires 18W PD. Verify your power bank supports PD output if using a WiFi 7 model.
