# Best 17-Inch Laptops (2026)

## What are the best 17-inch laptops in 2026?

## TL;DR

**Top pick: LG Gram Pro 17 (~$2,950)** — only 17-inch laptop with RTX 5050 GPU at 3.26 lbs. [src3, src7]
**Best value: ASUS ROG Strix G17 (~$1,600)** — Ryzen 9 7845HX + RTX 4070 gaming for around $1,600. [src6]
**Best budget: HP Laptop 17 (~$700)** — 17.3" FHD IPS + Ryzen 5 + 16GB RAM for everyday productivity. [src1]

## Summary

The 17-inch laptop market has narrowed considerably in 2026, with many manufacturers shifting to 16-inch or 18-inch form factors, but the remaining options cover every major use case from ultraportable productivity to high-performance gaming. [src1, src2] The LG Gram Pro 17 stands out as the best overall pick, weighing just 3.26 pounds while packing an Intel Core Ultra 9 285H processor, NVIDIA RTX 5050 GPU, and a 2560x1600 display — making it the world's lightest 17-inch laptop with a dedicated RTX GPU. [src3, src7] Its new Aerominum chassis material, developed by LG, reduces weight while meeting military-grade durability standards. [src3]

For gamers, the ASUS ROG Strix G17 remains a strong value with its AMD Ryzen 9 7845HX and RTX 4070, delivering 17.3" FHD 144Hz gaming from around $1,600 (down slightly from its ~$1,700 spring-2026 peak, though stock is now down to the last unit at this price). [src6] The Acer Nitro V 17 AI brings next-gen RTX 5070 graphics and AMD Ryzen 7 260 to the gaming segment at approximately $1,500 with a 17.3" FHD 144Hz panel. [src5] On the productivity side, the HP Envy 17 offers a polished aluminum design with a touchscreen and an Intel Core i7 + RTX 3050 configuration (currently unavailable at most Amazon sellers), while the HP Laptop 17 serves budget buyers needing a large screen for around $1599. [src4, src1]

## Best for Each Use Case

### Best Overall: LG Gram Pro 17 (2026) (~$2,950) — [Check price](https://knowledgelib.io/go/lg-gram-pro-17-2026)
The LG Gram Pro 17 is the rare laptop that delivers both a large 17-inch display and genuine portability at just 3.26 pounds. Its Intel Core Ultra 9 285H processor and NVIDIA RTX 5050 GPU handle demanding creative and productivity workloads, while the 90Wh battery provides up to 25 hours of video playback. The new Aerominum chassis material combines magnesium and aluminum alloy for scratch resistance and military-grade durability. Amazon's US street price is roughly $2,935 (against a $3,249 list), well above the early-2026 launch price near $1,900 as inventory has tightened. [src3, src7]

### Best Budget: HP Laptop 17 (2026) (~$700) — [Check price](https://knowledgelib.io/go/hp-laptop-17-2026)
For users who need a large 17.3-inch display without breaking the bank, the HP Laptop 17 delivers reliable performance with an AMD Ryzen 5 7430U processor, 16GB RAM, and a 512GB SSD in a Windows 11 Pro configuration for around $700. The anti-glare FHD IPS panel, Copilot key, and Bluetooth 5.4 make it a capable everyday machine for web browsing, document editing, video streaming, and basic multitasking, though the integrated Radeon graphics limit creative and gaming workloads. [src1, src2]

### Best for Gaming (Value): ASUS ROG Strix G17 (~$1,600) — [Check price](https://knowledgelib.io/go/asus-rog-strix-g17)
The ROG Strix G17 pairs AMD's 16-core Ryzen 9 7845HX with NVIDIA's RTX 4070 and a 17.3-inch FHD 144Hz display, delivering smooth high-refresh-rate gaming at 1080p with headroom for QHD external monitors via DisplayPort. Conductonaut Extreme liquid metal on the CPU and NVIDIA Advanced Optimus with MUX switch provide 5-10% better gaming performance than laptops without direct GPU output. At around $1,600 in mid-2026 (eased from its ~$1,700 spring peak), it remains one of the better gaming values in the 17-inch segment — though this Eclipse Gray (2024) SKU is now down to its last unit at this price. [src6, src1]

### Best for Gaming (Next-Gen): Acer Nitro V 17 AI (~$1,500) — [Check price](https://knowledgelib.io/go/acer-nitro-v-17-ai)
The Acer Nitro V 17 AI brings NVIDIA's Blackwell-architecture RTX 5070 GPU with DLSS 4 neural rendering and 798 AI TOPS to a 17.3-inch chassis. The AMD Ryzen 7 260 processor with NPU enables Copilot+ PC features. The verified Amazon SKU ships with a 17.3" FHD 144Hz display (1920x1080), 32GB DDR5, and a 1TB Gen 4 SSD — and dual-fan quad-intake cooling keeps thermals in check during extended gaming sessions. [src5]

### Best for Portability: LG Gram 17 (2026) (~$2,950) — [Check price](https://knowledgelib.io/go/lg-gram-17-2026)
At just 3.2 pounds, the LG Gram 17 (the Intel Arc integrated-graphics configuration of the gram Pro line) remains the lightest 17-inch laptop available, weighing less than many 14-inch competitors. The Intel Core Ultra 7 355 processor with Intel Arc integrated graphics handles productivity workloads efficiently, and the 77Wh battery delivers over 12 hours of real-world use. The 2560x1600 WQXGA display provides sharp text and ample workspace for multitasking. Amazon's US price for this Copilot+ config has climbed to ~$2,950 as gram inventory has tightened — at this price the dedicated-GPU Gram Pro 17 is the stronger buy unless you specifically want the lighter Intel Arc build. [src2, src3]

### Best for Creative Work: HP Envy 17 (currently unavailable on Amazon, MSRP historically ~$1,000) — [Check price](https://knowledgelib.io/go/hp-envy-17)
The HP Envy 17 combines a 17.3-inch FHD touchscreen display with a 13th-gen Intel Core i7 processor and NVIDIA RTX 3050 GPU in a premium aluminum and glass chassis. The touchscreen supports stylus input for illustration and photo editing, and the overall build quality — from the soft-touch keyboard to the responsive trackpad — makes it pleasant for long creative sessions. The ASIN is still listed but shows "Currently unavailable" on Amazon as of June 2026; the link will return live offers once HP restocks the SKU. [src4]

## Head-to-Head Comparisons

### LG Gram Pro 17 vs LG Gram 17
Both are sub-3.3-pound LG gram Pro variants with the same 17" 2560x1600 chassis and the 77–90Wh battery. As of mid-2026 Amazon prices them almost identically (~$2,935 vs ~$2,950) — so the Pro 17 (RTX 5050) is the clear pick because it adds a dedicated NVIDIA GPU at the same money; the Gram 17 (Intel Arc integrated) only makes sense if you specifically want the lighter Intel Arc build and slightly longer real-world battery. [src3, src7]
**Pick the Gram Pro 17 if:** you need a dedicated GPU for light gaming, CUDA/AI workloads, or video editing in a 3.3 lb chassis — at current pricing it costs the same as the Gram 17.
**Pick the Gram 17 if:** your workload is pure productivity (Office, code, browser) and you value the lightest possible 3.2 lb Intel Arc build with longer battery.

### ASUS ROG Strix G17 vs Acer Nitro V 17 AI
Both are ~6 lb gaming laptops with 17.3" FHD 144Hz panels around $1,500–$1,600. The Strix G17 (Ryzen 9 7845HX + RTX 4070, ~$1,600) is the proven, well-reviewed pick with extensive benchmark history; the Nitro V 17 AI (Ryzen 7 260 + RTX 5070, ~$1,500) is newer with Blackwell-architecture GPU and DLSS 4 support. [src5, src6]
**Pick the Strix G17 if:** you want a proven 16-core CPU with the most thoroughly reviewed gaming chassis and you value high-end cooling (Conductonaut Extreme liquid metal, MUX switch).
**Pick the Nitro V 17 AI if:** you want next-gen GPU features (DLSS 4 Multi Frame Generation, ray tracing) and a Copilot+ NPU for the same money.

### LG Gram Pro 17 vs ASUS ROG Strix G17
At Amazon's current US pricing the Gram Pro 17 (~$2,950) is almost double the Strix G17 (~$1,600) — but they serve almost opposite buyers. The Gram Pro 17 is a 3.26 lb productivity ultraportable that happens to have an RTX 5050; the Strix G17 is a 5.95 lb desktop-replacement gaming machine with a much faster RTX 4070. [src3, src6, src7]
**Pick the Gram Pro 17 if:** you carry your laptop daily, value Copilot+ NPU and 25-hour battery claims, and don't need top-tier gaming framerates.
**Pick the Strix G17 if:** you mostly game/create at a desk, want the strongest GPU per dollar in 17 inches, and don't mind 6 lbs in a bag.

### HP Laptop 17 vs HP Envy 17
Both are HP 17.3" FHD productivity laptops, but they're aimed at very different buyers. The HP Laptop 17 (~$700, Ryzen 5 + integrated, 512GB SSD) is a basic everyday machine; the HP Envy 17 (historically ~$1,000, Core i7 + RTX 3050 + touch) is a premium creative laptop currently unavailable on most Amazon listings. [src1, src4]
**Pick the HP Laptop 17 if:** you need an affordable big-screen laptop for browsing, email, and Office.
**Pick the HP Envy 17 if:** you want a touchscreen + discrete GPU for creative work — and are willing to wait for HP to restock or check non-Amazon retailers.

### Acer Nitro V 17 AI vs LG Gram Pro 17
Both ship with RTX-50-series GPUs and Copilot+ NPUs, but they're polar opposites on form-factor and price. The Nitro V 17 AI (~$1,500, 5.95 lb, RTX 5070, FHD 144Hz) is a desktop-replacement gaming laptop; the Gram Pro 17 (~$2,950, 3.26 lb, RTX 5050, 2560x1600) is the world's lightest 17-inch RTX laptop. [src3, src5, src7]
**Pick the Nitro V 17 AI if:** gaming framerates and value are the priority and the laptop stays mostly on a desk.
**Pick the Gram Pro 17 if:** weight, screen resolution, and battery life trump raw GPU performance.

--> Choose between the ASUS ROG Strix G17 (~$1,600, proven RTX 4070, FHD 144Hz) for the most thoroughly reviewed chassis or the Acer Nitro V 17 AI (~$1,500, next-gen RTX 5070, FHD 144Hz) for Blackwell architecture and DLSS 4 support. ### If portability is critical (under 4 lbs)

--> Prioritize color accuracy and touchscreen over raw GPU power. [src4, src7]

--> Only the LG Gram Pro 17 qualifies. No other manufacturer ships a 17-inch laptop with an RTX-class GPU below this weight. [src3, src7]

--> The Acer Nitro V 17 AI (~$1,500, RTX 5070) offers the best GPU-per-dollar in the segment; the ASUS ROG Strix G17 (~$1,600, RTX 4070) trades raw GPU value for a more proven thermal design and a higher-tier 16-core CPU. [src5, src6]

### Default recommendation
--> If price is no object, the LG Gram Pro 17 at ~$2,950 offers the best balance of performance, portability, and display quality. For most buyers, the ASUS ROG Strix G17 at ~$1,600 (gaming) or the HP Laptop 17 at ~$2935 (budget productivity) are the pragmatic picks while the 17-inch category continues to consolidate. ## Key Market Trends (2026)

- **Shrinking 17-inch category**: Manufacturers are increasingly shifting to 16-inch and 18-inch form factors, making true 17-inch options scarcer than in previous years. LG and HP remain the most committed to the form factor. [src1, src2]
- **Aerominum and advanced materials**: LG's new Aerominum chassis material (magnesium-aluminum alloy) enables sub-3.3-pound 17-inch laptops with dedicated GPUs, previously impossible at this screen size. [src3]
- **RTX 50-series Blackwell arrives in 17-inch**: NVIDIA's Blackwell architecture with DLSS 4 neural rendering is now available in 17-inch gaming laptops, bringing significant generational GPU improvements. [src5]
- **AI/NPU integration**: Both Intel Core Ultra and AMD Ryzen 7000 processors now include NPUs delivering 40-50+ TOPS for on-device AI tasks, enabling Copilot+ PC features across all price tiers. [src3, src5]
- **Extended battery life**: The LG Gram Pro 17 claims up to 25.5 hours of video playback with its 90Wh battery, though real-world productivity use delivers approximately 5-6 hours under moderate load. [src7]

## Important Caveats

- Prices fluctuate significantly across retailers and during sales events; the listed prices reflect approximate US street prices as of June 2026. The LG gram models in particular sit near ~$2,950 on Amazon, well above early-2026 launch pricing as inventory has tightened.
- Battery life claims vary dramatically by test methodology; manufacturer-reported video playback times can be 3-4x longer than real-world mixed usage
- The 17-inch laptop market is consolidating; some listed models may be succeeded by 16-inch or 18-inch replacements during 2026
- Gaming laptop weights (5.5-6+ lbs) effectively limit portability despite the large-screen advantage
- GPU performance varies by thermal design; the same RTX 4070 or RTX 5070 can perform 10-20% differently depending on the laptop's cooling solution and TDP configuration
