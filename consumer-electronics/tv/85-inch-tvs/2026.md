## What are the best 85-inch TVs in 2026?

## TL;DR

**Top pick: Samsung QN85QN90F (~$2,000) — Neo QLED Mini-LED with 2,500+ nits, Vision AI, and four HDMI 2.1 ports; a major price drop now puts the flagship near value-tier money.**

## Summary

The 85-inch TV market in mid-2026 spans a wide performance and price range, from ~$1,000 budget Mini-LEDs to $4,000+ premium OLEDs. Mini-LED technology dominates this size class, with the Samsung QN85QN90F (~$2,000) earning the top overall pick for its combination of Neo QLED Mini-LED brightness, Samsung Vision AI processing, and outstanding gaming features including four HDMI 2.1 ports with Game Hub — and a steep mid-year price cut (it sold above $2,800 in spring 2026) that now puts the flagship in value territory. The Hisense 85U8QG (~$1,800) still offers the best raw price-to-performance ratio with 5,000 nits peak brightness, 165Hz native refresh, and LD5600 local dimming zones. [src1, src2, src4]

For buyers willing to spend more, the LG OLED83C5PUA (~$3,300) delivers the best picture quality in this size class with perfect blacks, infinite contrast, and wide viewing angles — the only OLED option near 85 inches. The TCL 85QM8K (~$1,800) is the brightness champion among value picks with 5,000+ nits QD-Mini LED and Bang & Olufsen audio. Budget shoppers can get excellent 85-inch Mini-LED performance from the TCL 85QM6K (~$1,000) or Hisense 85U7N (~$1,100-1,400), both offering 144Hz panels, HDMI 2.1, and Dolby Vision at prices that were unimaginable two years ago. [src2, src3, src5]

## Head-to-Head Comparisons

### Samsung QN85QN90F vs Hisense 85U8QG
Both are flagship Mini-LEDs, but they target different buyers. The Samsung leans on Tizen, Vision AI processing, four HDMI 2.1 ports, and Game Hub; the Hisense out-specs it on raw brightness (5,000 vs 2,500+ nits) and refresh rate (165Hz vs 144Hz). After the Samsung's mid-2026 price cut both now land around $1,800-2,000, so the old $800 gap is largely gone — the decision is now ecosystem-and-ports (Samsung) vs raw-brightness-and-refresh (Hisense). [src1, src2, src4]

**Pick Samsung QN85QN90F if:** you own multiple consoles, want Game Hub/Vision AI, four HDMI 2.1 ports, and value Tizen polish.
**Pick Hisense 85U8QG if:** you want maximum brightness (5,000 nits) and a 165Hz panel at the same money.

### Hisense 85U8QG vs TCL 85QM8K
The two value-flagship brightness kings. Both hit 5,000+ nits with QD-Mini LED, both run 144-165Hz, and both ship with Google TV. The U8QG has more local dimming zones (LD5600) and better audio (4.1.2 with subwoofer); the QM8K runs ~$200 cheaper and adds Bang & Olufsen tuning and a wider anti-reflective coating. [src1, src4, src5]

**Pick Hisense 85U8QG if:** you want best-in-class local dimming, 165Hz, and built-in subwoofer audio.
**Pick TCL 85QM8K if:** you want the brightest panel at the lowest price and B&O-tuned sound.

### LG OLED83C5PUA vs Samsung QN85QN90F
The classic OLED-vs-Mini-LED matchup at 85 inches. The LG wins on contrast, black levels, and viewing angles — the only OLED near this size. The Samsung wins on peak brightness (2,500+ vs 1,300 nits), bright-room performance, and burn-in immunity. As of mid-2026 the Samsung (~$2,000) also costs about $1,300 less than the LG (~$3,300), so the Mini-LED now wins decisively on value too. [src2, src3, src6]

**Pick LG OLED83C5PUA if:** dark-room movies and pixel-perfect contrast are top priority.
**Pick Samsung QN85QN90F if:** the room has windows, you mix gaming/sports/streaming, or you worry about long-term OLED burn-in.

### TCL 85QM6K vs Hisense 85U7N
The two best sub-$1,400 85-inch Mini-LEDs. Both run 144Hz with HDMI 2.1 and QD color. The TCL is brighter (2,000+ vs 1,500 nits) and ~$100 cheaper on sale; the Hisense brings Dolby Vision IQ, deeper Full Array Local Dimming, and Google TV. [src3, src4, src5]

**Pick TCL 85QM6K if:** you want maximum brightness per dollar and frequent sub-$1,000 sale pricing.
**Pick Hisense 85U7N if:** you want Dolby Vision IQ and better black levels in dimmer rooms.

### Hisense 85U75QG vs TCL 85QM7K
Both target the $1,300-1,800 mid-range gamer. The Hisense has a 165Hz native panel, VRR 288, and three HDMI 2.1 ports — the gaming-spec winner at this price. The TCL counters with a 144Hz QD-Mini LED at 3,000+ nits and slightly better motion processing for movies. [src1, src4, src7]

**Pick Hisense 85U75QG if:** primary use is high-refresh gaming with Xbox Series X or PS5 Pro.
**Pick TCL 85QM7K if:** you want brighter HDR for mixed gaming, movies, and bright-room viewing.

## Key Market Trends (2026)

- **85-inch prices have collapsed**: Two years ago, a quality 85-inch Mini-LED TV cost $3,000+. In 2026, the TCL QM6K delivers capable Mini-LED at $1,000. Price-per-inch has dropped 40-50% across the segment. [src4, src5]
- **Mini-LED brightness exceeds 5,000 nits**: The Hisense U8QG and TCL QM8K both hit 5,000+ nits, making the brightness gap between LED and OLED wider than ever. For bright-room viewers, Mini-LED is the clear choice. [src1, src4]
- **OLED remains limited at 85 inches**: LG's 83-inch C5 is the only OLED option near this size under $4,000. Samsung's QD-OLED line tops out at 77 inches. True 85-inch OLED panels remain a premium niche. [src2, src3]
- **165Hz panels entering mid-range**: Hisense's U8QG and U75QG both offer 165Hz native refresh with VRR 288, features that were flagship-only in 2024. Combined with HDMI 2.1, 85-inch TVs are increasingly viable as primary gaming displays. [src1, src7]
- **Samsung Vision AI and AI processing**: The 2025 model year emphasizes AI-driven scene optimization across brands. Samsung's Vision AI, LG's AI Super Upscaling, and Sony's cognitive XR processor all use machine learning to automatically adjust picture and sound settings. [src2, src6]

## Important Caveats

- Prices are approximate US street prices as of June 2026 and move frequently in this size class — the Samsung QN90F, for example, fell from ~$2,800 to ~$2,000 between spring and mid-2026. Sales events (Prime Day, Black Friday) can drop prices a further 20-40%. Prices outside the US vary significantly.
- Availability fluctuates: as of June 2026 the Hisense 85U75QG and 85U7N show as temporarily unavailable on Amazon at times; both remain current models and restock periodically. Check the live listing before purchase.
- The LG OLED83C5PUA is 83 inches, not 85. It is included because it is the closest OLED alternative to 85-inch Mini-LED TVs.
- Brightness figures (nits) are peak measurements in small HDR highlights. Full-screen sustained brightness is typically 30-50% of peak values.
- Weight figures are approximate without stand. Add 15-25 lbs for the included stand. Professional wall mounting is recommended for TVs over 75 lbs.
- Smart TV platforms (Google TV, Tizen, webOS) collect viewing data by default. Review privacy settings during initial setup.
- 85-inch TVs require 200-450W depending on panel type and brightness. OLED consumes less power on average than full-brightness Mini-LED.
