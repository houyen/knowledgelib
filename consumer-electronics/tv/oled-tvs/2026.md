# Best OLED TVs (2026)

## What are the best OLED TVs in 2026?

## TL;DR

**Top pick: LG C6 OLED (~$1,800 for 65")** — Alpha 11 AI Gen3, 165Hz gaming, 1,355-nit HDR; now within ~$200-$500 of the C5 after continued price drops. **Best value: LG C5 OLED (~$1,300-$1,600 for 65")** — consensus best-overall MLA OLED, four HDMI 2.1, Dolby Vision. **Best budget: Samsung S90F (~$1,200 for 65")** — QD-OLED 65", 144Hz VRR, four HDMI 2.1. [src1, src2, src4]

## Summary

The 2026 OLED lineup is now fully available with all major 2026 models shipping. The LG G6 (now ~$2,800 for 65", down from $3,400 at launch) sets the picture-quality benchmark with LG Display's Primary RGB Tandem 2.0 panel, ~3,000 nits peak brightness (LG claims "3.9x brighter than conventional OLED"), and 165Hz gaming. The Samsung S95H (~$3,400 for 65") debuts a QD-OLED panel with ~2,700 nits at 10% window and adds HDR10+ Advanced -- Samsung's latest HDR format, and is now the priciest OLED in the lineup. Samsung's new mid-range S90H has dropped to ~$2,000 for 65" -- it replaces the S90F with 165Hz VRR, Glare Free 3.0 coating, and 15% more brightness than its predecessor. Both flagships ship with anti-reflective coatings. [src2, src3, src5, src7, src8]

For value, the 2025 models still dominate but the 2026 mid-range has caught up on price: the LG C5 at ~$1,300-$1,600 remains the consensus best-overall pick across RTINGS, Tom's Guide, and What Hi-Fi?. The continued price shake-up: the LG C6 (65") has fallen from ~$2,700 launch to ~$1,800 -- now only ~$200-$500 more than the C5, narrowing the value gap dramatically. The Samsung S95F sits at ~$2,200 for the 65" and Tom's Guide recommends it over the S95H at current prices. The LG G5 has firmed up to ~$2,300 for 65" as 2025 inventory tightens. The Sony Bravia 8 II is now ~$2,700 for the 65" (only ~11 in stock at Amazon as supply tightens). The Panasonic Z95B 65-inch ranges ~$1,700 at specialty retailers up to ~$3,000 at Amazon as the deepest 2025 discounts ended. The LG B5 is now ~$1,000 (back in stock but limited) at most retailers. Sony has no 2026 OLED -- the Bravia 8 II remains its flagship. [src1, src2, src4, src6, src7]

## Best for Each Use Case

### Best Overall Flagship: LG G6 OLED (~$2,800 for 65") -- [Check price](https://knowledgelib.io/go/lg-g6-oled-65)
LG's 2026 flagship debuts the Primary RGB Tandem 2.0 panel and Brightness Booster Ultra, delivering up to ~3,000 nits peak brightness -- LG claims 3.9x brighter than conventional OLED and 20% brighter than the G5. TechRadar calls it "the best OLED TV for watching in even bright rooms"; What Hi-Fi? confirms it "sets the highest possible benchmark for picture performance in 2026." Includes flush-mount wall bracket, four HDMI 2.1 ports at 4K 165Hz, 0.1ms response, NVIDIA G-SYNC and AMD FreeSync Premium, and the Alpha 11 AI Gen 3 processor. The 65" has dropped to ~$2,800 on LG.com (down from $3,400 at launch), making it notably cheaper than the Samsung S95H flagship. The G5 at ~$2,300 remains the value alternative. (Not currently stocked on Amazon -- buy direct from LG.) [src3, src6, src7]

### Brightest OLED Ever: Samsung S95H (~$3,400 for 65") -- [Check price](https://knowledgelib.io/go/samsung-s95h-oled-65)
Samsung's 2026 flagship uses a QD-OLED panel with ~35% brightness improvement over the S95F, measuring ~2,700 nits at 10% window (Samsung claims up to 3,000 nits peak). Available in 55" ($2,500), 65" ($3,400), 77" ($4,500), and 83" ($6,500; W-OLED, not QD-OLED). Features OLED Glare Free anti-reflective coating, four HDMI 2.1 ports at 4K 165Hz, NQ4 AI Gen3 processor, FreeSync Premium Pro, HDR10+ Advanced (Samsung's latest HDR format), and FloatLayer beveled metal frame with Art Mode. Tom's Guide recommends waiting for a price drop and buying the S95F instead: "The S95F is a stunning TV, and if the S95H only improves on the margins, I can't see myself recommending it over the S95F until it goes on sale." [src2, src5, src7]

### Best Value Overall: LG C5 OLED (~$1,300 for 65") -- [Check price](https://knowledgelib.io/go/lg-c5-oled-65-oled-tvs)
The LG C5 remains the consensus best-value OLED across RTINGS, Tom's Guide, and TechRadar. With the C6 now reviewed and confirmed as only a marginal upgrade, the C5 at its current sale price (~$1,300, down from $2,700 MSRP) is the strongest value pick in the 2026 OLED market. MLA-enhanced OLED evo with 1,180 nits peak brightness, Dolby Vision and Dolby Atmos, four HDMI 2.1 ports at 4K 144Hz with AMD FreeSync Premium and NVIDIA G-Sync. Available in 42", 48", 55", 65", 77", and 83". [src1, src2, src4]

### Best New Mid-Range: LG C6 OLED (~$1,800 for 65") -- [Check price](https://knowledgelib.io/go/lg-c6-oled-65)
The C6 65" has dropped from $2,700 launch to ~$1,800 (Amazon), narrowing the C6-vs-C5 gap from $1,000+ at launch to just ~$200-$500 today -- and that changes the recommendation. Alpha 11 AI Gen 3 processor, webOS 26 with Google Gemini and Microsoft Copilot integration, 165Hz gaming, four HDMI 2.1 ports, 1,355 nits HDR (vs C5's 1,180), and 9.1ms input lag. Note: 42-65" C6 uses standard W-OLED; only the 77-83" C6H gets the Primary RGB Tandem 2.0 panel with 3.2x brightness. At ~$1,800 the C6 is now the clear pick for nearly any buyer who can stretch a little beyond the C5 budget. [src2, src4]

### Best Value for Bright Rooms: Samsung S95F (~$2,200 for 65") -- [Check price](https://knowledgelib.io/go/samsung-s95f-oled-65)
With the S95H launched at $3,400, the 2025 S95F has dropped to ~$2,200 for the 65" -- still the second-brightest OLED ever measured (4,076 nits at 2% window, with OLED Glare Free matte finish). NQ4 AI Gen3 processor, 165Hz refresh, 9.4ms input lag. Tom's Guide recommends the S95F over the S95H at current prices: "I'd be hesitant to splash out on the S95H until it's had some time on the shelf to dip in price. The S95F is a stunning TV." No Dolby Vision (HDR10+ only). [src1, src2, src7]

### Best Wall-Mount Value: LG G5 OLED (~$2,300 for 65") -- [Check price](https://knowledgelib.io/go/lg-g5-oled-65-oled-tvs)
With the G6 now ~$2,800, the G5 sits at ~$2,300 for the 65" -- still LG's gallery-series OLED with "One Wall Design" that sits nearly flush, included wall mount, MLA + Tandem panel at 2,268 nits peak covering 82.42% of the Rec.2020 color gamut. 4K 165Hz, 12ms input lag. The G5-vs-G6 gap has narrowed to ~$500 as 2025-model inventory winds down, so weigh the G6's ~30% more brightness and Reflection Free Premium coating against the G5's lower price. [src1, src2, src3]

### Best for Movies and PS5: Sony Bravia 8 II (~$2,700 for 65") -- [Check price](https://knowledgelib.io/go/sony-bravia-8-ii-65)
Sony has no 2026 OLED -- the Bravia 8 II remains its flagship. Now ~$2,700 (Amazon, down from a $3,300 list) with only ~11 units in stock as supply tightens. QD-OLED panel 25% brighter than its predecessor. Sony's XR Processor with AI delivers studio-grade picture processing with the most natural colors and three-dimensional depth of any OLED tested. Acoustic Surface Audio+, exclusive PS5 calibration, two years of Sony Pictures Core. Input lag of 16.4ms limits competitive gaming. [src1, src2]

### Best for Home Theater / Audiophiles: Panasonic Z95B (~$1,700-$3,000 for 65") -- [Check price](https://knowledgelib.io/go/panasonic-z95b-65)
The only OLED supporting both Dolby Vision IQ and HDR10+ Adaptive simultaneously. Technics-tuned 360 Soundscape Pro with Dolby Atmos is powerful enough to skip a soundbar. Tandem OLED with ThermalFlow sustains 2,160 nits at 10% window. Ultra-low 9.2ms input lag. Only 2 HDMI 2.1 ports limit multi-console setups. Price has fluctuated significantly -- Tom's Guide cites ~$1,600-$1,700 at specialty retailers, while Amazon's 65" Z95BP is back up to ~$2,980 as the deepest 2025 discounts ended. Shop around. Caveat: Panasonic announced a manufacturing handoff to Skyworth in February 2026, creating uncertainty about future US support -- buy from authorized US sellers and confirm warranty coverage. [src1, src2, src6]

### Best Budget OLED: LG B5 OLED (~$1,000 for 65") -- [Check price](https://knowledgelib.io/go/lg-b5-oled-65)
The most affordable current-gen 65-inch OLED, now ~$997 on Amazon (back in stock but limited -- often only 1 unit). Perfect blacks, infinite contrast, Dolby Vision, four HDMI 2.1 ports with 4K 120Hz, and webOS 25. Peak brightness is lower than the C5 or G5, performing best in controlled lighting. The 55" is ~$800 on sale. Tom's Guide rates it the best value OLED: it supports 4K gaming at 120Hz and Dolby Vision HDR while costing significantly less than higher-end counterparts. [src1, src2]

### Best Value Samsung: Samsung S90F (~$1,200 for 65") -- [Check price](https://knowledgelib.io/go/samsung-s90f-oled-65)
Strong competitor to the LG C5 with QD-OLED in the 65" model, delivering excellent brightness and color volume. Four HDMI 2.1 ports at 4K 144Hz with FreeSync and G-Sync. Measured 9.2ms input lag. Now ~$1,198 (Amazon, down $500 from $1,697 list). Caveat: the 55" and 77" models remain subject to a "panel lottery" -- only the 65" is guaranteed QD-OLED. The S90H successor has dropped to ~$2,000 for 65" and adds 165Hz VRR, Glare Free 3.0 coating, and 15% more brightness, but the S90F at roughly half the price is still the better value pick. [src1, src2, src8]

### New Samsung Mid-Range: Samsung S90H (~$2,000 for 65") -- [Check price](https://knowledgelib.io/go/samsung-s90h-oled-65)
Samsung's 2026 replacement for the S90F. Available in 42-83" (QD-OLED in 55", 65", 77"; W-OLED Tandem in other sizes). Features 165Hz VRR (up from 144Hz), Glare Free 3.0 anti-reflective coating, NQ4 AI Gen3 processor, and OLED HDR+. 15% brighter than the S90F.
