## Summary

The 8K TV market collapsed between 2023 and 2026. TCL exited in 2023, Sony stopped producing the Z9K in 2025 with no successor, and LG halted both 8K OLED and 8K QNED LCD development in late 2025. Samsung now stands alone as the only major manufacturer releasing new 8K TVs in North America for 2026. Total 8K sales since 2015 sit at roughly 1.6 million units worldwide — versus nearly 1 billion 4K units in active use — which collapsed manufacturer interest. [src3, src5, src6]

For 2026, Samsung's lineup carries over the **QN900F** (mainstream 8K) and **QN990F** (flagship with Wireless One Connect Box) from 2025, plus the new **QN990H** — the company's only new 8K TV for 2026, which went on sale in Europe in April at 85 inches (EUR 9,999 / CHF 8,999) with a 98-inch to follow and a US launch still pending. RTINGS rates the QN990F as the single best 8K TV available, while reviews from Tom's Guide and Trusted Reviews argue the QN900F is actually better value because its upscaling on 1080p content is *cleaner* than the more expensive QN990F. Both run Samsung's NQ8 AI Gen2/Gen3 processor with hundreds of neural networks for 4K-to-8K upscaling. [src1, src2, src4, src7, src8, src10]

The practical problem in July 2026 is no longer which 8K TV is best — it is which one you can still buy. Samsung is closing out the QN900F ahead of the QN990H: the 75-inch is **Currently unavailable** on Amazon and the 65-inch is down to last-few stock, both without a live buy-box price. That pushes the 2024 **QN900D** — same NQ8 AI Gen2 processor, now ~$2,298 at 75-inch (62% off) and ~$3,499 at 85-inch (53% off) — into position as the best 8K purchase most buyers can actually complete. The QN990F remains in stock at all three sizes, with the 65-inch at ~$4,498. [src1, src7]

The remaining options — LG's QNED99T (2024) and Sony's Z9K (2022) — are end-of-life inventory with no firmware roadmap or successor. Both QNED99T SKUs now read Currently unavailable on Amazon, and Sony's Z9K has been liquidated hard: the 85-inch has fallen from ~$8,000 to ~$4,077, with single-digit stock. They are buyable only as legacy bargains, and only while stock lasts; do not expect future feature updates. Native 8K content remains essentially zero (no streamer offers it, no Blu-ray supports it), so every advantage on these TVs is realised through AI upscaling, not source resolution. [src3, src5, src6]

## Best for Each Use Case

### Best Overall (and best value): Samsung QN900D 75" (~$2,298, 62% off $5,998) — [Check price](https://knowledgelib.io/go/samsung-qn900d-75)
The best 8K TV you can actually complete a purchase on in July 2026, and the cheapest real 8K at any size. Same NQ8 AI Gen2 processor with 256 neural networks as the editorially-superior QN900F, ~2,000-nit HDR peak, 4 HDMI 2.1 ports via One Connect Box, Object Tracking Sound Pro. It gives up the QN900F's glare-free coating and Vision AI features, but it is In Stock at 62% off while the QN900F 75-inch is unavailable at any price — and at ~$2,298 it undercuts what the QN900F 75" cost even on its deepest discount (~$3,001). The 65-inch QN900D is the trap in this line: down to a single unit near ~$4,998, it costs more than a 75-inch for less screen. [src1, src7]

### Best Picture Quality (if you can find one): Samsung QN900F 75" — [Check price](https://knowledgelib.io/go/samsung-qn900f-75)
RTINGS, Tom's Guide, and Trusted Reviews all converge on the QN900F as the best 8K value of the generation. Trusted Reviews calls it "arguably the best 8K TV money can buy, not just because it's thousands cheaper" — the QN900F's 8K AI Upscaling Pro outperforms even the flagship QN990F on 1080p content (the QN990F's upscaling falls flat on lower-res sources). 1,446 local dimming zones at the 75-inch size, 1,588-nit HDR peak, native 120Hz / 240Hz VRR, glare-free coating, NQ8 AI Gen2 processor with 256 neural networks. **Caveat as of July 2026: Samsung is closing this line out ahead of the QN990H.** The 75-inch reads Currently unavailable on Amazon and the 65-inch is down to last-few units with no buy-box price; its last observed street prices were ~$3,001 and ~$2,798. Buy it if a retailer still has stock at those numbers — otherwise the QN900D 75" is the pick. [src1, src4]

### Best Flagship (Wireless Connect): Samsung QN990F 65" (~$4,498) — [Check price](https://knowledgelib.io/go/samsung-qn990f-65)
RTINGS' #1 pick overall in the 8K category, and now the only Samsung 8K line with healthy stock at every size. The QN990F's headline feature is the **Wireless One Connect Box**, which beams 8K @ 120Hz to the TV from up to 10 m via wireless. NQ8 AI Gen3 processor (newer than the QN900F's Gen2), 1,680-nit HDR peak (10% Movie-mode window), 4.2.2-ch 90 W speaker system. Catch: the wireless link adds ~36.5 ms input lag at 4K @ 60Hz — too slow for competitive gaming. The 65-inch has come down to ~$4,498 from $5,499; the 75-inch ($6,499 MSRP) and 85-inch ($9,999 MSRP) are in stock but currently show no Amazon buy-box price, so check the live page. [src2, src7, src8]

### Best for Massive Rooms (85-inch+): Samsung QN990H (EUR 9,999 / CHF 8,999 at 85")
Samsung's only new 8K TV for 2026. It went on sale in Europe in April 2026 at 85 inches (EUR 9,999 / CHF 8,999 ≈ $11,400) through Samsung's German and Swiss stores, with a 98-inch to follow; the US launch is still pending, so there is no Amazon listing yet. NQ8 AI Gen3, an 8K VA Mini-LED panel with Glare Free coating, a 6.2.4-channel 90 W Dolby Atmos speaker array, and a Wireless One Connect box carrying **five** HDMI 2.1 ports (8K @ 120Hz / 4K @ 240Hz). Runs One UI Tizen on Tizen 10. At this size the 8K-vs-4K resolution gain finally becomes detectable at typical living-room distances. [src9, src10]

### Best Big-Screen Value: Samsung QN900D 85" (~$3,499, 53% off $7,498) — [Check price](https://knowledgelib.io/go/samsung-qn900d-85)
The cheapest way into the only screen size where 8K measurably beats 4K. In Stock at ~$3,499 — down from the ~$4,500–$6,000 band it held earlier in 2026 — which puts an 85-inch 8K panel below the price of the 75-inch QN990F and barely above the 75-inch QN900D. Same NQ8 AI Gen2 processor, ~2,000-nit peak, One Connect Box. If you want the genuine 8K resolution benefit rather than the badge, this is the entry point. [src1, src7]

## Head-to-Head Comparisons

### Samsung QN900F vs Samsung QN990F
The QN990F is Samsung's flagship and RTINGS' top pick — but Trusted Reviews, T3, and Tom's Guide all argue the QN900F is better value because the QN900F's NQ8 AI Gen2 upscaler handles 1080p content *better* than the newer Gen3 in the QN990F (the Gen3 muddies low-res content according to T3). The QN990F adds wireless One Connect, a marginally newer processor, and ~5% more brightness. As of July 2026 the matchup is settled by availability rather than merit: Samsung is closing the QN900F out ahead of the QN990H, so the 75-inch is unavailable on Amazon while the QN990F is in stock at all three sizes (65" now ~$4,498, down from $5,499). [src1, src2, src4, src8]

**Pick QN900F if:** you can still find stock — it is the best 8K picture quality per dollar and most of your content is sub-4K (which it is, in 2026).
**Pick QN990F if:** you need wireless source connection, the 240Hz 4K wireless gaming mode, want the longest manufacturer support window, or simply need a Samsung 8K you can buy today.

### Samsung QN900F vs LG QNED99T
Samsung's NQ8 AI Gen2 upscaling is widely considered the gold standard, and the QN900F hits ~200 more nits of HDR peak than the LG. LG's counter-punch is **Dolby Vision support** — Samsung sets refuse to license it, sticking with HDR10+. If your media library has heavy Dolby Vision content (most Disney+ and Apple TV+ originals), the QNED99T preserves it; the QN900F does not. The LG is also EOL, so no firmware updates after late 2025 — and as of July 2026 both QNED99T SKUs read Currently unavailable on Amazon, which makes this matchup largely academic: neither side is reliably in stock. [src5, src7]

**Pick QN900F if:** you want active firmware support, the strongest upscaling engine, and HDR10+ content is fine — and you can find stock.
**Pick LG QNED99T if:** Dolby Vision is non-negotiable, you're comfortable buying a discontinued model, and you find remaining inventory. Otherwise the Sony Z9K is the fallback 8K Dolby Vision set.

### Samsung QN990F vs Sony Z9K
The QN990F is the only one of the two with a 2026-current platform — Sony exited 8K in 2025. Samsung wins on processor (NQ8 AI Gen3 vs Cognitive Processor XR from 2022), brightness (1,680 vs ~1,300 nits in HDR Movie), and wireless connectivity. Sony wins on built-in audio (8.2-ch with Acoustic Multi-Audio+ vs Samsung's 6.2.4-ch), on motion handling for sports/film, and on Dolby Vision. The clearance has now made it a price argument too: the Z9K 85" at ~$4,077 undercuts even the 75-inch QN990F, though only one unit is showing. [src2, src6, src7]

**Pick QN990F if:** you want current-gen support, the best 8K upscaler, and wireless source connection.
**Pick Sony Z9K if:** the steep clearance (85" at ~$4,077) lands while stock lasts and you care more about built-in audio and Dolby Vision than future firmware.

### Samsung QN900F vs Samsung QN900D (last gen)
Same processor family (NQ8 AI Gen2), same Object Tracking Sound Pro, same Tizen platform. The QN900F upgrades the panel coating to glare-free, bumps peak HDR brightness ~10–20%, and adds Samsung Vision AI features. On the merits the QN900F wins; in July 2026 the QN900D wins anyway, because it is the one in stock. **Pick QN900F if:** you can find stock at or near its last street price (~$3,001 at 75") and want the glare-free finish for a bright living room.
**Pick QN900D if:** you want to buy an 8K TV today — it is in stock at 75" and 85", ~$2,298 and ~$3,499, with the same processor. Avoid the 65-inch, which is stranded near ~$4,998.

## Key Market Trends (2026)

- **Sony, LG, and TCL all exited 8K**: Sony's last 8K (Z9K) discontinued in 2025 with no successor — its 2026 lineup, including the Bravia 8 II QD-OLED and the upcoming RGB Mini-LED, is exclusively 4K. LG halted both 8K OLED (Z3) and 8K QNED (QNED99T) panel development in late 2025. TCL left in 2023. Samsung is the only remaining major player. [src3, src5, src6]
- **Total cumulative 8K sales: ~1.6 M units since 2015**: Versus nearly 1 billion 4K sets in active use — manufacturers concluded the 8K bet was lost. [src5]
- **Native 8K content is still effectively zero**: No major streamer (Netflix, Disney+, Prime Video, Apple TV+, Max) offers 8K in 2026. No physical media format. Every advantage on these TVs comes from AI upscaling 4K-and-below to 8K, not source resolution. [src3]
- **Samsung's 2026 strategy is consolidation, not expansion**: Carry over QN900F and QN990F from 2025; add the QN990H as the only "new" SKU, launched in Europe in April 2026 at 85 inches (EUR 9,999 / CHF 8,999) with a 98-inch to follow and no US date yet. No QN800-class 8K refresh — Samsung's mid-range 2026 lineup is entirely 4K (QN80F/QN90F). [src9, src10]
- **The QN900F is being closed out, and Amazon 8K stock is evaporating**: By July 2026 the QN900F 75-inch and both LG QNED99T SKUs read Currently unavailable, the QN900F 65-inch and QN990F 85-inch carry stock but no buy-box price, and the QN900D 65-inch and Sony Z9K 85-inch are down to a single unit each. The 2024 QN900D is now the most reliably purchasable 8K TV in the US — a market where last year's model outlives this year's. [src9]
- **Clearance pricing has inverted the 8K ladder**: The Sony Z9K 85-inch has fallen ~49% (from ~$8,000 to ~$4,077) and the QN900D 85-inch sits at ~$3,499 (53% off), so an 85-inch legacy 8K set now costs less than a 75-inch current-gen QN990F. Buyers optimising for screen size per dollar should shop the EOL inventory, not the current lineup. [src6, src7]
- **Wireless One Connect Box is Samsung's headline 8K differentiator**: QN990F transmits 8K @ 120Hz / 4K @ 240Hz wirelessly up to 10 m. Removes the cable run problem for wall-mounted installs. Tradeoff: ~36 ms added input lag, problematic for competitive gaming. [src8]
- **EU energy efficiency rules suppress default 8K brightness**: 8K panels exceed the EEI cap by larger margins than 4K equivalents (more pixels at the same panel size = more backlight per pixel-area), forcing tighter Eco-mode defaults in EU firmware. [community reports]
- **At 75" and below, 8K is functionally invisible**: SMPTE/THX viewing-distance math shows that at typical 8 ft seating distance, 8K vs 4K is undetectable below ~85 inches even at 20/20 vision. The genuine resolution benefit only materialises on 85"+ panels at <8 ft. [src3]
