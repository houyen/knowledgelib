# Best TVs Under $300 (2026)

## What are the best TVs under $300 in 2026?

## TL;DR

**Top pick: Hisense 50QD7QF (~$280)** — only Mini-LED under $300, best contrast and HDR. **Best value: Hisense 55E6QF (~$300)** — 55-inch QLED with Dolby Vision, right at the ceiling. **Best budget: Insignia 50" F50 (~$160)** — competent 4K Fire TV for bedrooms or secondary screens. [src1, src2, src3]

## Summary

The sub-$300 TV market in 2026 is surprisingly competitive, with several brands pushing solid 4K picture quality, Dolby Vision, and capable smart platforms into this price range. The standout at this price is the Hisense 50QD7QF (~$280), which brings Mini-LED backlighting and quantum-dot color to under $300 for the first time in a 50-inch set — a feature previously reserved for $400+ TVs. [src1, src3] For buyers who prioritize screen size over panel technology, the Hisense 55E6QF (~$300) delivers a 55-inch QLED display with Dolby Vision and HDR10+ Adaptive right at the $300 ceiling — Hisense's E6 Cinema Series remains a top budget pick in 2026 roundups. [src2, src6, src9]

The rest of the under-$300 field splits into two tiers: QLED models (Hisense QD6QF, Toshiba C350) that offer decent color accuracy and HDR support, and basic LED models (Samsung DU7200, LG UT7570, Insignia F50) that prioritize brand recognition or ecosystem convenience over raw picture quality. None of these TVs support HDMI 2.1 or 4K@120Hz, so gamers needing high-refresh-rate output should look at the $300-$500 bracket instead. [src1, src4, src5]

Timing matters: 2026 model replacements from Hisense and TCL begin arriving in March-May 2026, pushing 2025 models to clearance pricing. The best deals in this bracket are typically on outgoing models with full retailer warranty intact. [src4, src8]

## Best for Each Use Case

### Best Overall: Hisense 50QD7QF (~$280) — [Check price](https://knowledgelib.io/go/hisense-50qd7qf)
The QD7QF is the only Mini-LED TV available under $300, delivering noticeably better contrast and HDR performance than any standard LED competitor at this price. Its quantum-dot layer produces wider color gamut, and the full-array local dimming reduces blooming in dark scenes. Fire TV provides a polished streaming interface with Alexa integration. [src1, src3]

### Best 55-inch: Hisense 55E6QF (~$300) — [Check price](https://knowledgelib.io/go/hisense-55e6qf)
If screen size matters more than panel technology, the 55E6QF delivers QLED color with Dolby Vision and HDR10+ Adaptive on a 55-inch display for around $300 — right at the price ceiling. The AI Light Sensor adjusts picture settings based on ambient room brightness. Solid option for living rooms where a 50-inch feels too small. [src2, src6, src9]

### Best 43-inch / Bedroom: Insignia 50" F50 (~$160) — [Check price](https://knowledgelib.io/go/insignia-f50-50)
At around $160, the Insignia F50 is the cheapest competent 4K TV in this comparison. It runs Fire TV with Alexa, has three HDMI ports including one with eARC, and delivers acceptable SDR picture quality for bedrooms, guest rooms, or dorm rooms where you want a secondary screen without breaking the bank. [src2, src8]

### Best for Gaming: Hisense 55QD6QF (~$260) — [Check price](https://knowledgelib.io/go/hisense-55qd6qf)
While no TV under $300 supports HDMI 2.1 or 4K@120Hz, the QD6QF offers Game Mode Plus with ALLM (Auto Low Latency Mode) and respectable input lag for 4K@60Hz console gaming on PS5 or Xbox Series X quality modes. Its VA panel delivers deeper blacks than IPS alternatives, which helps in darker game environments. [src5, src6]

### Best for Samsung Users: Samsung 50" DU7200 (~$250) — [Check price](https://knowledgelib.io/go/samsung-du7200-50)
If you are already invested in Samsung's ecosystem (SmartThings, Galaxy phones), the DU7200 offers seamless integration via Tizen OS. Picture quality is middle-of-the-road for this price — adequate for streaming but lacking Dolby Vision support. PurColor technology provides reasonable color accuracy for SDR content. [src7, src8]

### Best for Wide Viewing Angles: LG 55UT7570PUB (~$280) — [Check price](https://knowledgelib.io/go/lg-55ut7570pub)
The LG UT75 series uses an IPS panel, which maintains consistent color and brightness at wide viewing angles — a significant advantage over VA-panel competitors when multiple people watch from different positions in the room. Trade-off: lower native contrast ratio means weaker black levels in dark scenes. webOS is a mature platform with good app support. [src4, src7]

### Best for Streaming: TCL 55S450G (~$230) — [Check price](https://knowledgelib.io/go/tcl-55s450g)
For households centered on Google's ecosystem, the S450G runs Google TV with built-in Chromecast, Google Assistant, and Matter smart home support. At ~$230 for a 55-inch 4K display with Dolby Vision, it is one of the most affordable ways to get a large-screen streaming hub. [src4, src6]

### Best for Roku Users: Roku 55" Select Series (~$270) — [Check price](https://knowledgelib.io/go/roku-select-55)
The Roku-made Select Series is one of the cleanest streaming experiences at this price, with Roku OS, Smart Picture mode that suppresses the soap-opera effect, and an enhanced voice remote with a lost-remote finder. HDR support is HDR10 only (no Dolby Vision), and the 60Hz LED panel is middle-of-the-pack — buy it for the Roku UI, not the picture. [src9]

## Head-to-Head Comparisons

### Hisense 50QD7QF vs Hisense 55E6QF
The QD7QF wins on panel technology — its Mini-LED backlight with local dimming delivers noticeably deeper blacks and stronger HDR highlights than the E6QF's edge-lit QLED. The E6QF wins on screen real estate at the same approximate price. [src1, src2]

**Pick QD7QF if:** You watch a lot of HDR movies or dark scenes and want the best picture under $300.<br>**Pick E6QF if:** Screen size matters more than peak picture quality and the room has moderate ambient light.

### Hisense 55E6QF vs TCL 55S450G
Both are 55-inch sub-$260 options. The E6QF is a QLED with Dolby Vision and HDR10+ Adaptive; the S450G is basic LED with Dolby Vision but on the Google TV platform. Picture quality goes to Hisense; ecosystem goes to TCL. [src2, src4]

**Pick E6QF if:** You want the best 55-inch picture under $260 and don't care about smart platform.<br>**Pick S450G if:** You're in Google's ecosystem (Chromecast, Google Home, Matter) and want the platform polish.

### Insignia 50" F50 vs TCL 50S551F
Both are entry-level Fire TV 50-inch sets. The Insignia F50 is ~$30 cheaper but lacks Dolby Vision. The TCL 50S551F adds Dolby Vision and slightly better color but rarely drops below $220. [src2, src8]

**Pick F50 if:** Budget is the only thing that matters and you want under $200.<br>**Pick 50S551F if:** You stream Dolby Vision content (Netflix, Disney+) and the extra $30 is acceptable.

### LG 55UT7570PUB vs Hisense 55A7NF
Both are 55-inch LED sets around $250-$280. The LG uses an IPS panel for wide viewing angles; the Hisense uses VA for deeper blacks. LG runs webOS, Hisense runs Fire TV. [src4, src7]

**Pick UT7570 if:** Multiple viewers sit at sharp angles to the screen (family room, kitchen TV).<br>**Pick A7NF if:** Most viewing is head-on and dark-scene contrast matters more.

### Roku 55" Select Series vs Hisense 55E6QF
Both are 55-inch sub-$280 options. The E6QF wins on panel quality (QLED + Dolby Vision vs basic LED + HDR10). The Roku wins on smart-TV UX — Roku OS is generally the cleanest and most ad-restrained budget streaming platform. [src2, src9]

**Pick Roku Select if:** You value the Roku interface above all else and watch mostly SDR content.<br>**Pick E6QF if:** Picture quality and HDR support matter more than the streaming UI.

## Decision Logic

### If budget < $200
--> Insignia 50" F50 (~$160) is the cheapest competent 4K option here. Expect basic HDR and slower smart TV performance, but adequate picture quality for a secondary screen. [src2, src8]

### If primary use is movie streaming and picture quality matters most
--> Hisense 50QD7QF (~$280). The Mini-LED backlight with local dimming provides the best contrast and HDR experience under $300 by a significant margin. [src1, src3]

### If screen size is the top priority
--> Hisense 55E6QF (~$300). Largest screen with decent QLED picture quality right at the ceiling. The 55-inch display is 21% more screen area than a 50-inch. [src2, src6]

### If user needs wide viewing angles (family room with off-axis seating)
--> LG 55UT7570PUB (~$280). IPS panel maintains consistent colors at 60+ degree angles where VA panels visibly wash out. Accept weaker black levels as the trade-off. [src4, src7]

### If user is in the Samsung or Google ecosystem
--> Samsung DU7200 ($250) for Samsung/Tizen users, TCL S450G ($230) for Google/Chromecast users. Ecosystem integration reduces friction more than marginal picture quality differences at this price. [src7, src6]

### Default recommendation
--> Hisense 50QD7QF (~$280) for most buyers. Best balance of picture quality, features, and price. Only step down to the 55E6QF or QD6QF if 55-inch screen size is a hard requirement. [src1, src3]

## Key Market Trends (2026)

- **Mini-LED reaches sub-$300**: The Hisense QD7QF is the first Mini-LED TV under $300, a technology that was exclusive to $500+ models just one year ago. Expect TCL to follow with a sub-$300 Mini-LED by late 2026. [src1, src3]
- **QLED becomes baseline under $300**: Quantum-dot QLED panels are now standard at the $250 price point across Hisense and TCL lineups, replacing basic LED as the default technology. [src2, src5]
- **Fire TV dominance**: Amazon's Fire TV platform runs on the majority of sub-$300 TVs (Hisense, Toshiba, Insignia), making Alexa the default voice assistant at this price tier. Google TV and Tizen hold smaller shares. [src4, src8]
- **Clearance cycle creates value**: 2025 model clearance (March-May 2026) pushes prices 20-35% below launch MSRP, making this one of the best times to buy in the sub-$300 segment. [src4, src8]
- **Roku-made TVs gain share at $250-$300**: Roku's Select Series (sold direct under Roku branding) is now a mainstream pick for sub-$300 buyers prioritizing the Roku UI over panel technology. Yahoo Tech's May 2026 budget roundup lists the 55-inch Select Series at $268 — usually under $300 — as a top pick. [src9]

## Important Caveats

- Prices fluctuate frequently — check current pricing at point of purchase, as deals and clearance events can shift models in or out of the $300 threshold
- Audio quality is uniformly weak across all sub-$300 TVs — budget $50-$100 for a soundbar if audio matters
- HDR performance is limited at this price even on the best models — peak brightness rarely exceeds 400-500 nits, compared to 1000+ nits on mid-range TVs
- No VRR (Variable Refresh Rate) or HDMI 2.1 at this price point — competitive gamers should budget $400+ minimum
- Smart TV platforms may receive fewer years of software updates compared to premium models — expect 3-4 years of OS support
