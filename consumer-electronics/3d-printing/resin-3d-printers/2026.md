# Best Resin 3D Printers (2026)

## What are the best resin 3D printers in 2026?

## TL;DR

**Top pick: Elegoo Saturn 4 Ultra 16K (~$494) — 16K mono LCD + AI monitoring + tilt release + tank heating, the proven mid-range workhorse of 2026.**
**Best value: Creality HALOT-X1 (~$399) — the same 16K resolution and 170mm/h speed, now cut to ~$399 (from ~$579), the cheapest 16K printer.**

## Summary

The resin 3D printer market in mid-2026 is dominated by MSLA (masked stereolithography) technology, with 16K resolution now available from three manufacturers and print speeds reaching 170mm/h. The best overall pick is the Elegoo Saturn 4 Ultra 16K (~$494), which combines a 16K mono LCD (15,120 x 6,230 pixels) with AI-powered print monitoring, automatic leveling, and smart tank heating. The biggest June 2026 shake-up is the Creality HALOT-X1, whose moving-vat top-down 16K design has been cut to ~$399 (from ~$579-599) — the same resolution and a faster 170mm/h speed than the Saturn 4 Ultra for roughly $95 less, making it the new best-value 16K pick. The Anycubic Photon Mono M5s Pro (~$579) brings 14K resolution with leveling-free technology and integrated air heating, though it is now sold direct via Anycubic (out of stock on Amazon). For the best budget pick, the Elegoo Mars 5 Ultra (~$285) delivers 9K resolution with 18-micron XY accuracy. [src1, src2, src7]

The mid-range segment is fiercely competitive: the Anycubic Photon Mono M7 Pro (~$450) pushes 14K resolution at 170mm/h with dynamic heating, while the Creality Halot Mage S 14K (~$440) matches that resolution with 90%+ light uniformity and a built-in air purifier. For professionals, the Formlabs Form 4 (~$4,499, sold direct by Formlabs) uses proprietary Low Force Display technology for industrial-grade reliability and surface finish. Large-format users have excellent options in the Anycubic Photon Mono M7 Max (now ~$650) and Phrozen Sonic Mega 8K S (~$1,200). The resolution arms race is effectively over — 16K on a mid-range machine is now standard, and the features that separate good printers from great ones in 2026 are quality-of-life and reliability improvements. All consumer MSLA printers now use monochrome LCD screens that cure each layer in 1.5-3 seconds and last 2,000+ hours. [src1, src5, src6]

## Best for Each Use Case

### Best Overall: Elegoo Saturn 4 Ultra 16K (~$494) — [Check price](https://knowledgelib.io/go/elegoo-saturn-4-ultra-16k)
The Saturn 4 Ultra 16K represents the pinnacle of consumer resin printing in 2026 with its 10-inch 16K mono LCD delivering 15,120 x 6,230 pixel resolution at 14 x 19 micron XY accuracy. The smart tank heating system maintains resin at 30 degrees C for consistent curing even in cold workshops, and the tilt release technology enables rapid model separation at speeds up to 150mm/h without sacrificing quality. AI camera monitoring detects empty build plates and model warping in real time, while automatic leveling eliminates manual setup entirely. The street price settled to ~$494 in June 2026 (down from $500-520 earlier in the year, a 24% discount off the $649.99 list). It remains the proven, lowest-risk 16K pick, though the newly discounted Creality HALOT-X1 (~$399) now undercuts it on price. [src1, src3]

### Best Budget: Elegoo Mars 5 Ultra (~$285) — [Check price](https://knowledgelib.io/go/elegoo-mars-5-ultra)
At under $300, the Mars 5 Ultra packs an astonishing feature set: 9K resolution with 18-micron XY accuracy, automatic leveling via mechanical sensor, resin level detection, integrated webcam monitoring, Wi-Fi connectivity, and the same tilt release system found in the premium Saturn line. The ~$285 street price makes this arguably the best value in all of 3D printing. The compact 153 x 78 x 165mm build volume is ideal for miniatures and small parts, and the 150mm/h print speed matches printers costing twice as much. [src1, src2, src4]

### Best for Speed and Detail: Anycubic Photon Mono M7 Pro (~$450) — [Check price](https://knowledgelib.io/go/anycubic-photon-mono-m7-pro)
The M7 Pro leads the market in raw speed at 170mm/h with high-speed resin, achieved through the LighTurbo 3.0 COB light system with Fresnel lens optics. The 14K LCD (13,312 x 5,120 pixels) with 16.8 x 24.8 micron pixel size captures excellent detail. Dynamic heating maintains optimal resin temperature, and the optional auto-fill system enables extended unattended printing. Street price has settled near $450 — a 33% discount off the $669.99 list — making this the strongest value pick in the 14K segment. Advanced users will appreciate the 385nm wavelength light source that supports both standard and high-speed resins. [src1, src5]

### Best Value Mid-Range: Creality Halot Mage S 14K (~$440) — [Check price](https://knowledgelib.io/go/creality-halot-mage-s-14k)
The Halot Mage S matches the Anycubic M7 Pro's 14K resolution and identical 223 x 126 x 230mm build volume at a slightly lower price point. Its Integral Light Source 3.0 achieves 90%+ light uniformity for consistent curing across the entire build plate, and the Dynax+ mode boosts speeds 3-4x, completing a 15cm model in approximately one hour. The built-in activated carbon air purifier reduces resin odors, and dimensional accuracy of 0.02-0.09mm suits engineering prototypes. [src2, src5, src7]

### Best Professional: Formlabs Form 4 (~$4,499) — [Check price](https://knowledgelib.io/go/formlabs-form-4)
The Form 4 uses proprietary Low Force Display (LFD) technology with an ultra-high-power backlight (16 mW/cm2) and a dual-layer flexible film resin tank rated for 75,000+ layers. This delivers industrial-grade reliability with consistent print quality print after print. The 200 x 125 x 210mm build volume is modest, but the 40% reduction in cost per part versus the Form 3+ comes from 3.5x higher throughput. The extensive Formlabs resin library covers engineering, dental, medical, and jewelry applications. [src1, src7]

### Best Large Format: Anycubic Photon Mono M7 Max (~$650) — [Check price](https://knowledgelib.io/go/anycubic-photon-mono-m7-max)
With a massive 298 x 164 x 300mm build volume (14.7 liters), the M7 Max is the go-to choice for large models, terrain pieces, and batch production. The 13.6-inch 7K mono screen with COB + Fresnel lens light system achieves 90%+ uniformity across the entire large build area. Intelligent Release 2.0 with pressure sensors prevents print failures on large cross-sections. The auto-resin refill function enables truly unattended batch runs, and dynamic temperature control maintains print quality in varying ambient conditions. Street price dropped further to ~$650 in June 2026 (a 7% discount off the $699.99 list), narrowing the gap to the smaller mid-range printers. [src2, src3, src5]

### Best for Miniatures: Phrozen Sonic Mighty 8K (~$450) — [Check price](https://knowledgelib.io/go/phrozen-sonic-mighty-8k)
The Sonic Mighty 8K hits the sweet spot between resolution and build area for miniature painters and tabletop gaming enthusiasts. Street price holds at ~$450 (vs $899 MSRP) — making this 8K printer far more competitive against newer 14K-16K rivals. [src1, src4]

The HALOT-X1 introduces a genuinely novel mechanical approach: a top-down movable optical system where the resin vat moves downward while the build plate stays stationary, improving printing stability and reducing layer lines. The 10.1-inch 16K LCD (15,120 x 6,230 pixels) matches the Saturn 4 Ultra at 14 x 19 micron XY accuracy, and the 170mm/h print speed ties for the fastest in the consumer segment. Intelligent zoned exposure divides the light source into 92 independent zones, exposing only where needed and extending LCD lifespan. The twist-release build plate ejects prints in about three seconds. The Combo version adds automated resin feeding with laser level detection. In June 2026 its street price was cut to ~$399 (from ~$579-599, a 15% discount off the $470 list of the base unit) — that drop makes it the cheapest 16K printer on the market and a stronger value than the Saturn 4 Ultra (~$494) for buyers who want the latest mechanical design. [src2, src7]

The M5s Pro builds on the popular M5s with a 10.1-inch 14K LCD (13,312 x 5,120 pixels) delivering 17 x 25 micron XY accuracy across a generous 224 x 168 x 200mm build volume — the widest in its price class. The floating platform leveling-free system with internal sensors eliminates manual calibration entirely. The new Air Heater & Purifier module integrates heating (up to 40 degrees C) with air filtration. AI-powered resin residue detection prevents screen damage, and real-time peeling height adjustment improves success rates. Average print speed reaches 105mm/h with third-generation ACF film. As of June 2026 the M5s Pro is out of stock on Amazon and is sold direct from Anycubic at ~$579; buyers who want a leveling-free machine in stock should consider the Creality HALOT-X1 (~$399) instead. [src2, src6]

### Elegoo Saturn 4 Ultra 16K vs Creality HALOT-X1
Both ship the same 16K mono LCD (15,120 x 6,230 pixels) at 14 x 19 micron XY accuracy. The Saturn 4 Ultra is the proven workhorse at ~$494 with tilt release and AI monitoring; the HALOT-X1, now cut to ~$399 (from ~$579-599), introduces a moving-vat top-down design that ejects prints in 3 seconds via twist release and uses 92 zoned exposures to extend LCD lifespan. After June 2026's price cut the HALOT-X1 is both faster (170mm/h vs 150mm/h) and ~$95 cheaper; the Saturn 4 Ultra still wins on a more mature ecosystem, AI camera, and the largest community. [src1, src2, src7]

**Pick Saturn 4 Ultra 16K if:** you want the proven, lowest-risk 16K setup with mature firmware, AI camera, and the largest community.
**Pick HALOT-X1 if:** you want the fastest speed (170mm/h) and the lowest-priced 16K printer with the novel top-down mechanism.

### Anycubic Photon Mono M7 Pro vs Creality Halot Mage S 14K
Identical 14K resolution, identical 223x126x230mm build volume, and similar street prices (~$450 vs ~$440). The M7 Pro wins on raw speed (170mm/h vs 150mm/h) and the larger COB LighTurbo 3.0 light engine with dynamic heating. The Halot Mage S 14K wins on light uniformity (90%+) and includes a built-in activated carbon air purifier that the M7 Pro omits. [src1, src2, src5, src7]

**Pick Anycubic M7 Pro if:** print speed matters more than odor control and you want the dynamic heating system.
**Pick Halot Mage S 14K if:** you want the cheapest 14K-class printer with built-in air filtration and don't need >150mm/h speeds.

### Elegoo Mars 5 Ultra vs Anycubic Photon Mono 4
Both budget picks under $300. The Mars 5 Ultra (~$285) brings 9K resolution at 18-micron XY with the same tilt release and AI camera found in the premium Saturn line — 150mm/h speeds. The Photon Mono 4 (~$160) is even cheaper but uses a slower 70mm/h vertical-pull mechanism with no AI monitoring. The Mars 5 Ultra is the clear winner on features per dollar; the Photon Mono 4 wins only on absolute lowest price. [src1, src2, src4]

**Pick Mars 5 Ultra if:** you can stretch to ~$285 and want tilt release + AI + WiFi.
**Pick Photon Mono 4 if:** you need to stay near $160 and accept slower prints with no auto-monitoring.

### Phrozen Sonic Mighty 8K vs Anycubic Photon Mono M5s Pro
Two mid-range picks targeting different users. ## Important Caveats

- Prices shown are approximate U.S. retail prices as of late June 2026. Sales, bundles, and regional pricing vary. Elegoo and Anycubic frequently run 20-40% promotions, and 8K-class printers are being aggressively discounted as 14K-16K becomes the new mid-range standard. - Resin 3D printing requires post-processing: printed parts must be washed in isopropyl alcohol (or water-washable resin solvent) and UV-cured. A wash-and-cure station ($80-200) is a near-essential accessory not included in printer prices.
- Uncured resin is toxic and a skin sensitizer. Nitrile gloves, adequate ventilation, and safe disposal are mandatory. Many newer printers include air purifiers, but dedicated ventilation is still recommended.
- Build volume does not equal usable volume in all cases. Tilt release mechanisms, support structures, and resin drainage reduce effective print area by 5-15%.
- Print speed claims are maximum figures using high-speed resin and optimal settings. Real-world speeds with standard resin are typically 30-50% lower.
