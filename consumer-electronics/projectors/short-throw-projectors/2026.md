The short throw and ultra-short throw (UST) projector market in June 2026 has continued to shift decisively toward RGB triple-laser 4K models, with two flagships now both shipping at retail: the **AWOL Vision Aetherion Max** (now live on Amazon at ~$3,824, list $4,499, ProjectorCentral Highly Recommended at a measured 3,031 ANSI lumens) and the **Valerion VisionMaster Max** (street ~$3,399 down from $4,999 MSRP, 3,500 ISO lumens, short-throw 0.9-1.5:1). For buyers on a smaller budget, the **Hisense PT1** (~$2,198) holds its ProjectorCentral Highly Recommended status. The **Hisense C2 Ultra** has dropped to ~$1,898 as a flexible short-throw (not UST) with 0.9-1.5:1 throw ratio, 360-degree rotation, and JBL audio — now the cheapest model in the comparison. The **NOMVDIC P2000UST-RGB** remains delisted from amazon.com (buy link now points to the official NOMVDIC store, ~$3,299); buyers seeking a sub-$2,000 4K triple-laser short-throw should look at the C2 Ultra or the Formovie Theater (~$1,900). ## Best for Each Use Case

### Best Overall UST: Hisense PX3-PRO (~$2,498) — [Check price](https://knowledgelib.io/go/hisense-px3-pro-short-throw-projectors)
Still #1 on ProjectorCentral's 2026 list after a year on the market. The PX3-PRO's RGB triple-laser engine delivers 3,000 ANSI lumens with 110% BT.2020 color gamut coverage, producing vibrant, accurate colors that outperform most competitors at this price. The 50W Harman Kardon sound system with Dolby Atmos eliminates the need for external speakers in most rooms. As the world's first UST certified "Designed for Xbox," it supports 240Hz refresh rates and Auto Low Latency Mode for serious gaming. The 0.22:1 throw ratio projects 80-150 inches from just inches away. Amazon street price has continued to ease — now ~$2,498 (down from $2,696 in spring 2026 and the $3,499 launch MSRP), making it the best-priced flagship-class UST in the comparison. [src1, src3, src4]

### Best Brightness / Flagship: Hisense L9Q (~$4,998) — [Check price](https://knowledgelib.io/go/hisense-l9q)
Launched in late 2025 and rapidly promoted to ProjectorCentral's #3 UST, the L9Q is now the brightest consumer UST at 5,000 ANSI lumens — roughly 45% brighter than the Samsung Premiere 9 and enough to compete with afternoon sunlight through windows. Amazon street has fallen to ~$4,998 (list $5,999) in June 2026, down from ~$5,998 in spring. The 0.18:1 throw ratio projects up to 200 inches from 6-8 inches away. Pantone-validated color, triple RGB laser, and a 10-speaker 6.2.2 Devialet-designed audio system (most powerful built-in projector audio ever shipped) make it the most complete flagship. TechRadar calls it "a new ultra short throw benchmark." Its only peers are the Samsung Premiere 9 and the just-shipping AWOL Aetherion Max. [src1, src5, src10]

### Best New 2026 Flagship: AWOL Vision Aetherion Max (~$3,824, list $4,499) — [Check price](https://knowledgelib.io/go/awol-vision-aetherion-max)
Unveiled at CES 2026 and now shipping at retail (Amazon ASIN B0GN3XX19L, ~$3,824 street against a $4,499 list) after a record-setting $10M+ Kickstarter campaign, the Aetherion Max delivers a measured 3,031 ANSI lumens (manufacturer rates 3,300 ISO) with an RGB triple-laser engine, 6,000:1 native contrast (up to 22,000:1 dynamic with Enhanced Black Level per ProjectorCentral testing), and AWOL's new PixelLock optical alignment system that addresses the chromatic aberration and edge softness common on UST projectors. 240Hz refresh rate with VRR/ALLM and 3-14ms measured input latency make it a compelling alternative to the PX3-PRO for gaming. ProjectorCentral awarded it Highly Recommended in April 2026 and AVS Forum named it the 2026 Top Choice. Note: ProjectorCentral flagged highlight clipping in HDR with EBL enabled as a known issue under firmware development. Kickstarter backers received units at $2,299; that price is no longer available to new buyers. [src1, src7, src9, src11]

### Best Short-Throw Image Quality: Valerion VisionMaster Max (~$3,399) — [Check price](https://knowledgelib.io/go/valerion-visionmaster-max)
Unlike true UST models, the VisionMaster Max is a short-throw projector with a 0.9-1.5:1 throw ratio and optical zoom that projects up to 300 inches diagonal from 7+ feet away. Engadget calls its image quality "near-perfect." The 3,500 ISO lumen tri-laser light engine, flagship black levels, IMAX Enhanced certification, 3D support, HDR10+, and automated lens shift/zoom/focus place it in direct competition with $8,000-$10,000 long-throw flagships. 4ms 240Hz input mode handles gaming as well as the PX3-PRO. Inventory adjustments through H1 2026 dropped street price from a $4,999 MSRP to roughly $3,399 (list $3,999) by June. The best choice if you need short-throw flexibility (not UST) with reference-grade image quality. [src2, src8]

### Best Premium Living-Room UST: Samsung Premiere 9 LPU9D (~$4,614) — [Check price](https://knowledgelib.io/go/samsung-premiere-9-lpu9d)
Samsung's second-generation Premiere delivers 3,200 ANSI lumens (3,450 ISO claimed) with a triple RGB laser covering 154% of DCI-P3 color volume. The integrated 40W Dolby Atmos 2.2.2 speaker system is the most powerful built-in audio of any mainstream UST, often eliminating the need for a soundbar. Running on Tizen OS, it provides native access to Netflix, Disney+, YouTube, and all major streaming apps without an external streaming device. AI-powered Vision Booster adjusts contrast based on ambient lighting in real time. It trails the Hisense L9Q in raw brightness but remains more intuitive and better-integrated for a family TV replacement. Amazon street has dropped to ~$4,614 in June 2026 (list $5,998) — down ~9% from the spring $5,057. [src1, src6]

### Best for Bright Rooms: Epson EpiqVision Ultra LS800 (~$2,352) — [Check price](https://knowledgelib.io/go/epson-epiqvision-ultra-ls800)
The LS800's 4,000 ISO lumen output remains among the highest of any UST projector and is the only sub-$4,000 model that can match the Hisense L9Q for daytime viewing. Epson's 3LCD technology eliminates the rainbow effect (color fringing) that affects every single-chip DLP projector in this comparison, a significant advantage for viewers sensitive to this artifact. ## Important Caveats

- Prices are US Amazon street prices as of June 2026 and may vary by region and retailer. Amazon and specialty dealer prices on flagships like the PX3-PRO and L9Q frequently run 5-15% below list.
- Most UST projectors require a dedicated ALR (ambient light rejecting) screen for best results, which adds $500-2,000 to the total cost. Using a flat white wall results in significantly washed-out images.
- Throw ratios and screen sizes are manufacturer-specified maximums. Actual image quality may degrade at extreme sizes, particularly for models under 3,000 lumens.
- The Hisense C2 Ultra and Valerion VisionMaster Max are short-throw projectors (0.9-1.5:1 throw ratio), not ultra-short-throw. They require significantly more distance from the wall than true UST models — do not buy expecting to sit the unit inches below the screen.
- The AWOL Aetherion Max retail listing is now live on Amazon (ASIN B0GN3XX19L, ~$3,824 street against a $4,499 list). Kickstarter backers received units at $2,299; that price is no longer available to new buyers. ProjectorCentral has flagged a known HDR highlight-clipping issue with Enhanced Black Level enabled — AWOL has acknowledged this and is addressing via firmware.
- The NOMVDIC P2000UST-RGB is no longer sold on amazon.com; its buy link points to the official NOMVDIC store (nomvdic.io, ~$3,299). The LG CineBeam HU715Q (B09S6S83MD) is Currently Unavailable on Amazon as of June 2026 — a temporary stock gap; verify availability before purchase.
- DLP projectors (every non-Epson model in this list) may exhibit "rainbow effect" (brief flashes of color) visible to some viewers. Epson's 3LCD technology is the only UST option that avoids this completely.
- Manufacturer lumen ratings use different standards. ANSI lumens (PX3-PRO, L9Q, PT1, HU715Q, Aurora Pro MKII, P2000UST-RGB) and ISO 21118 lumens (LS800, Premiere 9, VisionMaster Max, Aetherion Max, LTV-3000 Pro, Formovie) produce roughly comparable numbers but are not identical tests.
- Built-in smart platforms and audio, while convenient, do not match the quality of dedicated streaming devices and external sound systems for serious home theater setups.
