# Best Photo Printers for Home Use (2026)

## What are the best photo printers for home use in 2026?

## TL;DR

**Top pick: Canon imagePROGRAF PRO-310 (~$749)** — pigment-based 13"/A3+ printer with 200-year archival prints, the successor to the discontinued PRO-300 and now in stock for ~$110 less.
**Best value: Epson EcoTank Photo ET-8550 (~$679)** — A3+ all-in-one with bottle-fed 6-ink system, ~$0.04 per 4x6 print (10x cheaper than cartridges).
**Best budget: Canon SELPHY CP1500 (~$159)** — pocket-sized 4x6 dye-sub printer with WiFi + SD slot, ~100-year archival prints, no clog risk.
The 2026 photo-printer market splits into four real categories: pro pigment (PRO-310/P700/P900), dye photo (PRO-200S), all-in-one EcoTank (ET-8500/8550), and portable dye-sub or instant film (SELPHY, Liene, Instax, Polaroid). Pick the category first — never compare across them on price alone. ## Summary

The 2026 home photo-printer market is dominated by four product categories that solve different problems and should not be cross-shopped on price. The single biggest change this year: **Canon retired both of its A3+ Pro printers.** The PIXMA PRO-200 and imagePROGRAF PRO-300 are discontinued and now read "Currently unavailable" on Amazon — replaced by the **imagePROGRAF PRO-310 (~$749)** and **PIXMA PRO-200S (~$530)**, both in stock and cheaper than the models they replace. [<a href="https://www.digitalcameraworld.com/tech/printers/canon-just-replaced-its-best-ever-13-inch-a3-printer">src11</a>, <a href="https://www.northlight-images.co.uk/canon-pro-310-printer-review/">src9</a>]

**Dedicated pro photo printers** (Canon imagePROGRAF PRO-310 ~$749, Epson SureColor P700 ~$699, Epson SureColor P900 ~$1,145) use 9-10 pigment inks for archival fine-art prints up to 13" or 17" wide and target serious photographers. Keith Cooper calls the PRO-310 "an evolution of the PRO-300, not a whole new printer" — same print head, but Lucia PRO II inks with better light and scratch resistance, an extended magenta gamut, and panorama length doubled to 1.8m. The **Canon PIXMA PRO-200S (~$530)** sits one tier below with 8 dye inks, and at ~$219 less than the PRO-310 it restores the price gap that the old PRO-200's mid-2026 spike had erased — so the dye-vs-pigment choice is once again about budget as well as archival life and media range. [<a href="https://www.northlight-images.co.uk/canon-pro-310-printer-review/">src9</a>, <a href="https://www.digitalcameraworld.com/tech/printers/canon-pixma-pro-200s-review-for-large-format-glossy-photo-prints-this-is-as-good-as-it-gets">src10</a>, <a href="https://www.trustedreviews.com/best/best-photo-printer-4261232">src3</a>]

The **all-in-one EcoTank class** (Epson ET-8500 at A4/8.5", ET-8550 at A3+/13") delivers near-pro photo quality alongside document scanning and copying, with bottle-fed ink that drops cost-per-4x6 to about ~$0.04 versus ~$0.40 for cartridge competitors — Epson's own claim, validated by Northlight Images. The ET-8550 ships with enough ink for ~2,300 photos. [<a href="https://www.northlight-images.co.uk/epson-et-8550-printer-review/">src7</a>, <a href="https://www.cnn.com/cnn-underscored/reviews/best-photo-printer">src2</a>]

For **portable and casual** use, the dye-sublimation Canon SELPHY CP1500 (~$159) remains Wirecutter and PC Guide's top pick for 4x6 prints with WiFi + SD card support and ~100-year archival rating. The **Liene M200 (~$170)** is a battery-powered 4x6 dye-sub alternative with 40 prints per charge. For instant-style sharing, **Fujifilm Instax Mini Link 3 (~$107)** prints credit-card-sized real instant film, the **Instax Square Link (~$130)** prints square 2.4" prints, and the **Polaroid Hi-Print 2x3 Gen 2 (~$140)** uses dye-sub (not Zink) for higher-quality 2x3 prints. [<a href="https://www.digitalcameraworld.com/buying-guides/best-portable-printers-for-photos">src5</a>, <a href="https://www.photoworkout.com/best-portable-photo-printers/">src6</a>, <a href="https://www.pcguide.com/printer/guide/best-photo-printer/">src4</a>]

## Best for Each Use Case

### Best Overall (Pro / Archival): Canon imagePROGRAF PRO-310 (~$749) — [Check price](https://knowledgelib.io/go/canon-imageprograf-pro-310)
The direct successor to the discontinued PRO-300, and the new consensus 13"/A3+ pick for serious photographers. Keith Cooper (Northlight Images) calls it "an evolution of the PRO-300, not a whole new printer": the print head and basic design carry over, but the Lucia PRO II ink set adds better light and scratch resistance, an extended magenta gamut, a bronzing-reduction quality mode for glossy papers, and doubles maximum page length to 1.8m for panoramas. Ten ink channels (9 pigment colors + a Chroma Optimizer clear coat) give it wide paper compatibility — canvas, fine art, thicker stock — plus ~200-year print durability. Cooper's verdict: "the Canon PRO-310 is an excellent printer. It's a printer, which if you get duff prints out of it, it is almost certainly a limitation of yours, not the printer." Downsides: small 14.4ml non-upgradeable cartridges, no replaceable maintenance tank, a dated 3-inch LCD, and 2.4GHz-only Wi-Fi. At ~$749 it currently sells for about $110 less than the PRO-300 it replaced. [src9, src11, src1]

### Best Dye Photo Printer (Vivid Color, Lower Ink Cost): Canon PIXMA PRO-200S (~$530) — [Check price](https://knowledgelib.io/go/canon-pixma-pro-200s)
The successor to the discontinued PRO-200, and the pick for hobbyist photographers who want exceptional A3+ photo prints with vivid glossy color. The 8-ink ChromaLife dye system includes three monochrome inks (black, gray, light gray) for B&W, and prints a bordered A3+ in ~90 seconds. Digital Camera World rates it 5 stars and concludes: "For vibrant color photo prints on glossy and luster paper, I think there's no beating the Canon Pixma Pro-200S" — the small dye molecules absorb fully beneath the paper's protective layer for "a completely smooth and uniform finish" without the bronzing pigment printers show on gloss. The trade-offs are real: it is "less ideal for matte paper" and fine-art media, and its dye inks are rated for 100+ years stored in an album versus 200+ for pigment. At ~$530 it undercuts the pigment PRO-310 by roughly $219, restoring the price gap the old PRO-200's mid-2026 spike had erased. Pick this if you print glossy/luster and archival life beyond ~100 years is not a priority. [src10, src3]

### Best for Fine-Art Matte and Monochrome: Epson SureColor P700 (~$699, currently out of stock) — [Check price](https://knowledgelib.io/go/epson-surecolor-p700)
CNN Underscored's "favorite among photographers" with a 10-color UltraChrome PRO10 pigment ink set tuned for accuracy, an extra orange/violet for an expanded gamut, and Epson's reputation for matte and fine-art paper rendering. 4.3-inch tilting touchscreen makes paper-type changes simple. Steeper learning curve than the Canon Pros, but rewards investment for B&W and matte fine-art workflows. [src1, src2, src3]

### Best for 17" Gallery Prints: Epson SureColor P900 (~$1,145) — [Check price](https://knowledgelib.io/go/epson-surecolor-p900)
Same 10-color UltraChrome PRO10 pigment engine as the P700, but in a 17"-wide chassis that supports borderless prints up to 17×22 inches and panoramas up to 129 inches with the optional roll-feed adapter. Wirecutter notes the P900 enables exhibition-quality home output. The trade-off is a larger desk footprint (16" deep, 35 lbs) and reports of paper-feed issues with thick stock. [src2, src3]

### Best Value All-in-One (A3+): Epson EcoTank Photo ET-8550 (~$679) — [Check price](https://knowledgelib.io/go/epson-ecotank-et-8550)
The 13"/A3+ multifunction EcoTank with a 6-color hybrid ink set (5 dye + pigment black) tuned for photo printing alongside document, scan, and copy duties. Northlight Images' detailed long-term review confirms ink cost drops to roughly $0.04 per 4x6 photo — vs ~$0.40 with cartridges — and the included ink prints ~2,300 photos before refill. The hybrid ink set is the headline trade-off: photo quality is lower than a pure pigment Pro printer, but the cost-per-print and all-in-one convenience are unmatched at this price. [src2, src7]

## Head-to-Head Comparisons

### Canon PIXMA PRO-200S vs Canon imagePROGRAF PRO-310
The current-generation version of the classic dye-vs-pigment decision, now that both old Pro models are discontinued. The PRO-200S uses 8 dye inks (~$530); the PRO-310 uses 9 pigment inks plus a Chroma Optimizer (~$749). Print quality on glossy paper favors the dye machine — Digital Camera World found the PRO-200S's small dye molecules absorb beneath the paper's protective layer for "a completely smooth and uniform finish" without the bronzing pigment shows on gloss. The PRO-310 wins on archival life (200+ vs 100+ years), water-resistance, matte and fine-art media, and paper compatibility (canvas, thicker stock). With the PRO-200S at ~$219 less, the up-front price gap that the old PRO-200's price spike had erased is back. [src9, src10]

**Pick PRO-200S if:** you mostly print glossy or luster color photos, want the lower price and lower per-print ink cost, and 100-year album archival life is enough.
**Pick PRO-310 if:** you sell or gift fine-art prints, need pigment archival life, print on matte/canvas/thicker stock, or want the 1.8m panorama length.

### Canon imagePROGRAF PRO-310 vs Epson SureColor P700
Both are 13"/A3+ pigment printers around $700-750. The long-standing generalization still holds: Canon excels at glossy color processing and is more economical with ink; Epson excels at matte and fine-art monochrome ("Canon is better for those who want to make vivid, glossy prints, while Epson is suitable for users who focus on artistic and monochrome photography"). The P700 has a 4.3" tilting touchscreen and broader Epson/3rd-party fine-art paper support; the PRO-310 has simpler workflow, better paper handling, and now doubles the P700 on panorama length (1.8m). Practical tiebreaker as of July 2026: the PRO-310 is in stock, the P700 is temporarily out of stock. [src1, src3, src9]

**Pick PRO-310 if:** glossy color is primary, you want lower ink cost, you value simpler paper handling, or you need it in stock now.
**Pick P700 if:** matte and fine-art workflows, B&W monochrome work, and the larger touchscreen matter most.

### Epson EcoTank ET-8550 vs Canon PIXMA PRO-200S
Different products solving different problems at a now-similar price (~$679 vs ~$530). The ET-8550 is an all-in-one (print + scan + copy) with bottle-fed ink at ~$0.04 per 4x6. The PRO-200S is a print-only photo specialist with cartridge ink at ~$0.95 per 4x6. Northlight Images' summary: the ET-8550 is "a very good (but not excellent) text printer that is also a very good (but not excellent) photo printer, a better than decent scanner, and an economical machine to use." The PRO-200S produces measurably better photo prints at higher per-print cost — and now costs ~$149 less up front, so the ET-8550's case rests on per-print cost and the scanner, not the sticker price. [src7, src10]

**Pick ET-8550 if:** you want one machine for documents, scans, and photos at the lowest per-print cost.
**Pick PRO-200S if:** photo print quality is the priority and you have a separate document printer or scanner.

### Canon SELPHY CP1500 vs Fujifilm Instax Mini Link 3
Two different output models. CP1500 produces 4x6 dye-sub prints with ~100-year archival life, prints from SD/USB/Wi-Fi/AirPrint, and costs ~$0.30 per print. The Mini Link 3 produces credit-card-sized real instant film prints (chemical photographic emulsion), Bluetooth-only from a phone, and costs ~$0.85 per print. CP1500 prints look like photo-lab 4x6s; Mini Link 3 prints look like classic Polaroid-style instant film with white borders. [src4, src5, src6]

**Pick CP1500 if:** you want full 4x6 lab-quality prints for albums, scrapbooks, and gifts.
**Pick Mini Link 3 if:** you want the timeless instant-film aesthetic, smaller credit-card prints, and don't care about per-print cost.

### Polaroid Hi-Print 2x3 Gen 2 vs Fujifilm Instax Mini Link 3
Both 2-inch-class pocket printers (~$140 vs ~$107) but with different chemistries. The Polaroid Hi-Print uses dye-sublimation (the same chemistry as SELPHY/Liene), which Digital Camera World notes is "tried and trusted" and "predates inkjet printing" — better color fidelity than Zink, peel-and-stick backing for journaling. **Pick Hi-Print Gen 2 if:** you want sticker-style prints for scrapbooking and journaling with longer-lasting dye-sub color.

## Important Caveats

- Prices are approximate USD street prices as of July 2026. Photo printers, especially the EcoTank line and pro pigment printers, see frequent $50-$200 promotional swings on Amazon and B&H. Verify current price and availability before purchase.
- The Canon PIXMA PRO-200 and imagePROGRAF PRO-300 are **discontinued** and read "Currently unavailable" on Amazon as of July 2026. This card recommends their successors (PRO-200S, PRO-310) instead. Remaining PRO-200/PRO-300 stock at third-party sellers is often marked up above the newer, better successors — check the successor price first.
- Availability is unusually tight across the pro tier right now: the Epson SureColor P700, SureColor P900, and Canon SELPHY QX20 all showed "temporarily out of stock" on Amazon at last verification, and the EcoTank ET-8500 showed only 1 unit left. Listed prices for out-of-stock models are the last observed street price and may move when stock returns.
- Ink cost-per-print figures are approximations. Real cost depends on coverage (dark/saturated photos use more ink), paper type, and whether the printer enters head-cleaning cycles between prints. Low-volume users will see effective per-print cost 2-4x higher than listed.
- Archival life ratings are vendor-claimed under specific paper, ink, and display conditions (typically Wilhelm Imaging Research). Real-world life depends on UV exposure, framing glass, and humidity. Pigment ink (PRO-310, P700, P900) outlasts dye ink (PRO-200S, ET-8550) by 2-3x in the same conditions.
- Inkjet printers (PRO-200S, PRO-310, P700, P900, ET-8500, ET-8550) all carry print-head clog risk if used less than weekly. Cleaning cycles waste ink. Dye-sub (SELPHY, Liene, Polaroid Hi-Print) and instant film (Instax) printers have no clog risk.
- HP Envy Photo all-in-one printers are no longer recommended in 2026 buyer guides — the line was discontinued in late 2024 and current HP printers prioritize documents over photo quality. The Epson EcoTank line is the recommended successor for most former HP Envy Photo buyers.
