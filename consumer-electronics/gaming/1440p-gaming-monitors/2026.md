## What are the best 1440p gaming monitors in 2026?

## TL;DR

**Top pick: ASUS ROG Swift PG27AQWP-W (~$1,099)** -- 540Hz 4th-gen Tandem WOLED, 380 nits sustained, DisplayPort 2.1a UHBR20. **Best value: LG UltraGear 27GX790A (~$560)** -- 480Hz WOLED with DisplayPort 2.1, now under $600. **Best budget: Alienware AW2726DM (~$350)** -- cheapest 1440p QD-OLED ever, 240Hz, 111% DCI-P3. ## Summary

The 1440p gaming monitor market in mid-2026 is dominated by 4th-generation Tandem OLED panels and an unprecedented OLED price collapse. The best overall 1440p gaming monitor is now the ASUS ROG Swift OLED PG27AQWP-W (~$1,099), a 540Hz 4th-gen Tandem WOLED with 0.02ms response time, DisplayPort 2.1a UHBR20, DisplayHDR True Black 500, and 380 nits sustained full-screen brightness -- a 49% increase over its predecessor. For 540Hz at a far lower price, the LG UltraGear 27GX790B-B (~$700) delivers 540Hz native via 4th-gen Tandem WOLED, while the LG 27GX790A (~$560) now offers 480Hz WOLED for under $600. [src1, src4, src5]

The biggest shift since Q1 2026 is the arrival of sub-$350 OLED monitors paired with a continued mid-range price slide. The Alienware AW2726DM (~$350), launched April 2026, delivers 240Hz QD-OLED with 0.03ms response time, 111% DCI-P3, and a 3-year burn-in warranty at the lowest OLED price ever -- undercutting even budget IPS panels. The AOC Q27G4ZD (~$494) adds another budget QD-OLED option at 240Hz with HDR True Black 400 and dual DisplayPort. On the high end, the MSI MPG 271QR X50 (~$700) pushes QD-OLED to 500Hz with USB-C 98W PD and DisplayPort 2.1a, while 360Hz QD-OLED has fallen sharply: the Alienware AW2725DF is now ~$500 and the ASUS XG27ACDNG ~$534. Budget non-OLED options remain strong: the Gigabyte M27Q3 (~$270, Newegg) delivers 320Hz IPS, while the AOC Q27G40XMN (~$280) pairs Mini-LED with 1,152 dimming zones for genuine HDR at a fraction of OLED pricing. [src2, src3, src6]

## Best for Each Use Case

### Best Overall: ASUS ROG Swift OLED PG27AQWP-W (~$1,099) -- [Check price](https://knowledgelib.io/go/asus-rog-swift-pg27aqwp-w)
The PG27AQWP-W is the new flagship 1440p gaming monitor, succeeding the PG27AQDP. Its 4th-gen Tandem WOLED panel delivers 540Hz at native 1440p (720Hz at 720p) with a 0.02ms response time, 109% DCI-P3, and DisplayHDR True Black 500. Full-screen brightness reaches 380 nits sustained -- a 49% increase over its predecessor -- with 1,500 nits HDR peak. DisplayPort 2.1a UHBR20 provides 80Gbps bandwidth for uncompressed 540Hz. Three USB 3.2 downstream ports, ASUS OLED Care Pro with Neo Proximity Sensor, and a premium semi-transparent chassis complete the package. [src1, src5, src8]

### Best for Competitive FPS: LG UltraGear 27GX790B-B (~$700) -- [Check price](https://knowledgelib.io/go/lg-27gx790b)
LG's 4th-gen Primary RGB Tandem WOLED delivers 540Hz at native 1440p with a 0.02ms response time and dual-mode 720Hz at 720p. DisplayPort 2.1 UHBR20 with full 80Gbps bandwidth supports 1440p at 540Hz without compression. At 335 nits sustained SDR brightness with DisplayHDR True Black 500, it is among the brightest 1440p OLEDs available. The TrueBlack glossy coating enhances contrast and eliminates the purple-tint issue seen on older panels. Originally $1,000, now ~$700 at Amazon (a $300 drop from launch). [src1, src4]

### Best Premium Value: ASUS ROG Swift PG27AQDP (~$664) -- [Check price](https://knowledgelib.io/go/asus-rog-swift-pg27aqdp)
The PG27AQDP has slid to ~$664, making it an outstanding value 480Hz OLED. Its WOLED panel delivers perfect blacks with a 1,500,000:1 contrast ratio, 99% DCI-P3 coverage, and true 10-bit color depth. The 480Hz refresh rate combined with 0.03ms response time results in virtually no motion blur. ASUS calibrates each unit individually, and the custom heatsink design maintains performance without fan noise. At its current price, it sits just above the LG 27GX790A while offering superior calibration. [src1, src3]

### Best 4th-Gen OLED Value: ASUS ROG Strix XG27AQWMG (~$649) -- [Check price](https://knowledgelib.io/go/asus-rog-strix-xg27aqwmg)
The first affordable 4th-gen Tandem OLED, the XG27AQWMG delivers 280Hz, 0.03ms, DisplayHDR True Black 500, and ASUS TrueBlack Glossy coating at $649. Its Tandem OLED technology provides 15% higher peak brightness (up to 1,600 nits HDR highlight), 25% wider color volume, and 60% longer lifespan vs. prior WOLED. RGB stripe subpixel layout ensures sharp text for mixed gaming and productivity. G-Sync compatible with Neo Proximity Sensor for automatic standby. [src4, src8]

### Best Brightness OLED: Gigabyte MO27Q28G (~$550) -- [Check price](https://knowledgelib.io/go/gigabyte-mo27q28g)
The MO27Q28G records the highest SDR brightness of any OLED gaming monitor at 341 nits sustained, with 1,500 nits peak HDR brightness and DisplayHDR True Black 500 certification. Its 4th-gen LG Tandem WOLED panel at 280Hz with 0.03ms delivers 99.5% DCI-P3 and 84% BT.2020 coverage. USB-C with KVM, HDMI 2.1, and G-Sync compatibility round out the feature set. At $550, it undercuts the ASUS XG27AQWMG while matching its panel generation. [src3, src4]

### Best Feature-Rich QD-OLED: MSI MPG 271QR X50 (~$700) -- [Check price](https://knowledgelib.io/go/msi-mpg-271qr-x50)
MSI's flagship pushes QD-OLED to 500Hz with DisplayPort 2.1a UHBR20, USB-C 98W PD, KVM switch, and DisplayHDR True Black 500. Color coverage reaches 108% DCI-P3, and the built-in NPU powers OLED Care 3.0 for intelligent burn-in prevention. HDMI 2.1 supports 4K 120Hz for console gaming. At ~$700 (down from $899 MSRP), it offers the most connectivity of any QD-OLED gaming monitor. 3-year burn-in warranty included. [src4, src7]

### Best Mid-Range 360Hz QD-OLED: Alienware AW2725DF (~$500) -- [Check price](https://knowledgelib.io/go/alienware-aw2725d)
The world's first 360Hz QD-OLED gaming monitor delivers a 0.03ms response time, 99.3% DCI-P3 color coverage, DisplayHDR 400 True Black, and USB-C connectivity -- now down to ~$500, making 360Hz QD-OLED a near-budget proposition. Identical panel generation to the ASUS XG27ACDNG with comparable refresh rate, but Dell's 3-year burn-in warranty and Advanced Exchange Service set it apart for risk-averse buyers. AlienFX 3-zone RGB and a fully adjustable hexagonal stand round out the package. [src1, src4]

### Best Budget QD-OLED: AOC Q27G4ZD (~$494) -- [Check price](https://knowledgelib.io/go/aoc-q27g4zd)
The Q27G4ZD delivers genuine QD-OLED picture quality at ~$494 -- still among the cheapest 240Hz QD-OLEDs. Its 240Hz refresh rate with 0.03ms response time provides excellent motion clarity, and color coverage reaches 110% DCI-P3. Dual DisplayPort and dual HDMI 2.0 ports offer flexible connectivity. The main trade-offs vs. pricier OLEDs are limited to HDMI 2.0 (no 4K 120Hz console support) and slightly lower brightness. 3-year zero-bright-dot warranty included. [src3, src6]

### Cheapest OLED: Alienware AW2726DM (~$350) -- [Check price](https://knowledgelib.io/go/alienware-aw2726dm)
Launched April 2026, the AW2726DM is the cheapest 1440p OLED monitor ever made. Its 3rd-gen QD-OLED panel delivers 240Hz with 0.03ms response time and 111% DCI-P3 color coverage at a price that undercuts most IPS monitors. The updated subpixel layout improves text clarity over older QD-OLEDs. Trade-offs are significant: 200 nits full-screen brightness, no USB ports, no speakers, and HDMI limited to 120Hz. Best for dark-room gamers who want OLED blacks at the absolute lowest cost. 3-year burn-in warranty. [src6]

### Best Budget High-Refresh: Gigabyte M27Q3 (~$270) -- [Check price](https://knowledgelib.io/go/gigabyte-m27q3)
