# Best Indie Games on PC (2026)

## What are the best indie games on PC in 2026?

## TL;DR

**Top pick: Balatro (~$15) — the modern indie benchmark: a poker roguelike-deckbuilder with Metacritic 90, 100% of OpenCritic critics recommending, and 2024 GOTY-tier acclaim that still dominates "best of" lists.**
**Best recent release: Mina the Hollower (~$20) — Yacht Club's May 2026 Zelda-like is Metacritic's highest-rated game of 2026 (90 PC, 98% OpenCritic recommend) and sold 500,000+ copies in two weeks.**
**Best budget pick: Vampire Survivors (~$5) — a $4.99 "bullet heaven" roguelite with Metacritic 87 PC that delivers dozens of hours and is the cheapest great game on this list.**
Indie PC in 2026 spans cozy farming sims, deckbuilders, metroidvanias, and narrative RPGs — and small studios keep matching or beating AAA on critical reception. [<a href="https://www.pcgamesn.com/best-indie-games">src1</a>, <a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Balatro">src4</a>, <a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mina_the_Hollower">src10</a>]

## Summary

The PC indie scene in 2026 is in remarkable health, with small-team releases routinely matching or outscoring AAA titles. **Balatro** (LocalThunk / Playstack, February 2024) remains the defining example — a one-developer poker-themed roguelike-deckbuilder with a PC Metacritic of 90, a 100% critic-recommend rating on OpenCritic, multiple Game of the Year wins, and over 5 million copies sold. It still appears near the top of nearly every "best indie games" roundup. Established roguelikes like **Slay the Spire** (MegaCrit, 2020, PC Metacritic 89, OpenCritic 98%) and **Hades** (Supergiant, 2020, PC Metacritic 93) anchor the deckbuilder and action-roguelike categories, and Hades's sequel **Hades II** (Supergiant, full 1.0 September 2025) launched to roughly Metacritic 94 on PC. [src1, src4, src5]

2026's own breakout releases are already among the list's strongest picks. **Mina the Hollower** (Yacht Club Games, May 28, 2026) — a top-down, whip-and-burrow Zelda-like from the Shovel Knight team — landed at a PC Metacritic of 90 with 98% of OpenCritic critics recommending, drew 10/10s from IGN, Eurogamer, and Shacknews, sold 500,000+ copies within two weeks, and is Metacritic's highest-rated game of 2026 so far. **Mewgenics** (Edmund McMillen / Tyler Glaiel, February 10, 2026) — the 13-year passion project from the creator of The Binding of Isaac — is a turn-based tactical roguelike built on a deep cat-breeding genetics system; it scored Metacritic 88 / OpenCritic 94%, became the first 2026 game to earn OpenCritic "Must-Play" status, and sold over 1 million copies in its first week. [src10, src11, src12]

The biggest event of 2025 for indie PC was the September 4, 2025 launch of **Hollow Knight: Silksong** (Team Cherry) — the seven-years-anticipated metroidvania that arrived to a Metacritic of 90 and went on to win Game of the Year at the 2025 Steam Awards, with a free "Sea of Sorrow" expansion announced for 2026 and still unreleased as of July 2026. [src8] The metroidvania and platformer space is otherwise stacked: the original **Hollow Knight** (Team Cherry, 2017, PC Metacritic 87) is still a top-of-genre recommendation at ~$15, **Animal Well** (Billy Basso / Bigmode, May 2024, Metacritic 90) is a dense single-author puzzle-metroidvania, **Celeste** (Maddy Makes Games, 2018, Metacritic 92) is the gold-standard precision platformer with a moving mental-health narrative, and **Pizza Tower** (Tour de Pizza, 2023) is the kinetic Wario Land-style cult hit. [src1, src6, src7]

For other tastes, **Stardew Valley** (ConcernedApe, 2016, Metacritic 88) is the cozy farming-sim RPG benchmark — with up to 8-player co-op and a rare physical PC edition — while **Dave the Diver** (Mintrocket, 2023, PC Metacritic 90) blends scuba exploration with sushi-restaurant management. **Cult of the Lamb** (Massive Monster / Devolver, 2022) mixes roguelike combat with cult management, **Sea of Stars** (Sabotage Studio, 2023, Metacritic 87) is a love letter to 16-bit JRPGs with co-op, **Tunic** (Andrew Shouldice / Finji, 2022, Metacritic 88) is a Zelda-like with an in-game instruction-manual mystery, **Outer Wilds** (Mobius Digital, 2019, Metacritic 85) is the once-in-a-generation exploration mystery best played blind, and **Disco Elysium: The Final Cut** (ZA/UM, 2019, PC Metacritic 97) is the highest-rated narrative RPG on this list — a dialogue-and-skill-check detective story with no combat. [src1, src2, src3]

## Best for Each Use Case

### Best Overall: Balatro (~$15) — [Check price](https://knowledgelib.io/go/balatro-pc)
The single most-recommended modern indie game on PC. Balatro (LocalThunk, February 2024) takes the rules of poker and turns them into a roguelike-deckbuilder of "just one more run" addictiveness: each run, you build a hand, buy jokers and consumables, and chase escalating score targets. PC Metacritic 90, 100% of OpenCritic critics recommending, GDC Game of the Year, 5M+ copies. Cheap, deep, and runs on anything. [src1, src4]

### Best Recent Release (2025): Hollow Knight: Silksong (~$20) — [Check price](https://knowledgelib.io/go/hollow-knight-silksong-pc)
Team Cherry's follow-up to Hollow Knight finally launched on September 4, 2025 after seven years of anticipation — and landed at Metacritic 90 with immediate best-of-year placements. You play Hornet through a sprawling, hand-drawn kingdom with faster, more aggressive combat than the original. A free "Sea of Sorrow" expansion is slated for 2026. [src1, src6]

### Best New Release (2026): Mina the Hollower (~$20) — [Check price](https://knowledgelib.io/go/mina-the-hollower-pc)
The best-reviewed game of 2026 so far, from the Shovel Knight studio. Mina the Hollower (Yacht Club Games, May 28, 2026) is a top-down action-adventure in the Link's Awakening mould: you whip enemies, burrow underground to dodge and reposition, and pick apart a cursed island across a nonlinear world. PC Metacritic 90, 98% of OpenCritic critics recommending, 10/10s from IGN, Eurogamer, and Shacknews, and 500,000+ copies sold in two weeks. ~20-30 hours, ~$20. [src10, src12]

### Best Tactical / Roguelike RPG: Mewgenics (~$30) — [Check price](https://knowledgelib.io/go/mewgenics-pc)
The deepest time-sink on this list. Mewgenics (Edmund McMillen / Tyler Glaiel, February 10, 2026) is the 13-year passion project from The Binding of Isaac's creator: a turn-based tactical roguelike where you breed generations of cats with inherited traits and mutations, then send squads of four into grid combat across 1,000+ abilities and 900+ items. Metacritic 88, OpenCritic 94% recommend, the first 2026 game to hit OpenCritic "Must-Play," and 1M+ copies in week one. McMillen pegs a full playthrough at 150-200 hours and 100% completion at 500+. [src11, src13]

### Best Metroidvania: Hollow Knight (~$15) — [Check price](https://knowledgelib.io/go/hollow-knight-pc)
Still the genre benchmark nearly a decade on. Hollow Knight (Team Cherry, 2017, PC Metacritic 87, OpenCritic 97%) is an enormous, atmospheric, hand-drawn metroidvania with tight combat, a haunting world, and 30-60 hours of content for ~$15. If you want the densest puzzle-flavored alternative, Animal Well (Billy Basso / Bigmode, 2024, Metacritic 90) is a tighter 4-8 hour core that rewards obsessive exploration. [src1, src7]

### Best Roguelike / Deckbuilder: Slay the Spire (~$25) — [Check price](https://knowledgelib.io/go/slay-the-spire-pc)
The card that started the roguelike-deckbuilder boom. Slay the Spire (MegaCrit, 2020, PC Metacritic 89, OpenCritic 98%) is the most-imitated deckbuilder ever, with near-infinite replayability across four characters and a brutal Ascension difficulty ladder. Its sequel Slay the Spire 2 launched into Early Access on PC on March 5, 2026 (~$25; full 1.0 expected ~2027) with a new 4-player co-op mode (Steam friends only — no matchmaking, no couch co-op) and quickly became one of the fastest-earning indie games ever. For the action-roguelike flavor instead, Hades (Supergiant, 2020, PC Metacritic 93) and its sequel Hades II (2025, Metacritic ~94 PC) are the genre's best; for a turn-based tactical spin, Mewgenics (2026, Metacritic 88) is the deepest new entry. [src1, src5, src11, src14]

### Best Cozy / Relaxing: Stardew Valley (~$15) — [Check price](https://knowledgelib.io/go/stardew-valley-pc)
The cozy benchmark every farming sim is measured against. Stardew Valley (ConcernedApe, 2016, Metacritic 88, OpenCritic 99%) layers farming, fishing, mining, combat, and townsfolk relationships into one of the deepest comfort games ever, with up to 8-player co-op, free content updates, and one of the rare physical PC editions. Dave the Diver (Mintrocket, 2023, PC Metacritic 90) is the chill-but-busier alternative — scuba dives by day, sushi restaurant by night. [src1, src2]

### Best Narrative / Story-Driven: Disco Elysium: The Final Cut (~$40) — [Check price](https://knowledgelib.io/go/disco-elysium-pc)
The highest-rated game on this list at PC Metacritic 97. Disco Elysium (ZA/UM, 2019; Final Cut 2021) is a detective RPG with no combat — you solve a murder through dialogue, skill checks, and arguing with the 24 voices in your own head. Dense, funny, devastating, and unlike anything else. For a shorter cozy-but-emotional pick, Celeste (Maddy Makes Games, 2018, Metacritic 92) pairs precision platforming with a moving mental-health story (with generous assist options). [src1, src2]

### Best Puzzle / Mystery: Outer Wilds (~$25) — [Check price](https://knowledgelib.io/go/outer-wilds-pc)
A once-in-a-generation exploration mystery best gone into completely blind. Outer Wilds (Mobius Digital, 2019, Metacritic 85) drops you in a tiny hand-built solar system stuck in a 22-minute time loop; the entire game is knowledge, and finishing it changes how you think about games. Tunic (Andrew Shouldice / Finji, 2022, Metacritic 88) is the action-adventure cousin — a Zelda-like whose real puzzle is decoding its in-game instruction manual. [src1, src2]

### Best Under $10 / Cheapest: Vampire Survivors (~$5) — [Check price](https://knowledgelib.io/go/vampire-survivors-pc)
The best game on this list you can buy for the price of a coffee. Vampire Survivors (poncle, 2022, PC Metacritic 87) is a "bullet heaven" roguelite — you walk around, weapons fire automatically, and the screen fills with hundreds of enemies and absurd build synergies. Dozens of hours of unlocks, cheap DLC, and local co-op, all for $4.99. [src1, src2]

### Best Pure Platformer: Pizza Tower (~$20) — [Check price](https://knowledgelib.io/go/pizza-tower-pc)
A frantic, expressive, Wario Land-style 2D platformer that became an instant cult classic. Pizza Tower (Tour de Pizza, 2023) is all about speed, momentum, and over-the-top animation, with a score-attack structure that rewards mastery. ~8-15 hours and a soundtrack people obsess over. [src1, src2]

### Best Genre Mash-Up: Cult of the Lamb (~$25) — [Check price](https://knowledgelib.io/go/cult-of-the-lamb-pc)
Roguelike dungeon combat meets cozy cult management. Cult of the Lamb (Massive Monster / Devolver, 2022, PC Metacritic 82, OpenCritic 88%) has you clear procedural crawls by day and recruit, feed, and indoctrinate followers by night; later updates added an online versus mode and a co-op-style "Unholy Alliance" pairing. [src1, src2]

### Best 16-bit-style RPG: Sea of Stars (~$35) — [Check price](https://knowledgelib.io/go/sea-of-stars-pc)
A loving throwback to Chrono Trigger-era JRPGs. Sea of Stars (Sabotage Studio, 2023, Metacritic 87) pairs gorgeous pixel art with a timed-hit turn-based battle system, a day-night puzzle mechanic, and local couch co-op. A polished 25-40 hour adventure. [src1, src2]

## Head-to-Head Comparisons

### Balatro vs Slay the Spire
Both are top-tier roguelike-deckbuilders, but they aim at different itches. Slay the Spire (PC Metacritic 89) is the deeper, more strategic build-a-deck-from-scratch experience with four distinct characters and a long mastery ladder. Balatro (PC Metacritic 90) is faster, more chaotic, and built around poker hands and joker synergies — easier to pick up, just as hard to put down. Balatro is the better entry point; Slay the Spire is the deeper long-haul. [src1, src4]

**Pick Balatro if:** you want the most addictive, accessible "one more run" deckbuilder.
**Pick Slay the Spire if:** you want the deepest strategic deckbuilding and don't mind a steeper learning curve.

### Hollow Knight vs Hollow Knight: Silksong
Same studio, same genre — the question is just which you play first. Hollow Knight (2017, PC Metacritic 87) is cheaper (~$15), more sprawling and exploration-heavy, and the canonical starting point. Silksong (2025, Metacritic 90) is faster, more combat-forward, and slightly more demanding, with a brand-new protagonist (Hornet) and kingdom. Newcomers should start with the original; returning fans go straight to Silksong. [src1, src6]

**Pick Hollow Knight if:** it's your first Team Cherry game or you want the larger, cheaper world.
**Pick Silksong if:** you've played the original and want faster, more aggressive combat.

### Hades vs Hades II
Supergiant's two action-roguelikes are both genre-best, but II is bigger. Hades (2020, PC Metacritic 93) is the leaner, tighter, more focused original — still the recommended entry point. Hades II (full 1.0 September 2025, Metacritic ~94 PC) expands with a witchcraft system, two surface/underworld biomes' worth of content, and a new protagonist (Melinoë). Play Hades first; Hades II is the richer follow-up. [src1, src5]

**Pick Hades if:** you want the leaner, perfectly tuned original (and a great starting point).
**Pick Hades II if:** you've finished Hades and want a bigger, more sprawling sequel.

### Stardew Valley vs Dave the Diver
Both are cozy, but with different rhythms. Stardew Valley (PC Metacritic 88) is the open-ended, slow-burn life sim — farm, fish, mine, befriend the town, no clock pushing you. Dave the Diver (PC Metacritic 90) is a loop game — dive and collect fish by day, run the sushi bar by night — with more structure and a stronger story. Stardew is the better unwind-forever game; Dave is the better "I want progression and an arc." [src1, src2]

**Pick Stardew Valley if:** you want an open-ended life sim with co-op and endless mods.
**Pick Dave the Diver if:** you want a tighter, story-driven loop with day-night structure.

### Outer Wilds vs Disco Elysium
Two of the most acclaimed "thinking" indies, and a near-perfect either/or. Outer Wilds (Metacritic 85) is wordless discovery — its content is knowledge you piece together in a time-looped solar system, and it can only be experienced once. Disco Elysium (PC Metacritic 97) is the opposite: wall-to-wall writing, a detective story you talk and skill-check your way through, no combat. Both are masterpieces; neither is "casual." [src1, src2]

**Pick Outer Wilds if:** you want exploration, mystery, and "aha" moments — and can play it blind.
**Pick Disco Elysium if:** you want the best writing in games and don't need action at all.

## Important Caveats

- Prices are typical USD digital storefront (Steam / itch.io) list prices as of July 2026, not sale prices. Indie games go on deep sale extremely often — actual prices fluctuate 30-75%, and Mewgenics was 25% off ($22.49) during a July 2026 weekend deal. Vampire Survivors and Balatro in particular are frequently a few dollars.
- Metacritic / OpenCritic scores reflect critic consensus at time of writing and may shift. Some are PC-platform aggregates and differ slightly from console aggregates; Hades II's score is for the post-Early-Access 1.0 release.
- "Indie" is loosely defined here. Several picks are publisher-backed (Devolver, Bigmode, Playstack, Finji, Annapurna-adjacent) but are small-team productions widely treated as indie. Purists may exclude some entries.
- Genre taste dominates. There is no single "best indie game" for everyone — use the Best-for and Decision Logic sections. A deckbuilder fan and a cozy-sim fan should not buy the same game.
- Some titles need a controller for the best experience (Celeste, Hollow Knight, Hades, Tunic), and 3D entries (Outer Wilds, Hades II, Tunic) have meaningful PC minimum specs — check the Steam page before buying.
