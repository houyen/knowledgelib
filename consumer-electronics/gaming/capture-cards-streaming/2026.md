## Summary

The capture card market in late June 2026 keeps trending cheaper on the 4K60 and HDMI 2.1 tiers: the Elgato 4K S has dropped to ~$136 on Amazon (from ~$160), making it the single cheapest 4K60-capable card on the market — now $4 under the AVerMedia Live Gamer Ultra S (GC553Pro, ~$140), still PC Gamer's top pick. The Elgato 4K X — the only HDMI 2.1 USB card in stock — has fallen to ~$200 (from ~$248), putting 4K144 VRR passthrough within reach of mid-range buyers. The AVerMedia Live Gamer Extreme 3 (GC551G2) holds at ~$110, the budget 4K30/4K60-HDR-passthrough champion. The Elgato HD60 X has climbed back to ~$149 (from ~$120) and now sits between the 4K-capable 4K S and Ultra S, weakening its value case. [src1, src2, src5, src6, src9]

For HDMI 2.1 features (4K120+ passthrough, 4K VRR), the Elgato 4K X (~$200) is the only externally-available USB option this cycle — the AVerMedia Live Gamer Ultra 2.1 (~$280 list) remains unavailable at Amazon US. The market now splits into five clear tiers: budget 4K passthrough at ~$110 (Live Gamer Extreme 3, undercuts most 1080p cards), mid-range 4K60 USB at $136-149 (Elgato 4K S, Live Gamer Ultra S, Elgato HD60 X), premium HDMI 2.1 external at ~$200 (Elgato 4K X), and high-end PCIe cards for pro setups (Elgato 4K Pro ~$250, AVerMedia Live Gamer 4K 2.1 ~$210, dual-source Live Gamer Duo ~$230). Several budget cards (NZXT Signal HD60, Genki ShadowCast 2, Elgato Game Capture Neo) remain temporarily unavailable on Amazon. [src1, src2, src4, src5, src9]

## Best for Each Use Case

### Best Overall (HDMI 2.1): Elgato 4K X (~$200) — [Check price](https://knowledgelib.io/go/elgato-4k-x)
With the AVerMedia Live Gamer Ultra 2.1 currently unavailable at Amazon, the Elgato 4K X (~$200) is the top external pick for streamers who need HDMI 2.1 features. HDMI 2.1 interface with full 4K144 VRR passthrough means you never sacrifice gaming experience. 4K144 capture or 1080p240, HDR10, USB 3.2 Gen 2 for ultra-low latency, and Stream Deck integration. The 4K X has just dropped to ~$200 (from ~$248) and is the only HDMI 2.1 external USB card consistently in stock — making future-proof 4K120+ capture more affordable than ever. [src1, src2, src4]

### Best Value 4K: AVerMedia Live Gamer Ultra S (~$140) — [Check price](https://knowledgelib.io/go/avermedia-live-gamer-ultra-s)
PC Gamer's top pick. The GC553Pro delivers 4K60 capture with VRR passthrough, 240fps recording at 1080p, 5.1 surround sound, and ultra-wide resolution support (3440x1440). Supports RGB24 color format for true-to-life colors. The trade-off vs the Ultra 2.1 is HDMI 2.0 instead of 2.1, so passthrough maxes at 4K60 rather than 4K144. The Ultra S holds at ~$140 at Amazon — now just $4 more than the Elgato 4K S ($136), the two cheapest 4K60 cards on the market and effectively interchangeable on price. Pick the Ultra S for 5.1 surround sound and 240fps recording. [src1, src5]

### Cheapest 4K / Best for Mac/iPad: Elgato 4K S (~$136) — [Check price](https://knowledgelib.io/go/elgato-4k-s)
The Elgato 4K S matches the AVerMedia Ultra S on specs -- 4K60 capture, 1440p120/1080p240, HDR10, VRR passthrough -- and at ~$136 (down from ~$160) it is now the single cheapest 4K60 capture card on the market, edging out even the Ultra S ($140). Its standout advantage is full Mac and iPad compatibility with UVC plug-and-play, making it the only 4K60 capture card purpose-built for the Apple ecosystem. Same compact form factor as the 4K X (112 x 72 x 18mm). PC Gamer gave it a near-perfect review. [src1, src6]

### Best Proven 1080p: Elgato HD60 X (~$149) — [Check price](https://knowledgelib.io/go/elgato-hd60-x)
The HD60 X remains the go-to for streamers who primarily broadcast at 1080p60 and value bulletproof reliability over raw specs. Rock-solid 1080p60 HDR10 capture with 4K30 recording, VRR passthrough up to 4K60, and a proven track record. Amazon pricing has climbed back to ~$149 (from a ~$120 low), which now puts it above the 4K-capable Elgato 4K S ($136) and Ultra S ($140) — for less money either of those offers full 4K60 capture, making the HD60 X harder to recommend unless you need its USB 3.0 compatibility with older laptops or specifically prefer the Elgato ecosystem. [src2, src3]

### Best Premium External: Elgato 4K X (~$200) — [Check price](https://knowledgelib.io/go/elgato-4k-x)
The premium external choice for creators who need to capture at 4K144 or 1080p240 for high-quality YouTube content. HDMI 2.1 interface with full 4K144 VRR passthrough. Stream Deck integration makes it ideal for the Elgato ecosystem. At ~$200 (down from ~$248), it is the only HDMI 2.1 external card consistently in stock at Amazon US — the AVerMedia Live Gamer Ultra 2.1 is currently unavailable. PCGamesN rates it the best 4K capture card available. [src1, src4]

### Best Budget 4K30: AVerMedia Live Gamer Extreme 3 (~$110) — [Check price](https://knowledgelib.io/go/avermedia-live-gamer-extreme-3)

It captures at 4K30 (or QHD120) with VRR and passes through 4K60 HDR — ideal for PS5/Xbox Series X gamers who want to play at 4K60 HDR while recording at YouTube-friendly 4K30. ## Head-to-Head Comparisons

### AVerMedia Live Gamer Ultra S vs Elgato 4K S
At current Amazon pricing (~$140 vs ~$136), the Elgato 4K S has slipped just under the AVerMedia Ultra S — they are now effectively the same price and the two cheapest 4K60 cards on the market. The Ultra S adds 5.1 surround sound and 240fps recording at 1080p; the Elgato 4K S is the only 4K60 card with first-class Mac and iPad UVC support, making it the safer pick for Apple-centric creators. Both offer 4K60 HDR capture with VRR passthrough and ultra-low latency. [src1, src5, src6]

**Pick AVerMedia Live Gamer Ultra S if:** you are on Windows and want 5.1 audio and 240fps recording at 1080p.
**Pick Elgato 4K S if:** you record on a Mac or iPad, want the cheapest 4K60 card, or are already invested in the Elgato ecosystem (Stream Deck, Wave XLR, 4K Capture Utility).

### Elgato 4K X vs AVerMedia Live Gamer Ultra 2.1
With the Ultra 2.1 currently unavailable at Amazon US, the Elgato 4K X (~$200) is the only externally-stocked HDMI 2.1 USB card — and at its new ~$200 price it is now ~$80 cheaper than the Ultra 2.1's $280 list while matching it on 4K144 VRR passthrough. The Ultra 2.1's edges are 5.1 surround sound and ultra-wide resolution support, neither of which most streamers need. The 4K X also has tighter Stream Deck and Elgato 4K Capture Utility integration. [src1, src4, src7]

**Pick Elgato 4K X if:** you need HDMI 2.1, want a card actually in stock today at the lower price, or want Stream Deck integration.
**Pick AVerMedia Live Gamer Ultra 2.1 if:** you must have 5.1 surround sound for the capture pipeline, and you can wait for stock to return.

### AVerMedia Live Gamer Extreme 3 vs Elgato HD60 X
At ~$110 vs ~$149 Amazon pricing, the Extreme 3 is now ~$39 cheaper AND captures at 4K30 with 4K60 HDR passthrough, while the HD60 X caps at 1080p60 capture and 4K60 passthrough. For PS5/Xbox Series X owners who want to play at 4K60 HDR, the Extreme 3 is the obvious upgrade and the better value. The HD60 X retains its USB 3.0 compatibility advantage with older laptops where USB 3.2 Gen 2 is not available. [src2, src9]

**Pick AVerMedia Live Gamer Extreme 3 if:** you want 4K60 HDR passthrough and 4K30 recording on a PS5 or Xbox Series X, your PC has USB 3.2 Gen 2, and you want to save ~$39.
**Pick Elgato HD60 X if:** your laptop only has USB 3.0, you broadcast at 1080p60 and never need 4K, or you want Elgato ecosystem compatibility.
