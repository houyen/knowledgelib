# Best Earbuds (2026)

## What are the best earbuds in 2026?

## TL;DR

**Top pick: Sony WF-1000XM6 (~$298) — class-leading ANC (88% noise reduction) + studio-tuned sound, the consensus best overall.**
**Best value: Soundcore Liberty 4 Pro (~$110) — flagship-adjacent sound, LDAC, and a 4.8 MDAQS score for about a third of the flagship price.**
**Best budget: CMF Buds 2 Plus (~$59) — a 4.8 MDAQS sound score matching the Sony flagship for a fraction of the cost.**
On iPhone, the AirPods Pro 3 (~$219) is the best pick; for pure silence, the Bose QC Ultra Earbuds Gen 2 (~$299) win.
[<a href="https://www.soundguys.com/best-wireless-earbuds-2-14313/">src2</a>, <a href="https://www.rtings.com/headphones/reviews/best/wireless-earbuds">src1</a>, <a href="https://www.tomsguide.com/us/best-wireless-earbuds,review-6160.html">src3</a>]

## Summary

The 2026 true-wireless market is defined by two facts: the flagships are better than ever, and the budget tier has nearly caught up on sound. The **Sony WF-1000XM6** (~$298, the 2026 flagship) is the consensus best overall — SoundGuys measured ~88% average noise reduction and a 4.8/5 MDAQS sound score, and RTINGS named it the top pick overall and for Android because its feature set isn't locked to one phone brand. [src1, src2, src5] For pure noise cancellation, the **Bose QuietComfort Ultra Earbuds (2nd Gen)** (~$299) remain the silence king, with the best ANC Bose has shipped plus a new Cinema Mode and wireless charging. [src6] On iPhone, the **Apple AirPods Pro 3** (~$219, still below its $249 launch) is the no-brainer — SoundGuys logged ~90% noise reduction (the highest measured) plus clinical-grade hearing-aid, heart-rate, and live-translation features for the Apple ecosystem. [src2, src7]

For Android purists who don't want Sony or Apple, the **Technics EAH-AZ100** (~$235) delivers reference-class hi-fi sound, LDAC/LC3, Dolby Atmos head tracking, and a measured 10h 40m battery. [src2] Samsung phone owners get the most from the **Galaxy Buds3 Pro** (~$198), and Pixel owners from the **Google Pixel Buds Pro 2** (~$211). The biggest story of 2026, though, is value: the **Soundcore Liberty 4 Pro** (~$110) and the **CMF Buds 2 Plus** (~$59) both hit a 4.8 MDAQS sound score — the same number as the $298 Sony flagship — making "you don't need to spend $300" the truest it has ever been. [src2] The **Sony WF-C710N** (~$100) is the safe sub-$100 default. [src1, src2]

## Best for Each Use Case

### Best Overall: Sony WF-1000XM6 (~$298) — [Check price](https://knowledgelib.io/go/sony-wf-1000xm6)
The 2026 Sony flagship is the consensus best overall across RTINGS, SoundGuys, and What Hi-Fi. SoundGuys measured ~88% average noise reduction and a 4.8/5 MDAQS sound score; the HD Noise Cancelling Processor QN3e is 3× faster than the previous generation, and AI beamforming "trained on 500 million voice samples" gives it the best calls in the category. It's the safest no-regrets pick for anyone who isn't locked into Apple. [src1, src2, src4]

### Best for iPhone: Apple AirPods Pro 3 (~$219) — [Check price](https://knowledgelib.io/go/airpods-pro-3)
SoundGuys logged ~90% average noise reduction — the highest they've measured — and Tom's Guide called the AirPods Pro 3 the pair that "arrived to conquer all." On iPhone you also get clinical-grade hearing-aid mode, heart-rate sensing, and live translation, plus instant pairing and Find My. The catch: no LDAC, so Android users lose hi-res codec support. Now ~$219, still below its $249 launch. [src2, src3, src7]

### Best ANC: Bose QuietComfort Ultra Earbuds (2nd Gen) (~$299) — [Check price](https://knowledgelib.io/go/bose-quietcomfort-ultra-earbuds-2nd-gen)
If absolute silence is the priority, the Bose Gen 2 are the pick — multiple reviewers lean Bose for "pure noise cancelling." CustomTune adapts ANC to your ear shape, and the Gen 2 adds AI call filtering, a Cinema Mode for spatializing video, and wireless charging. Tradeoffs: only ~6h battery and no LDAC. [src6]

### Best for Android (audiophile): Technics EAH-AZ100 (~$235) — [Check price](https://knowledgelib.io/go/technics-eah-az100)
RTINGS' pick for Android users who want premium ANC without defaulting to Sony or Apple. Magnetic-fluid drivers deliver reference-class hi-fi sound, with LDAC and LC3, Dolby Atmos head tracking, 3-device multipoint, and a measured 10h 40m of battery. Now ~$235 (down from $299.99). [src2, src5]

### Best for Samsung Phones: Samsung Galaxy Buds3 Pro (~$198) — [Check price](https://knowledgelib.io/go/samsung-galaxy-buds3-pro)
On a recent Galaxy phone, setup is seamless, device switching is smooth, and you unlock UHQ (24-bit) audio, 360 Audio with head tracking, and ultra-wideband mics. Outside the Samsung bubble you lose the higher-end codec perks, so this is a buy-it-for-the-ecosystem pick. [src7]

### Best Sound (premium): Sennheiser Momentum True Wireless 4 (~$180) — [Check price](https://knowledgelib.io/go/sennheiser-momentum-true-wireless-4)
Sennheiser's signature warm, detailed tuning with aptX Lossless, LE Audio/LC3, and Auracast support, a 30-hour total battery, and adaptive ANC. The audiophile pick for listeners who care about timbre more than the last 5% of ANC. [src2, src4]

### Best for Pixel / Google: Google Pixel Buds Pro 2 (~$211) — [Check price](https://knowledgelib.io/go/google-pixel-buds-pro-2)
Tightest integration with Pixel phones and the Gemini assistant, Silent Seal 2.0 ANC powered by Google's Tensor A1 chip, and a strong 8h-per-bud / 30h-total battery. Now ~$211 (still below its $229 list). [src2]

### Best Value: Soundcore Liberty 4 Pro (~$110) — [Check price](https://knowledgelib.io/go/soundcore-liberty-4-pro)
SoundGuys calls it "flagship-adjacent performance" — a 4.8/5 MDAQS sound score (same as the $298 Sony), LDAC, an 8-band EQ, 7-sensor real-time adaptive ANC, AI 6-mic calls, and a touch-display charging case. The clearest "you don't need to spend $300" argument in the lineup. [src2]

### Best Under $100: Sony WF-C710N (~$100) — [Check price](https://knowledgelib.io/go/sony-wf-c710n)
The safe sub-$100 default: ~85% noise reduction, Sony's DSEE upscaling, a refined balanced tuning, and 30h total battery. Now ~$100 (down from a $129.99 list), and frequently under $100 on sale. The best pick when you want a recognizable brand without paying flagship money. [src1, src2]

### Best Budget: CMF Buds 2 Plus (~$59) — [Check price](https://knowledgelib.io/go/cmf-buds-2-plus)
The shock of 2026: SoundGuys gave it a 4.8/5 MDAQS sound score — matching the Sony WF-1000XM6 — plus ~83% noise reduction, LDAC, 12mm drivers, and a category-record 61.5h total battery, all for ~$59. Best for Android (LDAC); iPhone users lose some quality on AAC. [src1, src2]

### Best Ultra-Budget: Soundcore Space A40 (~$49) — [Check price](https://knowledgelib.io/go/soundcore-space-a40)
A perennial budget favorite: adaptive ANC, LDAC, multipoint, and a 50h total battery. Older than the CMF, but still absurd value now that it regularly sits under $50. [src1, src2]

## Head-to-Head Comparisons

### Sony WF-1000XM6 vs Apple AirPods Pro 3
The XM6 wins on raw versatility — LDAC hi-res, app-level EQ, and the best calls — and isn't locked to one phone brand, which is why RTINGS rates it best overall and best for Android. The AirPods Pro 3 win on iPhone integration and measured the highest noise reduction (~90%), but lose codec support on Android. [src1, src2, src3]

**Pick Sony WF-1000XM6 if:** you're on Android, want hi-res LDAC, or want the most flexible flagship.
**Pick AirPods Pro 3 if:** you're on iPhone and want hearing-aid/heart-rate/translation features.

### Bose QC Ultra Earbuds Gen 2 vs Sony WF-1000XM6
The Bose edge out the Sony for absolute pure noise cancellation and add Immersive/Cinema audio, but cost more (~$299 vs ~$298 after Sony's discount), have shorter battery (~6h vs 8h), and lack LDAC. The Sony is the more complete all-rounder. [src1, src2, src6]

**Pick Bose QC Ultra Earbuds Gen 2 if:** silence is your #1 priority above everything else.
**Pick Sony WF-1000XM6 if:** you want the best balance of ANC, sound, battery, and codecs.

### Technics EAH-AZ100 vs Sony WF-1000XM6
Both are top-tier for Android. The Technics wins on outright sound quality (reference-class hi-fi tuning), battery (10h vs 8h per bud), and price (~$235 vs ~$298), while the Sony wins on ANC strength and call quality. [src2, src5]

**Pick Technics EAH-AZ100 if:** sound quality and battery matter most and you want to save ~$60.
**Pick Sony WF-1000XM6 if:** you want the strongest ANC and best calls in a flagship.

### Soundcore Liberty 4 Pro vs CMF Buds 2 Plus
Both score 4.8 MDAQS on sound. The Liberty 4 Pro (~$110) adds stronger 7-sensor adaptive ANC, a touch-display case, and better calls; the CMF (~$59) wins decisively on price and battery (61.5h). [src2]

**Pick Soundcore Liberty 4 Pro if:** you want flagship-level features for about a third of the flagship price.
**Pick CMF Buds 2 Plus if:** you want the most sound-per-dollar and the longest battery, full stop.

### CMF Buds 2 Plus vs Sony WF-C710N
The CMF wins on sound score (4.8 vs solid), LDAC hi-res, and battery (61.5h vs 30h) at a lower price (~$59 vs ~$100). The Sony WF-C710N wins on brand trust, fit, and being codec-agnostic (no LDAC dependency), which matters on iPhone. [src1, src2]

**Pick CMF Buds 2 Plus if:** you're on Android and want maximum value.
**Pick Sony WF-C710N if:** you're on iPhone or want a trusted brand under $100.

## Important Caveats

- Prices are approximate US Amazon street prices as of July 2026 and move 15-40% on promotions — Soundcore, CMF, and Sony in particular fluctuate week to week.
- Codec advantages (LDAC, LC3, aptX, Auracast) require an Android source device. On iPhone, every model here falls back to AAC, which neutralizes the hi-res edge of Technics, Sony, Sennheiser, and CMF.
- Ecosystem features (AirPods hearing aid / heart rate / translation; Galaxy UHQ / 360 Audio; Pixel Gemini) need the matching phone to fully work — outside that, these are "just earbuds."
- ANC and sound figures cited are lab measurements (RTINGS, SoundGuys MDAQS) that assume an optimal ear-tip seal; a poor fit can cut effective isolation by 10-20 dB on any model.
- Manufacturer ANC percentages (e.g., Soundcore's "98%") are marketing figures from controlled tests and are not directly comparable across brands. SoundGuys' independent lab figures are more reliable for cross-brand comparison.
