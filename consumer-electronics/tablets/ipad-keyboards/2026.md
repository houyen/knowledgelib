# Best iPad Keyboards (2026)

## What are the best iPad keyboards in 2026?

## TL;DR

**Top pick: Apple Magic Keyboard for iPad Pro M4/M5 (~$289/$334)** — aluminum palm rest, haptic trackpad, 14-key function row, Smart Connector (no charging). **Best value: Logitech Combo Touch (~$170-$200)** — larger trackpad, detachable keyboard, full edge protection at roughly 65% of Apple's price. **Best budget with trackpad: Typecase Edge (~$130)** — Wirecutter's pick: floating Magic-style design, 11-color backlit keys, trackpad, Bluetooth. [src1, src2, src4]

## Summary

The iPad keyboard market in 2026 is dominated by two tiers: Apple's premium Magic Keyboard lineup ($199-$334) and Logitech's value-packed alternatives ($69-$200). The Apple Magic Keyboard for iPad Pro M4 ($289/$334) remains the gold standard with its aluminum palm rest, haptic trackpad, function row, and floating cantilever design — and the same hardware carried over unchanged for the iPad Pro M5 (October 2025), so no new keyboard purchase is needed for M4→M5 upgraders. The Logitech Combo Touch ($170-$200) delivers 90% of the experience at roughly 65% of the cost, with a larger trackpad, detachable keyboard, adjustable kickstand, and full edge protection. [src1, src2, src3, src8, src9]

For budget-conscious buyers, the ESR Rebound 360 (~$105) and Typecase Edge (~$130) offer Magic Keyboard-style floating designs with trackpads and backlighting at a fraction of Apple's cost. Wirecutter names the Typecase Edge as their top pick for iPad Pro keyboard cases, noting solid typing and trackpad performance at well under half Apple's price. For the standard iPad (10th gen / A16), the Logitech Slim Folio ($69-$90) and Apple Magic Keyboard Folio ($199) are the main contenders. [src4, src5]

The key decision in 2026 is whether you need a trackpad: Smart Connector keyboards from Apple and Logitech require no charging and pair instantly, while Bluetooth alternatives offer more flexibility but need periodic recharging. Every iPad Pro and iPad Air model now supports trackpad cursors, making trackpad-equipped keyboards increasingly essential for productivity workflows. [src2, src7]

## Best for Each Use Case

### Best Overall (iPad Pro): Apple Magic Keyboard (~$289-$334) — [Check price](https://knowledgelib.io/go/apple-magic-keyboard-ipad-pro-11-m4)
The 2024 redesign for iPad Pro M4 brought a 14-key function row, aluminum palm rest, larger haptic trackpad, and USB-C pass-through charging. The floating cantilever design allows smooth angle adjustment, and Smart Connector means zero charging or pairing required. At half a pound lighter than the previous generation, it turns the iPad Pro into a genuine laptop replacement. The haptic trackpad is a significant upgrade — silent, precise, and supporting full Multi-Touch gestures. [src1, src2, src6]

### Best Overall (iPad Air): Apple Magic Keyboard for iPad Air (~$242-$299) — [Check price](https://knowledgelib.io/go/apple-magic-keyboard-ipad-air-11)
Apple's keyboard for iPad Air shares the floating cantilever design but lacks the function row and aluminum palm rest of the Pro version. Still delivers excellent typing feel, reliable trackpad, and instant Smart Connector pairing. Compatible across iPad Air M2, M3, and M4 generations. [src2, src3]

### Best Value with Trackpad: Logitech Combo Touch (~$170-$200) — [Check price](https://knowledgelib.io/go/logitech-combo-touch-ipad-pro-11-m4)
The consensus value pick across RTINGS, Tom's Guide, Macworld, and Cult of Mac — and now frequently discounted to ~$170 (Air) / ~$200 (Pro). The 2024 redesign features a trackpad that is 67% larger than the previous model and nearly 50% larger than Apple's Magic Keyboard trackpad on the 13-inch version. Detachable keyboard converts from laptop to tablet mode instantly. Adjustable kickstand offers far more viewing angles than Apple's fixed cantilever. Smart Connector powered — no charging needed. Full edge protection included. The mechanical trackpad click is louder than Apple's haptic version, but functional. [src1, src2, src3, src7]

### Best Budget with Trackpad: Typecase Edge (~$130) — [Check price](https://knowledgelib.io/go/typecase-edge-ipad-pro-11-m4)
Wirecutter's top pick for iPad Pro keyboard cases. Offers a floating Magic Keyboard-style design with 11-color backlit keys, multi-touch trackpad, pencil holder, and magnetic stand — all at well under half of Apple's price. Connects via Bluetooth (requires periodic charging). Build quality is noticeably below Apple and Logitech, but for occasional typing sessions it is a strong value. Available for iPad Pro M4/M5 and iPad Air M2/M3/M4. (Note: the iPad Pro 11-inch listing has risen to ~$130 from earlier ~$80 pricing — the ESR Rebound 360 at ~$105 is now the cheaper trackpad-equipped option.) [src4, src5]

### Best for Standard iPad: Apple Magic Keyboard Folio (~$199) — [Check price](https://knowledgelib.io/go/apple-magic-keyboard-folio-ipad-10)
The only Apple keyboard option for the iPad 10th gen and A16, now commonly $199 (down from its $249 list price). Two-piece detachable design with Smart Connector, built-in click trackpad, 14-key function row, and 1mm scissor key travel. The magnetic attachment is secure and the back panel protects the rear of the iPad. Not backlit, which is a notable omission. [src2, src3]

### Best Budget for Standard iPad: Logitech Slim Folio (~$69-$90) — [Check price](https://knowledgelib.io/go/logitech-slim-folio-ipad-10)
Wirecutter's recommendation for the 10th-gen iPad, frequently discounted to ~$69. Bluetooth keyboard with three modes (typing, viewing, reading), comfortable laptop-style keys, and 3-year battery life on coin cell batteries. No trackpad and no backlighting, but it costs a fraction of the Magic Keyboard Folio. Solid typing experience for notes, email, and documents. [src4, src5]

### Best Portable Standalone: Logitech Keys-To-Go 2 (~$80) — [Check price](https://knowledgelib.io/go/logitech-keys-to-go-2)
Ultra-slim standalone Bluetooth keyboard with built-in cover. Weighs just 222g and is 4.5mm thin. Backlit keys, 3-year battery life, and multi-device switching (pairs with up to 3 devices). Works with any iPad, iPhone, Mac, or Apple TV. Ideal for users who want a keyboard without adding bulk to their iPad case. No trackpad. [src3, src5]

## Head-to-Head Comparisons

### Apple Magic Keyboard vs Logitech Combo Touch (iPad Pro M4/M5)
The premium Magic Keyboard wins on typing feel (1mm aluminum-backed scissor keys), trackpad precision (haptic, glass), and build quality. Combo Touch wins on price (~$90 cheaper — ~$200 vs ~$289), trackpad size (up to 50% larger on 13-inch), edge protection, and viewing-angle flexibility (kickstand vs fixed cantilever). [src1, src7]

**Pick Magic Keyboard if:** You want the most refined typing/trackpad experience and don't need case-style edge protection.
**Pick Combo Touch if:** You want maximum protection, a detachable keyboard for tablet mode, or simply better value.

### Apple Magic Keyboard for iPad Pro M4 vs M5 (same hardware)
Apple did not release a new Magic Keyboard for the M5. The M5 iPad Pro uses the exact same Magic Keyboard introduced with the M4 in May 2024 — Smart Connector pinout, dimensions, and floating cantilever are unchanged. A January 2026 firmware update added minor stability fixes for both M4 and M5 owners. [src8, src9]

**Pick the M4-era Magic Keyboard:** Whether you own an M4 or M5 iPad Pro, this is the only Apple keyboard option — there is no M5-specific model.
**Skip a new keyboard purchase if upgrading M4 → M5:** Your existing Magic Keyboard works without modification.

### Typecase Edge vs ESR Rebound 360 (budget trackpad cases)
ESR Rebound 360 (~$105) is now the cheaper of the two and wins on case-style protection (removable hard shell) and cross-model compatibility (fits both iPad Pro M4/M5 and iPad Air M2/M3/M4). Typecase Edge (~$130) wins on backlight customization (11 colors) and its floating Magic-style design, and carries Wirecutter's recommendation. [src4, src5]

**Pick Typecase Edge if:** You want the floating Magic-style design with custom backlighting and the editorial pick, and don't need full-case armor.
**Pick ESR Rebound 360 if:** You want the lowest price plus protection close to the Combo Touch, or you swap between iPad Pro and iPad Air.

### Apple Magic Keyboard Folio vs Logitech Slim Folio (standard iPad 10th gen / A16)
Magic Keyboard Folio wins on trackpad (built-in click trackpad), function row (14 keys), and Smart Connector simplicity. Slim Folio wins on price (~$69 vs ~$199), battery life (3-year coin-cell), and standalone-Bluetooth use across other devices. Neither is backlit. [src2, src4]

**Pick Magic Keyboard Folio if:** You need a trackpad on the standard iPad and the ~$199 price is acceptable.
**Pick Slim Folio if:** You want a sub-$100 keyboard for notes/email and don't need a trackpad.

### Logitech Combo Touch vs Logitech Keys-To-Go 2 (Logitech-only matchup)
Combo Touch is a full case-keyboard combo with trackpad, designed to live on your iPad. Keys-To-Go 2 is a 222g standalone keyboard that pairs with any iPad/iPhone/Mac via Bluetooth. [src3, src5]

**Pick Combo Touch if:** You want a daily laptop-replacement setup with trackpad and full protection.
**Pick Keys-To-Go 2 if:** You want a travel keyboard that pairs across multiple devices and adds zero bulk to your iPad's everyday case.

## Decision Logic

### If user has iPad Pro 11-inch or 13-inch M4/M5
--> Apple Magic Keyboard ($289/$334) for the best typing + trackpad experience, or Logitech Combo Touch (~$200) for better value, protection, and a larger trackpad. [src1, src7]

--> Logitech Combo Touch ($170/$200) offers the best balance of features, protection, and price. Apple Magic Keyboard for iPad Air ($242/$299) if they prefer Apple's ecosystem integration and haptic trackpad. [src2, src3]

### If budget < $110
--> ESR Rebound 360 (~$105) for iPad Pro/Air with a trackpad and removable protective case. Logitech Slim Folio (~$69-$90) for standard iPad without trackpad. Both are solid for occasional use. [src4, src5]

### If trackpad is essential
--> Eliminate Logitech Slim Folio, Keys-To-Go 2, and Flip Folio from consideration. Apple Magic Keyboard, Logitech Combo Touch, Typecase Edge, and ESR Rebound 360 all include trackpads. [src1, src2]

### If user wants maximum protection
--> Logitech Combo Touch is the only premium option with full edge protection. Apple Magic Keyboard leaves the top and sides of the iPad exposed. For budget protection, the ESR Rebound 360 includes a removable protective case. [src5, src7]

### If user primarily sketches or draws (tablet mode matters)
--> Logitech Combo Touch with its detachable keyboard, or Apple Smart Folio (case-only, no keyboard) for the lightest setup. The Magic Keyboard's cantilever design does not support flat tablet use. [src2, src3]

### Default recommendation
--> Logitech Combo Touch for the user's specific iPad model. It offers the best combination of typing quality, trackpad size, protection, versatility, and value across all iPad Pro and iPad Air models. [src1, src2, src7]

## Key Market Trends (2026)

- **Haptic trackpads replacing mechanical**: Apple's 2024 Magic Keyboard introduced a haptic trackpad with no physical click — silent and more precise. Logitech still uses mechanical trackpads but is expected to follow. [src6, src7]
- **Function rows becoming standard**: The 2024 Magic Keyboard for iPad Pro added a 14-key function row. Logitech Combo Touch includes iPadOS shortcut keys. Budget keyboards are adding function rows too. [src2, src6]
- **Smart Connector dominance**: Smart Connector keyboards (zero pairing, zero charging) are increasingly preferred over Bluetooth alternatives. Both Apple and Logitech use Smart Connector for their flagship iPad keyboards. [src1, src3]
- **Budget alternatives closing the gap**: Typecase, ESR, and other third-party brands now offer Magic Keyboard-style floating designs with trackpads at 35-45% of Apple's price (ESR Rebound 360 ~$105, Typecase Edge ~$130). Build quality varies, but functionality is surprisingly competitive. [src4, src5]
- **iPad Pro M4 keyboard redesign incompatibility**: The 2024 Magic Keyboard redesign broke backward compatibility — keyboards made for pre-M4 iPad Pro models do not fit the M4/M5. This forces upgrades and fragments the used market. [src6]
- **iPad Pro M5 reuses M4 keyboard hardware**: Apple did not release a new Magic Keyboard for the M5 (October 2025 launch). The same Magic Keyboard introduced with the M4 carries over unchanged. A January 2026 firmware update added minor stability fixes for M4/M5 users. [src8, src9]
- **AI and iPad productivity convergence**: With Apple Intelligence and iPadOS 26 windowing improvements, keyboard + trackpad setups are more relevant than ever for iPad productivity workflows. [src2, src3, src9]

## Important Caveats

- Prices are approximate US street prices as of June 2026. Apple keyboard pricing is fairly stable (Magic Keyboard Folio and the Air keyboard now sit below list), and third-party brands frequently run Amazon sales of 20-40% — so budget picks like the Typecase Edge can swing $50+ either way.
- iPad keyboard compatibility is model-specific. An 11-inch keyboard will not fit a 13-inch iPad, and Pro keyboards will not fit Air or standard iPad models (and vice versa). Always verify your exact iPad model before purchasing.
- Smart Connector keyboards (Apple, Logitech) draw power from the iPad — this slightly reduces iPad battery life. Bluetooth keyboards have their own batteries but require periodic charging or battery replacement.
- Typing feel is subjective. Apple's 1mm scissor keys feel shallower than Logitech's 1.4mm keys. Users coming from laptop keyboards may prefer the deeper Logitech travel.
- Trackpad size and precision vary significantly. Apple's haptic trackpad is the most precise, but Logitech's mechanical trackpad is physically larger on 13-inch models.
