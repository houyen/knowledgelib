# Best Monitor Arms and Mounts (2026)

## What are the best monitor arms and mounts in 2026?

## TL;DR

**Top pick: Ergotron LX Pro (~$199)** — tool-free install, 22 lb capacity, 10-year warranty. **Best value: MSI MAG MT201 (~$85)** — 44 lb capacity at a quarter of premium-arm pricing. **Best budget: NB North Bayou F80 (~$20)** — real gas-spring arm for 17-30" monitors. [src1, src2, src4]

## Summary

The monitor arm market in 2026 is dominated by Ergotron, which holds three of the top five positions across major review sites. The Ergotron LX Pro (~$199) is the consensus best overall pick, offering tool-free installation, smooth Constant Force Technology, and a 10-year warranty. For heavy ultrawide monitors up to 49 inches (42 lbs), the Ergotron HX (~$329) remains unmatched. Budget shoppers can get a true gas-spring arm for under $25 with the NB North Bayou F80 (~$20), step up to the MSI MAG MT201 (~$85) for a 44 lb capacity, or pick the VIVO Premium Aluminum (~$70) for refined build at a lower price. [src1, src2, src4]

The Fully Jarvis (~$195) matches the Ergotron LX Pro in smoothness and range of motion while offering an industry-leading 15-year warranty, though the parent company Fully has faced uncertainty and the arm is currently listed as unavailable on Amazon (check Fully.com). The Ergotron Trace (~$411) sets the premium benchmark with its refined design and straight-up-and-down pole movement, while the Humanscale M10 (~$455) targets high-end office environments with patented weight-compensating spring technology. All models listed support standard VESA 75x75mm and 100x100mm mounting patterns. [src1, src2, src7]

## Best for Each Use Case

### Best Overall: Ergotron LX Pro (~$199) -- [Check price](https://knowledgelib.io/go/ergotron-lx-pro-monitor-arms)
The Ergotron LX Pro earns its top ranking with an exceptionally easy tool-free installation using a clever handhold clamp mechanism. It offers 360 degrees of swivel, -15 to 75 degrees of tilt, and 13 inches of height adjustment via patented Constant Force Technology. The lift mechanism has passed Ergotron's 10,000-cycle motion test, ensuring years of smooth operation. Supports monitors up to 34 inches and 22 lbs with VESA 75x75 or 100x100mm. [src1, src2, src5]

### Best for Heavy/Ultrawide Monitors: Ergotron HX (~$329) -- [Check price](https://knowledgelib.io/go/ergotron-hx)
The Ergotron HX is the only arm rated to hold monitors up to 42 lbs and screen sizes up to 49 inches. For massive ultrawide monitors like the Samsung Odyssey G9 or LG 49WQ95C, the HX is unmatched. Its overbuilt construction uses Velcro straps for cable management instead of cheap zip ties. Available with standard or HD pivot for deeply curved 1000R panels. [src2, src4, src6]

### Best Warranty: Fully Jarvis (~$195) -- [Check price](https://knowledgelib.io/go/herman-miller-jarvis)
Wirecutter's top pick after assessing 25 monitor arms. Moves smoothly into a wide range of positions, holds monitors up to 32 inches securely, and is easy to set up. The standout feature is a monumental 15-year warranty that exceeds Ergotron's 10-year coverage. Offers 13 inches of vertical range, 24 inches of side-to-side extension, 90-degree tilt, and 360-degree pan. As of July 2026, the Amazon listing is out of stock ("currently unavailable"); check Fully.com to buy direct. [src1, src4]

### Best Premium: Ergotron Trace (~$411) -- [Check price](https://knowledgelib.io/go/ergotron-trace)
The most refined monitor arm available under $400. Achieves straight up-and-down movement with smooth spring action inside the center pole. The single arm pans 240 degrees, rotates 360 degrees, and tilts 80 degrees. PCWorld calls it the benchmark for what a high-end ergonomic monitor mount should feel like. Supports monitors up to 38 inches and 21.5 lbs. [src2, src7]

### Best Budget Heavy-Duty: MSI MAG MT201 (~$85) -- [Check price](https://knowledgelib.io/go/msi-mag-mt201)
A remarkable value with 44 lbs of weight capacity at ~$85, far exceeding many arms costing three to four times more. Supports screens from 17 to 49 inches with VESA 75x75 and 100x100mm patterns. Spring-assisted adjustment with +50/-20 degree tilt, 180-degree swivel, and 180-degree rotation. Includes both clamp and grommet mounting hardware. Currently ~15% off its $99.99 list price on Amazon (it has dipped as low as $70 during sales). [src2, src5, src6]

### Best Mid-Budget Value: VIVO Premium Aluminum (~$70) -- [Check price](https://knowledgelib.io/go/vivo-premium-aluminum)
Delivers 80% of the Ergotron experience at roughly a third of the price. Pneumatic gas spring arm allows tool-free height adjustment for monitors up to 32 inches and 20 lbs. Aluminum construction with integrated cable management. Includes both clamp and grommet mounting hardware. Over 17,000 positive reviews with a 4.5/5 average on Amazon. (Note: price climbed from ~$40 to ~$70 in 2026.) [src4, src6]

### Best Ultra-Budget: NB North Bayou F80 (~$20) -- [Check price](https://knowledgelib.io/go/north-bayou-f80)
The cheapest legitimate gas-spring monitor arm available, now under $25. Supports 17-30 inch monitors up to 19.8 lbs with aluminum alloy construction. Offers 85 degrees of backward tilt, 30 degrees forward, 360-degree rotation, 10 inches of lift, and 90-degree swivel. Cable management may feel flimsy on the upper arm segment, but at this price point it's the unmatched value pick. [src4, src6]

## Head-to-Head Comparisons

### Ergotron LX Pro vs Ergotron LX
At ~$199 vs ~$200, the LX Pro and the original LX now sit within a dollar of each other on Amazon. The LX Pro adds tool-free clamp installation, a refined cable spine, and a slightly cleaner industrial design; the original LX adds 3 lbs of capacity (25 vs 22 lbs) and 1" of reach. [src1, src2]
**Pick LX Pro if:** you value tool-free setup, smoother movement, and modern aesthetics.
**Pick LX if:** you need to mount a heavier monitor (22-25 lb range) or want the proven 10+ year design.

### Ergotron LX Pro vs Fully Jarvis
Both ~$195-$199 when both are in stock. Jarvis offers a 15-year warranty vs LX Pro's 10 years and is Wirecutter's longstanding top pick. As of July 2026 the Jarvis Amazon listing is out of stock ("currently unavailable") — often only buyable via Fully.com. The LX Pro is the more reliable purchase right now. [src1, src2]
**Pick LX Pro if:** you want guaranteed in-stock availability and tool-free install.
**Pick Jarvis if:** the 15-year warranty matters and you can wait for stock or buy direct.

### Ergotron HX vs MSI MAG MT201
HX at $329 vs MT201 at ~$85. The MT201 wins on raw spec (44 vs 42 lb capacity, similar reach) and price. The HX wins on Constant Force smoothness, 10-year warranty (vs 3 yr), and the optional HD pivot for deeply curved ultrawides. [src2, src4, src6]
**Pick HX if:** the monitor costs over $1,500 or has a 1000R curve; warranty and build matter long-term.
**Pick MT201 if:** budget is tight, the monitor is under $800, and you accept a 3-year warranty.

### VIVO Premium Aluminum vs MSI MAG MT201
VIVO ~$70 vs MT201 ~$85. VIVO has cleaner aluminum styling and a 20 lb / 32" rating at a slightly lower price. MT201 doubles VIVO's capacity (44 lbs) and supports up to 49" screens. [src5, src6]
**Pick VIVO if:** monitor is ≤32" and ≤20 lbs; you prefer refined aesthetics.
**Pick MT201 if:** monitor is ultrawide, heavy, or both.

### Ergotron LX Dual vs Two Single LX Pro Arms
LX Dual ~$427 (one base, two arms) vs two LX Pro ~$398 (two bases, two arms). The gap has narrowed to ~$30 after a 2026 LX Dual price cut. The LX Dual saves desk-edge real estate; two singles still cost a little less and give independent positioning. [src2, src3]
**Pick LX Dual if:** desk edge space is tight or symmetric matching matters.
**Pick two singles if:** budget matters or you may rearrange monitors later.

## Decision Logic

### If monitor weighs over 25 lbs or is 35+ inches ultrawide
--> Ergotron HX (~$329) is the only mainstream arm rated for 20-42 lbs. The MSI MAG MT201 (~$85) handles up to 44 lbs on paper but lacks the build quality for premium heavy panels. For deeply curved 1000R ultrawides, get the HX with HD pivot. [src2, src4]

### If budget < $25
--> NB North Bayou F80 (~$20) is the cheapest legitimate gas-spring arm for monitors under 19.8 lbs and 30 inches. Avoid sub-$15 unbranded arms; build quality is unreliable at that price. [src4, src6]

### If budget < $90
--> Either MSI MAG MT201 (~$85) for heavy or large screens (up to 44 lbs / 49 inches) or VIVO Premium Aluminum (~$70) for refined finish on standard monitors. Both have gas springs and VESA 75/100 support. [src4, src5, src6]

### If primary concern is long-term durability and warranty
--> Fully Jarvis (~$195) with its 15-year warranty, or any Ergotron model with 10-year warranty. Avoid budget arms from unknown brands with 1-2 year warranties if the arm will hold an expensive monitor. [src1, src4]

### If mounting dual monitors side-by-side
--> Ergotron LX Dual (~$427) for two monitors up to 27 inches / 20 lbs each. The LX Dual now sits only ~$30 above two single LX Pro arms (~$398 combined) after a 2026 price cut, so the unified base is easier to justify; two singles still give independent positioning if you may rearrange later. [src2, src3]

### If you want the smoothest, most premium experience
--> Ergotron Trace (~$411) for refined vertical movement and modern aesthetics. Humanscale M10 (~$455) if you need maximum weight capacity with patented weight-compensating springs. [src2, src7]

### Default recommendation
--> Ergotron LX Pro (~$199) is the safest pick for unknown requirements. Tool-free setup, 22 lb capacity covers most standard and ultrawide monitors up to 34 inches, 10-year warranty, and smooth Constant Force Technology. [src1, src2, src5]

## Important Caveats

- Prices are approximate US street prices as of July 2026. Sales, regional pricing, and color variants can shift prices significantly.
- All listed arms require VESA-compatible monitors (75x75mm or 100x100mm mounting holes). Some monitors, particularly Apple displays and certain Samsung models, do not include VESA mounts without an adapter.
- Gas spring arms have minimum weight thresholds. Mounting a monitor below the minimum weight causes the arm to drift upward. Check both the minimum and maximum weight ratings.
- Desk thickness and edge profile matter. Clamp mounts need a flat edge and sufficient thickness (typically 0.4-3.9 inches). Rounded desk edges or desks thinner than 0.8 inches may require grommet mounting.
- Cable management quality varies widely. Ergotron uses external routing clips on most models; the Trace and Humanscale M10 offer superior integrated cable channels.
