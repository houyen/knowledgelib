## What are the best 240Hz and 360Hz gaming monitors in 2026?

## TL;DR

**Top pick: ASUS ROG Swift PG27AQDP (~$799)** — 480Hz 1440p WOLED, world's fastest mainstream OLED, recent street-price drop from ~$1,000. **Best value: MSI MAG 271QPX QD-OLED (~$579)** — cheapest 360Hz QD-OLED with USB-C after a mid-2026 price drop. **Best budget: Dell SE2726HG (~$130)** — genuine 240Hz IPS at commodity price. ## Summary

The high-refresh-rate monitor market in 2026 has been transformed by OLED technology pushing into the 360Hz-540Hz range at 1440p, while 4K 240Hz panels have become viable for the first time. QD-OLED and WOLED panels dominate the premium tier, delivering near-instant 0.03ms response times, infinite contrast ratios, and wide color gamuts that IPS monitors cannot match. The ASUS ROG Swift PG27AQDP (~$799 street, down from $1,000 MSRP) is the best overall high-refresh-rate monitor, offering 480Hz at 1440p OLED with exceptional motion clarity and HDR. For value buyers, the MSI MAG 271QPX QD-OLED (~$579 after a mid-2026 price drop) delivers 360Hz 1440p performance at the lowest price for a QD-OLED panel. [src1, src2, src5, src9]

Budget-conscious competitive gamers now have compelling options too. The Dell SE2726HG delivers 240Hz IPS performance at just $130, while the Gigabyte M27Q X offers 240Hz at 1440p with a KVM switch for ~$250. At the ultra-premium end, the ASUS ROG Swift PG27AQWP-W pushes to 540Hz (or 720Hz at 720p) using Tandem OLED technology at $1,099. The standout value shift of mid-2026 is the QD-OLED price collapse: Samsung's Odyssey OLED G6 G60SF now hits 500Hz at 1440p QD-OLED for ~$600 (down from $999), and the US-launched MSI MAG 272QP QD-OLED X50 brings the same 500Hz QD-OLED panel with USB-C Power Delivery at ~$650. The gap between "fast enough" and "absolute fastest" has never been wider in price or narrower in real-world perceptible difference. [src1, src4, src6, src9]

## Head-to-Head Comparisons

### ASUS ROG Swift PG27AQDP vs Samsung Odyssey OLED G6 G60SD
With the PG27AQDP now street-priced at ~$799, the gap between 480Hz WOLED and 360Hz QD-OLED has narrowed to ~$100. PG27AQDP wins on motion clarity (120Hz of additional refresh), HDR peaks (1,300 nits vs ~1,000), and AI-Assistant features. G60SD wins on color volume (QD-OLED's wider DCI-P3) and matte anti-glare coating. [src1, src2]

**Pick PG27AQDP if:** Motion clarity matters most or you play CS2 / Valorant where every extra Hz counts.
**Pick G60SD if:** Color saturation matters (creative use) or you have ambient room lighting where matte beats glossy.

### ASUS ROG Swift PG27AQWP-W vs Samsung Odyssey OLED G6 G60SF
The PG27AQWP-W holds at ~$1,099, but the G60SF has collapsed to ~$600 in mid-2026 — a ~$500 gap. PG27AQWP-W uses Tandem WOLED (540Hz, 0.02ms, 720Hz dual-mode at 720p). G60SF uses Gen-3 QD-OLED (500Hz, 0.03ms). Tom's Hardware: PG27AQWP-W is faster on paper; G60SF wins on color volume and now on value by a wide margin. [src1, src2]

**Pick PG27AQWP-W if:** You're a competitive esports pro who can perceive 540Hz vs 500Hz, or you want 720Hz dual-mode for tournaments.
**Pick G60SF if:** You value QD-OLED color saturation or want the cheapest 500Hz OLED — it is now ~$500 less than the Tandem WOLED.

### MSI MAG 271QPX QD-OLED vs MSI MAG 272QP QD-OLED X50
Both are MSI 1440p QD-OLEDs. The 271QPX is older (360Hz, ~$579). The 272QP X50 is the 2026 refresh (500Hz, ~$650) with 15W USB-C PD, brighter HDR True Black 500 certification, and a Gen-3 QD-OLED panel. After mid-2026 price drops the 500Hz X50 is only ~$70 more. [src9]

**Pick 271QPX if:** Budget is the priority and 360Hz is sufficient.
**Pick 272QP X50 if:** You need USB-C docking, higher peak brightness, or want maximum refresh-rate headroom.

### ASUS ROG Swift PG27UCDM vs ASUS ROG Swift PG27AQDP
The 4K-vs-fastest-1440p question. PG27UCDM is 4K 240Hz QD-OLED (166 PPI, DP 2.1). PG27AQDP is 1440p 480Hz WOLED. Same family, different priorities. [src2, src6]

**Pick PG27UCDM if:** You want a single monitor for both AAA cinematic gaming and productivity (4K text clarity, DP 2.1 future-proofing).
**Pick PG27AQDP if:** Competitive frame rates matter more than pixel density; 1440p 480Hz lets a mid-range GPU saturate the panel.

### Alienware AW2524HF vs ASUS ROG Swift PG27AQDP
The 1080p IPS-vs-1440p OLED question for competitive gaming. AW2524HF is 24.5-inch 500Hz IPS, ~$532. PG27AQDP is 27-inch 480Hz WOLED, ~$799. Pro CS2 / Valorant players still favor 24" 1080p for tighter visual targets and lower GPU load. [src2, src4]

**Pick AW2524HF if:** You compete at the highest tier of CS2 / Valorant, or your GPU can't drive 1440p at 480fps.
**Pick PG27AQDP if:** You also play AAA / mixed titles and value HDR and OLED contrast for non-competitive time.

## Key Market Trends (2026)

- **OLED dominates 360Hz+**: Every 360Hz or faster monitor worth recommending in 2026 uses OLED (QD-OLED, WOLED, or Tandem OLED). IPS remains competitive only at 240Hz or budget price points. [src1, src2]

- **DisplayPort 2.1 becoming essential**: 4K 240Hz and uncompressed 1440p 480Hz require DP 2.1 UHBR20 bandwidth. Only NVIDIA RTX 50-series and AMD RDNA 4 GPUs ship with DP 2.1. [src2, src6]
- **Price erosion in QD-OLED**: 1440p 360Hz QD-OLED panels dropped from $1,100+ in 2024 to ~$580-630 by mid-2026, making OLED accessible to mainstream gamers. The 500Hz tier collapsed too — Samsung's G60SF fell from $999 to ~$600 and MSI's 272QP X50 from ~$850 to ~$650. The ASUS PG27AQDP also saw a major street-price drop from ~$1,000 to ~$799. [src5, src6, src9]
- **Tandem OLED debuts**: ASUS's dual-stacked OLED panel (PG27AQWP-W) doubles brightness and lifespan while pushing to 540Hz. Expect wider adoption by late 2026. [src1]
- **240Hz becomes budget**: Dell's SE2726HG at $130 proves 240Hz IPS is now commodity hardware. The "high refresh rate" floor has shifted from 144Hz to 240Hz. [src6]

## Important Caveats

- Prices are approximate US street prices as of June 2026. Regional pricing and sales vary significantly.
- OLED burn-in risk is real but mitigated by manufacturer technologies (pixel shifting, logo dimming, OLED Care). All listed OLED monitors include 3-year burn-in warranties.
- Refresh rate benefits diminish above 240Hz for most users. The perceptual difference between 360Hz and 480Hz is measurable but subtle compared to the 60Hz-to-144Hz jump. Professional esports players may notice; casual gamers likely will not.
- GPU requirements scale non-linearly. Driving 480Hz at 1440p in competitive titles requires minimum RTX 4080 / RX 7900 XT class hardware. Demanding AAA games will run at 120-200fps even on flagship GPUs.
- DisplayPort version matters. Monitors advertising 480Hz+ at 1440p require DP 1.4 with DSC at minimum; DP 2.1 eliminates compression artifacts. Check your GPU's port specifications before purchasing.
