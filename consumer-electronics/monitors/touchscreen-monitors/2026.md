# Best Touchscreen Monitors (2026)

## What are the best touchscreen monitors in 2026?

## TL;DR

**Top pick: Dell P2424HT (~$445)** — articulating stand, USB-C hub + Gigabit Ethernet, smoothest 10-point touch in class. **Best value: HP E24t G5 (~$295)** — the only sub-$300 desktop pick, Advanced In-Cell Touch + 75Hz. **Best budget: ViewSonic TD1655 (~$300)** — 15.6" portable with included stylus and dual USB-C. [src1, src2, src4]

## Summary

The touchscreen monitor market in 2026 spans from portable 15.6-inch travel companions to premium 27-inch 4K desktop panels. For most office users, the Dell P2424HT (~$1400) is the standout pick, combining a uniquely articulating stand, USB-C hub with Ethernet, and responsive 10-point touch in a well-reviewed package. The ASUS BE24ECSBT climbed back to ~$369 as of this refresh, so the HP E24t G5 (~$295) is now the best-value desktop pick — the only sub-$300 option — with Advanced In-Cell Touch and a 75Hz panel. For creative professionals who need 4K resolution, the Alogic Clarity Pro Touch (~$1,400) is the only 27-inch 4K touchscreen with a built-in retractable webcam and 99% Adobe RGB. [src1, src2, src4]

The market is split into three tiers: budget portable monitors ($300-$430), mainstream 24-inch desktop displays ($295-$445), and premium 4K panels ($1,400+). Nearly every desktop model now ships with USB-C connectivity and 10-point projected capacitive or in-cell touch. The biggest constraint remains resolution — 4K touchscreens are rare and expensive, and macOS still lacks native touch support. [src3, src4, src7]

## Best for Each Use Case

### Best Overall: Dell P2424HT (~$445) — [Check price](https://knowledgelib.io/go/dell-p2424ht-touchscreen-monitors)
The Dell P2424HT earned praise from both Tom's Guide and PCWorld for its uniquely articulating arm stand that pivots at both top and bottom, making it easy to tilt the display flat for touch interaction. USB-C input drives three USB-A ports, a second USB-C, and a Gigabit Ethernet port — a full docking solution in one cable. Touch response is smooth and consistent. [src1, src2]

### Best USB-C Hub / Docking: ASUS BE24ECSBT (~$369) — [Check price](https://knowledgelib.io/go/asus-be24ecsbt)
Delivers 80W USB-C power delivery (highest in class), three USB 3.2 Gen1 downstream ports, and DisplayPort daisy-chaining for multi-monitor setups. The projected capacitive 10-point touch is responsive and the 100% sRGB IPS panel produces accurate colors. TUV Rheinland-certified eye care reduces strain. [src3, src4]

### Best Value / Enterprise: HP E24t G5 (~$295) — [Check price](https://knowledgelib.io/go/hp-e24t-g5)
At ~$295 the HP E24t G5 is now the only sub-$300 desktop touchscreen in this roundup after the ASUS BE24ECSBT climbed back above $360. HP's Advanced In-Cell Touch (AIT) technology integrates the touch sensor into the display panel itself, reducing parallax and improving accuracy. The 75Hz refresh rate is above average for this category. HP Display Center software provides centralized management. Ergonomic stand with height, tilt, swivel, and pivot adjustability. [src4, src6]

### Best for Drawing / Stylus Use: ViewSonic TD2455 (~$400) — [Check price](https://knowledgelib.io/go/viewsonic-td2455)
Ships with an included stylus pen (magnetically attaches to the base) and a dual-hinge ergonomic stand that transitions from desktop to podium mode. The in-cell touch technology minimizes parallax offset. USB-C with 60W charging, plus HDMI and DisplayPort in/out for daisy-chaining. Three-sided ultra-thin bezel design. [src4, src6]

### Best 4K / Creative Professional: Alogic Clarity Pro Touch 27 (~$1,400) — [Check price](https://knowledgelib.io/go/alogic-clarity-pro-touch)
The only 27-inch 4K (3840x2160) touchscreen monitor with a built-in retractable 8MP webcam. Color accuracy is exceptional: 100% sRGB, 99% Adobe RGB, 97% DCI-P3. HDR 400 certified. The 10-point One Glass Solution (OGS) touch panel minimizes parallax. An 8-in-1 USB hub with 65W power delivery rounds out the connectivity. [src5]

### Best Budget Large Screen: Acer VT270 bmizx (~$320) — [Check price](https://knowledgelib.io/go/acer-vt270-bmizx)
The most affordable 27-inch touchscreen option with 10-point touch, 75Hz refresh rate, and 99% sRGB coverage. Adaptive-Sync eliminates tearing during video playback. Connectivity is more basic (HDMI and VGA, no USB-C), which keeps the price down. The original VT270 listing went unavailable; the current live listing is the refreshed UT272 revision of the same 27" Acer touch panel. Good for kiosks, reception desks, and shared workspaces. [src4, src6]

### Best Portable (No Battery): ASUS ZenScreen Touch MB16AHT (~$431) — [Check price](https://knowledgelib.io/go/asus-zenscreen-mb16aht)
A 15.6-inch FHD portable touchscreen with 10-point touch, dual USB-C and mini-HDMI, a fold-out kickstand, and a tripod socket. Touch works across Windows, Chrome OS, and Linux. Weighs under 900g. No built-in battery (draws power from the host device), which keeps it lighter than the battery-equipped MB16AMT. Note: ASUS's first-party Amazon listing went unavailable, so the only live listing is a third-party seller at ~$431 — a steep markup for a portable that historically sold near $280; if you can find it at MSRP elsewhere it is better value than the current Amazon price implies. [src3, src4]

### Best Portable (With Stylus): ViewSonic TD1655 (~$300) — [Check price](https://knowledgelib.io/go/viewsonic-td1655)
A 15.6-inch portable touchscreen with an included active stylus pen, dual USB-C with 60W power pass-through, built-in speakers, and a protective magnetic cover that doubles as a stand. vTouch software enables basic macOS touch support. Particularly useful for on-site presentations and collaborative annotation. [src4, src6]

## Head-to-Head Comparisons

### Dell P2424HT vs ASUS BE24ECSBT
At identical 23.8" 1080p IPS panels with 10-point touch and USB-C PD, the Dell ($445) adds Gigabit Ethernet and an articulating arm stand that pivots flat for kiosk-style touch; the ASUS ($369) doesn't have Ethernet but pushes 80W PD vs Dell's 65W and supports DisplayPort daisy-chaining. With the ASUS now only ~$75 cheaper, the Dell's Ethernet hub and articulating stand are easier to justify. [src1, src3]

**Pick Dell P2424HT if:** you need Ethernet in the monitor hub, or you frequently tilt the screen flat to use touch like a tablet.
**Pick ASUS BE24ECSBT if:** you need 80W to charge a power-hungry laptop, or you're chaining two monitors via DisplayPort.

### HP E24t G5 vs ASUS BE24ECSBT
Both target enterprise/office deployment, but the price gap has widened: HP ($295) is now ~$74 cheaper than the ASUS ($369). HP wins on touch precision via Advanced In-Cell Touch (AIT) and 75Hz refresh rate, but uses USB-B upstream (no power delivery, no laptop charging). ASUS is a true USB-C single-cable solution. [src4, src6]

**Pick HP E24t G5 if:** you want the lowest price, a fleet IT setup with HP Display Center, or the lowest parallax for handwriting/signing, and your laptop docks separately.
**Pick ASUS BE24ECSBT if:** you want single-cable USB-C docking with 80W charging for your laptop and will pay the ~$74 premium for it.

### Alogic Clarity Pro Touch vs Acer ProCreator PE320QXT
Both are premium creative-pro touchscreens. Alogic ($1,400) is 27" 4K with a retractable 8MP webcam and HDR 400. Acer's new PE320QXT ($1,999) jumps to 32" 6K (6016×3384) with Calman-verified Delta E < 1, but at a 40% premium. [src5, src8]

**Pick Alogic Clarity Pro Touch if:** you need 27" 4K touch with built-in webcam under $1,500, and DCI-P3 97% is enough for your color workflow.
**Pick Acer ProCreator PE320QXT if:** you need 6K resolution, 32" screen real estate, or Delta E < 1 print-proofing accuracy and budget exceeds $1,900.

### ViewSonic TD1655 vs ASUS ZenScreen MB16AHT
Both are 15.6" FHD portable touchscreens, but pricing has diverged: the TD1655 is ~$300 while the MB16AHT's only live Amazon listing is a third-party seller at ~$431. TD1655 ships with an active stylus and a smart cover stand; MB16AHT skips the stylus but is lighter (under 900g) with a fold-out kickstand and tripod mount. At current Amazon prices the TD1655 is the clear value winner. [src3, src4]

**Pick ViewSonic TD1655 if:** you want the lower price, need stylus input for annotation, and you'll use the included macOS vTouch software.
**Pick ASUS ZenScreen MB16AHT if:** weight matters (under 900g), or you'll mount it on a tripod for presentations/streaming — and only if you can source it near MSRP.

### Dell P2424HT vs ViewSonic TD2455
Both are 24" 1080p desktop touch monitors with USB-C. Dell ($445) has Gigabit Ethernet and the articulating stand. TD2455 ($400) ships with a stylus pen and a dual-hinge ergonomic stand that transitions to podium mode, and now undercuts the Dell by ~$45. [src1, src4]

**Pick Dell P2424HT if:** you want the Ethernet hub and don't need a stylus.
**Pick ViewSonic TD2455 if:** you want pen-based annotation on a 24" desk display and the podium-mode stand for shared/standing-desk use.

## Key Market Trends (2026)

- **USB-C hub integration is standard**: Nearly every new touchscreen monitor ships with USB-C for single-cable data, video, and power delivery. Older VGA/DVI-only models are being phased out. [src3, src4]
- **In-cell and OGS touch reduce parallax**: Advanced touch technologies that integrate the sensor into the display panel (HP AIT, Alogic OGS, ViewSonic in-cell) deliver near-zero parallax, closing the gap with dedicated pen displays. [src5, src6]
- **4K touchscreens remain premium**: Despite 4K being mainstream for non-touch monitors, only a handful of 4K touchscreen panels exist, all above $1,000. Expect prices to drop as panel yields improve through 2027. [src5, src7]
- **6K touch arrives in 2026**: Acer launched the ProCreator PE320QXT in April 2026 — the first 32-inch 6K (6016x3384) touchscreen monitor at $1,999. It pairs Calman-verified color accuracy (99% Adobe RGB / 99% DCI-P3, Delta E < 1) with full 10-point touch and an 8MP webcam, signaling that ultra-high-resolution touch is moving into the prosumer tier. [src8]
- **Portable touchscreen segment growing fast**: The portable monitor market is projected to exceed $1.8 billion by 2032, driven by remote work and USB-C adoption. Touch-enabled portables represent the fastest-growing sub-segment. [src7]
- **macOS touch support still absent**: Apple continues to withhold native touchscreen support from macOS, limiting the Mac-compatible touchscreen market to third-party software workarounds. [src3]

## Important Caveats

- Prices are approximate U.S. street prices as of July 2026. Sales, regional pricing, and retailer promotions cause frequent variation. As of this refresh, the original Amazon listings for the Dell P2424HT, Acer VT270, and ASUS ZenScreen MB16AHT went "currently unavailable" and buy links were repointed to live in-stock listings (a Dell P2424HT bundle at ~$445, the Acer UT272 revision at ~$320, and a third-party MB16AHT listing at ~$431). The MB16AHT's only live Amazon listing carries a steep markup over its ~$280 historical street price — check other retailers before buying. Verify stock and price before ordering.
- Most desktop touchscreen monitors are limited to 1080p. If you need higher resolution with touch, expect to pay 3-4x the price of a standard 1080p touch display.
- Touch responsiveness varies with driver installation. Windows provides the best native multi-touch support; Linux support depends on the kernel version and DE; macOS requires third-party workarounds.
- Parallax (the gap between your finger and the touched pixel) varies significantly between models. In-cell and OGS panels have the lowest parallax; surface-mounted capacitive panels have the highest.
