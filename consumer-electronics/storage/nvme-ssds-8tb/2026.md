## Summary

The 8TB consumer NVMe SSD market in 2026 has matured technically but pricing and availability have gone haywire. The **WD Black SN8100 8TB** (shipping since October 2025) is now the drive to beat: SanDisk's BiCS8 TLC and Silicon Motion SM2508 platform delivers 14,900/13,200 MB/s sequential and 2.2M/2.4M random IOPS on 4,800 TBW of endurance while averaging just ~7.5W — reviewers rate the SN8100 the fastest consumer SSD ever made, beating the Samsung 9100 Pro in 3DMark storage testing. At ~$1,900 on Amazon it now undercuts the **Samsung 9100 Pro 8TB** (~$1,999, heatsink ~$1,870), which remains the more thoroughly tested option at 8TB specifically and holds a narrow sequential-write edge (13,400 MB/s). The **WD Black SN850X 8TB** (launched late 2024) is still the consensus best PCIe 4.0 8TB drive — Tom's Hardware calls it "the best 8TB SSD on the market with good all-around performance and no scary surprises" — and after a ~16% drop to ~$1,200 it is now simply the cheapest in-stock 8TB drive of any kind. The **Lexar NM790 8TB** (~$1,400) has risen ~7% and lost its budget crown, but is now the only in-stock single-sided 8TB drive, which makes it the default for laptops and the PS5. [src1, src2, src3, src5, src8, src10, src11, src12]

The bigger story since June is availability, not price. Only four 8TB families are still purchasable on Amazon — WD Black SN8100, Samsung 9100 Pro, WD Black SN850X, and Lexar NM790. The Corsair MP600 Pro XT, NH, and LPX, the TeamGroup MP44, the Inland Performance Plus, the Sabrent Rocket 4 Plus heatsink SKU, and the Sabrent Rocket Q have all gone unavailable, and the remaining Sabrent Rocket 4 Plus 8TB has spiked to ~$3,200 — more than either PCIe 5.0 flagship, which disqualifies it on price alone. Top-end pricing has actually eased 10-16% since June as PCIe 5.0 supply improved, but the mid-tier has thinned out entirely. Micron's exit from the Crucial consumer business (announced December 2025, shipments ended February 2026) permanently removes the T700/T705 line from this category. Two 4TB TLC drives still cost far less than one 8TB drive, but consume a second M.2 slot. [src6, src9, src10, src13]

## Head-to-Head Comparisons

### WD Black SN8100 8TB vs Samsung 9100 Pro 8TB
The two PCIe 5.0 8TB flagships, now separated by ~$100. On paper they are near-identical: the SN8100 reads marginally faster (14,900 vs 14,800 MB/s), the 9100 Pro writes marginally faster (13,400 vs 13,200 MB/s), and both carry 4,800 TBW and five-year warranties. Two things break the tie. The SN8100 averages ~7.5W against the 9100 Pro's 11-14W, so it throttles less on long transfers and needs less cooling, and reviewers rate the SN8100 platform the fastest consumer SSD ever made, ahead of the 9100 Pro in 3DMark storage testing. Against that, the 9100 Pro 8TB has been independently tested at 8TB by three outlets while SN8100 review data covers only 1-4TB. [src2, src3, src10, src11, src12]

**Pick WD Black SN8100 8TB if:** You want the fastest and cheapest PCIe 5.0 8TB drive and a cooler, lower-power drive — the default choice.
**Pick Samsung 9100 Pro 8TB if:** You want the 8TB SKU with published independent test data, or the heatsink bundle (~$1,870) at a lower price than the bare SN8100.

### WD Black SN8100 8TB vs WD Black SN850X 8TB
Same brand, two generations apart, ~$700 between them. The SN8100 is ~2x faster on sequentials (14,900 vs 7,200 MB/s) and multiples faster on random IOPS, and draws less power despite it. But for gaming, OS, and general productivity the SN850X is indistinguishable in real-world use — PCIe 5.0 bandwidth only materializes in 8K video transfers, large AI dataset reads, and direct-storage GPU streaming. The SN850X is also double-sided, where the SN8100's PCB configuration at 8TB is unpublished. [src1, src10, src11]

**Pick WD Black SN8100 8TB if:** PCIe 5.0 board + sustained sequential / AI / 8K video workload, and the ~$700 premium is justified by the workload.
**Pick WD Black SN850X 8TB if:** Gaming, OS, or general productivity — saves ~$700 with no perceptible loss, and it is the cheapest 8TB drive on the market right now.

### WD Black SN850X 8TB vs Lexar NM790 8TB
The June ordering has reversed. SN850X (~$1,200) is now ~$200 *cheaper* than the NM790 (~$1,400) while also having a DRAM cache, mature firmware, and ~30% better random write IOPS — the NM790's only remaining advantages are a higher TBW rating (6,000 vs 4,800) and its single-sided PCB. That form factor is now the entire argument for it: SN850X is double-sided and desktop-only at 8TB, while the NM790 is the only in-stock 8TB drive that fits a laptop or PS5. [src1, src5, src9]

**Pick WD Black SN850X 8TB if:** Desktop with double-sided M.2 clearance — it is cheaper and better on every axis that matters.
**Pick Lexar NM790 8TB if:** Laptop, PS5, or ITX board that cannot take a double-sided drive — you are paying ~$200 for the form factor.

### Samsung 9100 Pro 8TB vs Sabrent Rocket 4 Plus 8TB
This matchup has collapsed. The 9100 Pro roughly doubles peak speeds on PCIe 5.0 while the Rocket 4 Plus is purely PCIe 4.0 (7,100 MB/s ceiling) — and after a ~33% spike the Rocket 4 Plus (~$3,200) now costs ~$1,200 *more* than the 9100 Pro (~$1,999). The Rocket 4 Plus retains a genuine advantage in endurance (6,000 vs 4,800 TBW) and Sabrent's post-SLC-cache write consistency, but no endurance edge justifies paying 60% more for half the speed. [src2, src4, src9]

**Pick Samsung 9100 Pro 8TB if:** Essentially always at current pricing.
**Pick Sabrent Rocket 4 Plus 8TB if:** Only if it falls back under ~$1,300 and you need maximum TBW on a PCIe 4.0 system.

### WD Black SN850X 8TB vs Corsair MP600 Pro XT 8TB
Nearly identical PCIe 4.0 performance (7,200/6,600 vs 7,000/6,100 MB/s). SN850X uses Kioxia BiCS6 TLC + WD Triton controller (mature, low power). MP600 Pro XT uses Phison E18 + Micron 176L TLC and includes an aluminum heatspreader out of the box. SN850X has better random IOPS and a higher TBW (4,800 vs 3,000); MP600 Pro XT has better thermal management without an external cooler. Academic for now: the MP600 Pro XT remains out of stock on Amazon while the SN850X (~$1,200, heatsink ~$1,250) is the cheapest 8TB drive available. [src1, src6]

**Pick WD Black SN850X 8TB if:** You want the best raw performance, an in-stock option, and the lower price — currently the only real answer.
**Pick Corsair MP600 Pro XT 8TB if:** It returns to stock and you need an integrated heatspreader where the stock motherboard M.2 cooler is inadequate.

- **WD Black SN8100 reaches 8TB and takes the crown**: SanDisk shipped the 8TB SN8100 in October 2025 (14,900/13,200 MB/s, 2.2M/2.4M IOPS, 4,800 TBW, BiCS8 TLC on a tuned SM2508 controller) at a $999.99 launch MSRP / $1,049.99 with heatsink. - **The 8TB shelf emptied out**: The defining 2026 shift is availability, not price. Only four families are still purchasable — WD Black SN8100, Samsung 9100 Pro, WD Black SN850X, Lexar NM790. Corsair's entire MP600 Pro 8TB line (XT, NH, LPX), TeamGroup MP44, Inland Performance Plus, the Sabrent Rocket 4 Plus heatsink SKU, and Sabrent Rocket Q have all gone unavailable, and the surviving Sabrent Rocket 4 Plus 8TB spiked ~33% to ~$3,200. [src9]
- **Top-end prices eased while the mid-tier vanished**: Against the run of the shortage, PCIe 5.0 and premium PCIe 4.0 pricing fell 10-16% between June and July 2026 (Samsung 9100 Pro 8TB ~$2,230 → ~$1,999; WD Black SN850X 8TB ~$1,429 → ~$1,200), even as the Lexar NM790 8TB rose ~7% to ~$1,400. Prices remain roughly 2x their 2025 lows; NAND wafer spot pricing is up several hundred percent vs mid-2025 and the shortage is forecast to persist into 2027. [src9]
- **Micron exits Crucial, killing a PCIe 5.0 line**: Micron announced on 3 December 2025 that it would wind down the Crucial consumer business, with shipments ending February 2026 — permanently removing the T700/T705 Gen5 drives that helped popularize PCIe 5.0. It is the clearest signal yet that NAND makers are reallocating consumer supply to AI/enterprise customers. [src13]
- **PCIe 5.0 efficiency stops being an oxymoron**: The old rule — PCIe 5.0 means 11-14W and mandatory active cooling — no longer holds universally. The SN8100 averages ~7.5W, which SanDisk claims is over 100% more efficient than its previous Gen4 flagships, and removes the heatsink from the buying decision. The Samsung 9100 Pro and MSI Spatium M580 still draw 11-14W sustained. [src10, src11, src12]
- **DRAM-less HMB closes the gap**: Lexar NM790 and TeamGroup MP44 prove HMB-based 8TB drives can hit 7,000 MB/s reads and maintain 6,000 TBW endurance — within 5-10% of DRAM drives on most workloads. But the cost advantage that justified them has evaporated: the DRAM-equipped SN850X 8TB is now ~$200 cheaper than the DRAM-less NM790, leaving form factor as HMB's only remaining argument at 8TB. [src5, src7, src9]
- **Two-sided 2280 becomes standard at 8TB**: Samsung 9100 Pro and WD Black SN850X both went two-sided to fit 8TB on a 2280 PCB — locks them out of laptops, PS5, and many ITX boards. With the TeamGroup MP44 and Corsair MP600 Pro LPX now unavailable, the Lexar NM790 is the last in-stock single-sided 8TB drive, and it commands a ~$200 premium over the cheaper double-sided SN850X purely for that compatibility. [src1, src2, src3]
- **QLC retreats from premium tier**: Sabrent Rocket Q 8TB (QLC PCIe 3.0) is now functionally obsolete vs TLC alternatives at similar prices, and is out of stock. No major manufacturer shipped new 8TB QLC drives in 2025-2026. TLC dominates the 8TB consumer category. [src6]
- **Two 4TB drives often beat one 8TB on $/TB**: A pair of 4TB WD Black SN850X drives still runs less than a single ~$1,200 8TB SN850X, though the SN850X's price drop has narrowed the gap. Single-stick 8TB justifies its premium when M.2 slots are scarce (laptops, ITX) or when single-volume convenience matters. [src1, src9]

- Prices are approximate US street prices verified 16 July 2026 during the AI-driven NAND shortage. 8TB drives still run roughly 2x their 2025 lows despite a 10-16% easing at the top end since June; expect continued volatility, and note that eight of the fourteen SKUs below are currently unavailable. Check Tom's Hardware SSD price tracker before purchase.
- Only four 8TB families are in stock as of 16 July 2026: WD Black SN8100, Samsung 9100 Pro, WD Black SN850X, and Lexar NM790. Corsair MP600 Pro XT/NH/LPX, TeamGroup MP44, Inland Performance Plus, Sabrent Rocket 4 Plus (heatsink) and Sabrent Rocket Q are all unavailable — they remain in the table for reference and in case they return to stock.
- Prices shown for out-of-stock rows are the LAST OBSERVED street price on the date noted, not a current or expected price. Several were last seen before the NAND shortage deepened — if those SKUs return to stock, expect them to list far higher. Treat them as historical reference only.
- Samsung 9100 Pro 8TB and WD Black SN850X 8TB use double-sided M.2 2280 PCBs. Many laptops, the PS5, and some compact ITX motherboards only support single-sided drives — verify before buying. The Lexar NM790 8TB is the only in-stock single-sided 8TB alternative.
- SanDisk has not published whether the WD Black SN8100 **8TB** PCB is single- or double-sided. The 1-4TB SN8100 capacities are single-sided, but do not assume the 8TB SKU is — verify slot clearance before buying it for a laptop, PS5, or ITX build.
- Independent 8TB-specific test data does not yet exist for the WD Black SN8100 8TB — published reviews cover the 1TB, 2TB, and 4TB capacities. Its 8TB figures here are manufacturer specifications. The Samsung 9100 Pro 8TB is the option with third-party 8TB benchmarks.
- Amazon's listing title for the SN8100 8TB advertises an 11,000 MB/s write speed, which is the 1TB SKU's rating; SanDisk and press coverage of the 8TB launch both specify 13,200 MB/s.
- Most PCIe 5.0 drives (Samsung 9100 Pro, MSI Spatium M580) draw 11-14W sustained and require active or substantial passive cooling. Without it, sustained writes throttle 40-60% within 30-60 seconds. The WD Black SN8100 is the exception at ~7.5W average.
- QLC NAND drives (Sabrent Rocket Q) have severe post-SLC-cache write cliffs and 3-4x lower endurance than TLC. Suitable only for read-heavy archival use.
- Manufacturer sequential read/write figures are peak burst speeds with empty drives. Sustained throughput on a 70%+ full drive can be 30-50% lower, especially on DRAM-less drives.
- Crucial T705 8TB will never ship — Micron exited the Crucial consumer business entirely, ending shipments in February 2026. Samsung 990 Pro 8TB, MSI Spatium M580 FROZR 8TB, Solidigm P44 Pro 8TB, and Seagate FireCuda 540 8TB remain unreleased as of July 2026, with maximum capacities of 4TB or below.
- SanDisk is retiring the WD_Black brand in favour of "SanDisk Optimus" during 2026 — the SN8100 is being rebranded the Optimus GX Pro 8100 with unchanged hardware. Expect listings under either name.
