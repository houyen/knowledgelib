# Phones With User-Replaceable Batteries (2026)

## Which phones have user-replaceable batteries in 2026?

## TL;DR

**Top pick: Fairphone 6 (~$749) — 10/10 iFixit repairability, 8 years OS support to ~2033, 12 user-replaceable parts, two-screw battery access.**
**Best value: HMD Skyline (~$400) — 9/10 iFixit, the only one of these you can actually buy on Amazon US today, $30 batteries via iFixit partnership.**
**Best budget: Nokia C12 (~$80) — true pop-off back, classic 3,000mAh removable cell, no tools needed.**
With the EU 2027 user-replaceability mandate looming, every major OEM is now redesigning batteries — but in 2026 only a handful of phones already comply, and the roster is shrinking: the Fairphone 5 and Nokia G42 were both discontinued over the past year. [src2, src1, src10]

## Summary

The user-replaceable-battery phone category in 2026 is split into two camps. **True pop-off back phones** (Samsung Galaxy XCover7 / XCover7 Pro, RugOne Xever 7 Pro, Nokia C12) let you swap batteries in 30 seconds with no tools — the classic 2010s experience preserved in rugged and ultra-budget niches. **EU 2027-compliant "user-replaceable in ~5 minutes with basic tools"** phones (Fairphone 6, HMD Skyline, Nokia G42/G310) require unscrewing 2-6 screws or pulling adhesive tabs but still satisfy the upcoming regulation and ship with iFixit-grade repair guides plus 5-year OEM parts availability. [src5, src4]

The **EU Battery Regulation (2023/1542)** kicks in 18 February 2027, requiring portable batteries in EU-sold phones to be removable using "commonly available tools" without specialized heat or solvents, with replacement parts available for 5 years at "reasonable price." Apple has already begun retrofitting compliance: the iPhone 16 / 16 Plus uses ionic-liquid adhesive that releases under low-voltage current, but the iPhone 16 Pro / Pro Max still uses stretch-release pull tabs. Samsung's response has been the rugged-only XCover line; flagship Galaxy S models remain glued. Fairphone and HMD have been ahead of the regulation for years and dominate this category by default in 2026. [src9, src4]

**Availability is now the category's binding constraint, not specs.** Fairphone discontinued the Fairphone 5 on 12 January 2026 (new stock sold out; refurbished "New Life Edition" units only), and HMD's Nokia G42 page now reads "no longer available" — both survive here as reference rows, not live recommendations. Fairphone has no Amazon US listing at all: US buyers go through Murena's /e/OS edition, permanently repriced from $899 to **$749** in January 2026. Of the nine models below, only the HMD Skyline, both Galaxy XCover7 variants, the RugOne Xever 7 Pro, and the Nokia C12 have a live Amazon US listing today. [src10, src11]

## Best for Each Use Case

### Best Overall (Sustainability + Longest Support): Fairphone 6 (~$749) — [Check price](https://knowledgelib.io/go/fairphone-6)
Perfect 10/10 on iFixit's repairability scale. Twelve user-replaceable parts (battery, screen, cameras, USB-C port, speakers, more), 5-year manufacturer warranty, ships with a screwdriver, and Fairphone-promised software support through approximately 2033 — roughly 8 years total. Battery upgraded to 4,415mAh from the FP5's 4,200mAh. Snapdragon 7s Gen 3, 8GB RAM, 256GB storage. Trade-off vs FP5: backplate no longer simply pops off — now requires undoing two small screws first. Still the best phone you can buy if longevity is the goal. **Buying it in the US means Murena's /e/OS edition at $749** (permanently cut from the $899 launch price in January 2026) — a de-Googled Android build with no Play Services, optimized for T-Mobile and its MVNOs, not compatible with Verizon. In the EU it is €549 direct from Fairphone (€599 for the /e/OS variant). There is no Amazon US listing. [src6, src2, src11]

### Best Value (US-Friendly): HMD Skyline (~$400) — [Check price](https://knowledgelib.io/go/hmd-skyline)
9/10 iFixit repairability score, sold directly via HMD's iFixit Repair Hub partnership. Battery uses thin adhesive with a built-in plastic liner — pull-tab separates it without the heat or solvents that disqualify most modern phones. Replacement battery ~$30 (£22.99). 144Hz pOLED 6.55-inch display, 108MP triple camera, 50MP selfie, Snapdragon 7s Gen 1, 8/256GB. The first Qi2 wireless charging Android phone. Available off-the-shelf in the US via Amazon, Best Buy, and Target — now the *only* iFixit-partnered repairable phone in this card with reliable US retail stock, which is most of why it is the default pick. [src5, src2]

### Best Rugged / Field Work: Samsung Galaxy XCover7 Pro (~$557) — [Check price](https://knowledgelib.io/go/samsung-galaxy-xcover7-pro)
True tool-free pop-off back covering a removable 4,350mAh cell — the only phone Samsung currently ships with this property. IP68, MIL-STD-810H, Gorilla Glass Victus+, Snapdragon 7s Gen 3, Wi-Fi 6E, Bluetooth 5.4, NFC, expandable microSD up to 2TB, pogo-pin fast charging dock support. Seven years of OS and security updates — matching Samsung's flagship line. The benchmark for field/warehouse/EMS phones where battery hot-swap during a shift is non-negotiable. [src7, src1]

### Best Budget Rugged: Samsung Galaxy XCover7 (~$400) — [Check price](https://knowledgelib.io/go/samsung-galaxy-xcover7)
The non-Pro Galaxy XCover7. Same true pop-off design, IP68 + MIL-STD-810H + Gorilla Glass Victus+, drop-tested to 1.5m onto concrete. Dimensity 6100+, 6GB/128GB, 4,050mAh removable battery, 7 years of updates, Android 14 (upgradable to Android 16). Best fit for fleet deployments where the Pro's premium specs aren't needed. Note the gap to the Pro has narrowed to roughly $157 as the non-Pro's street price firmed up to $399.99 — at that spread the Pro is the better buy for most field use. [src1]

### Best Hot-Swap / Thermal Field Work: RugOne Xever 7 Pro (~$660) — [Check price](https://knowledgelib.io/go/rugone-xever-7-pro)
The first phone to make battery swapping genuinely *hot*: an internal buffer cell keeps the phone powered for about a 3-minute window while you change the main 5,550mAh battery, with no restart and no lost app state. It ships with a **spare battery, two battery covers, and a folding charging station** that tops up the phone (28W) and a second battery at once — so a full shift on swaps needs no charger downtime at all. Dimensity 7025, 6.67-inch 120Hz AMOLED at 2,200 nits, IP68/IP69K, MIL-STD-810H, 6.5-foot drop rating, Android 15 with 3 OS generations promised. The Pro adds a FLIR Lepton 3.5 thermal sensor (-10°C to 450°C) plus a 64MP infrared night-vision camera, which is the reason to pay over the $529 non-Pro. At 325g it is a brick, and RugOne is an emerging brand with no iFixit teardown or repairability score — but nothing else here hot-swaps. [src12]

### Best US-Friendly Mid-Budget: Nokia G310 5G (~$99 renewed) — [Check price](https://knowledgelib.io/go/nokia-g310-5g)
The North-American HMD-branded equivalent of the Nokia G42. User-replaceable display, battery (5,000mAh), and charging port via the same iFixit partnership. Certified for major US 5G networks. Snapdragon 4 Gen 2, 6.56-inch 120Hz LCD. Best repair-first option that also works on US carriers without GSM compatibility caveats — **but new unlocked stock is gone** (MSRP was ~$190): the buy link now points to an Amazon Renewed unlocked TA-1573 at $98.72, the only live listing for the exact model. HMD retired the Nokia phone brand and no official G310 product page remains. Renewed units carry Amazon's 90-day guarantee; confirm the model code (TA-1573) and condition at checkout. [src1, src3]

### Best Ultra-Budget Classic Pop-Off: Nokia C12 (~$80) — [Check price](https://knowledgelib.io/go/nokia-c12)
True pop-off back, user-removable 3,000mAh battery — the classic experience, no tools. 6.3-inch HD+ display, Android 12 (Go edition), 2GB RAM, 64GB storage. Limited spec sheet, but unbeatable as a backup phone, kid's first phone, travel burner, or for users who simply refuse to live with sealed batteries. Note that some regional SKUs are non-removable; verify by model code before purchase, and note this GSM-only unit does not work on Verizon or AT&T. [src2]

Neither has a live Amazon US listing, so both buy links point to the manufacturer's own product page. [src10, src8, src2]

## Head-to-Head Comparisons

### Fairphone 6 vs HMD Skyline
The Fairphone 6 wins on longevity (8-year support vs 3-4 years) and repairability (10/10 vs 9/10) but costs nearly 2x more ($749 vs $400) and, in the US, only exists as Murena's de-Googled /e/OS edition — no Play Services, T-Mobile bands only, no Amazon listing. The Skyline matches almost all of the practical "DIY a battery in your kitchen" experience at half the price, with iFixit-supplied parts, guides, and ordinary Android. [src5, src6, src11]

**Pick Fairphone 6 if:** You will keep the phone 5+ years, value sustainability/ethical sourcing, and either live in the EU or actively want a de-Googled phone.
**Pick HMD Skyline if:** You're in the US, want Play Services and next-day stock, and prioritize value.

### Fairphone 6 vs Samsung Galaxy XCover7 Pro
The XCover7 Pro pairs a true tool-free pop-off back with flagship-tier 7-year support — uniquely good for shift workers who genuinely swap batteries. The Fairphone 6 wins on repairability of every other component, on display, and on ethical sourcing, and costs ~$192 more. Both run mid-range Snapdragon 7s Gen 3 silicon. Availability breaks the tie for most US buyers: the XCover7 Pro is one Amazon click, the FP6 is a Murena order. [src7, src6]

**Pick Fairphone 6 if:** You want full-device repair, not just battery swap, and you're a long-haul user.
**Pick XCover7 Pro if:** You need a rugged work phone with battery swaps during shifts, on US carriers.

### RugOne Xever 7 Pro vs Samsung Galaxy XCover7 Pro
The two rugged swappable-battery options, ~$103 apart. The Xever 7 Pro is the only phone here that swaps batteries *without powering down* — a buffer cell gives you ~3 minutes — and it includes a second 5,550mAh battery and a dual charging stand in the box, plus a FLIR thermal camera. The XCover7 Pro counters with 7 years of Samsung OS/security updates (vs 3), Knox and enterprise fleet tooling, pogo-pin dock support, a 100g-lighter body, and a brand that will still be servicing it in 2032. [src12, src7]

**Pick RugOne Xever 7 Pro if:** You need true hot-swap continuity, thermal imaging, or night vision — utilities, HVAC, inspection, search-and-rescue.
**Pick XCover7 Pro if:** You're deploying a managed fleet, need long support and carrier certification, and can accept a 30-second power-off swap.

### HMD Skyline vs Nokia G310 5G

## Key Market Trends (2026)

- **EU Battery Regulation 2023/1542 effective 18 February 2027**: Phones sold in the EU/EEA must have batteries removable using "commonly available tools," with replacement parts available for 5 years at "reasonable price." Drives every major OEM's roadmap; Apple has begun retrofit (iPhone 16/16 Plus ionic adhesive) but full flagship compliance remains pending. [src4, src9]
- **"User-replaceable" redefined**: The EU rule does not require pop-off backs — pull-tab adhesive plus a few screws (HMD Skyline, Fairphone 6, Nokia G42) is acceptable. True tool-free pop-off (Samsung XCover7 line, Nokia C12) is now the niche, not the norm. [src5, src4]
- **iFixit-OEM partnerships standardize repair access**: HMD (G42, G310, Skyline), Fairphone, Samsung Self-Service, Google, and Apple all now publish OEM-blessed iFixit guides + parts. iFixit-stocked battery prices: Skyline $30, G42 $25, Fairphone ~€40. [src5, src8]
- **Removable-battery flagships still don't exist**: No 2026 model from Apple, Samsung Galaxy S, Google Pixel, OnePlus, Xiaomi flagship line, or Sony Xperia offers user-removable batteries. The category remains mid-range, rugged, or budget-only. [src1, src2]
- **Long software support is the new selling point**: Fairphone 6 promises ~8 years; Samsung XCover7 line gets 7 years; HMD Skyline 3 years OS / 4 years security. Removable-battery buyers correlate strongly with long-keep behavior. [src6, src1]
- **The roster is shrinking faster than the EU rule is filling it**: Fairphone ended Fairphone 5 sales on 12 January 2026 and HMD retired the Nokia G42 5G, removing two of this category's flagship repairable phones inside a year — while the 2027 mandate that is supposed to replace them is still 19 months out. Buyers in 2026 face fewer options than in 2025, not more. [src10, src2]
- **Hot-swap arrives from the margins, not the majors**: RugOne's Xever 7 Pro (December 2025) is the first phone with a buffer cell that keeps it running through a battery change — no restart, ~3-minute window — and it ships with a spare battery as standard. The innovation came from an emerging rugged brand rather than Samsung, HMD, or Fairphone, which is where this category's genuinely new ideas now originate. [src12]
- **Apple's electrically-released adhesive**: iPhone 16 / 16 Plus battery debonds with a 9V battery + alligator clips applied for ~90 seconds. Pro / Pro Max still on stretch-release pull tabs. None qualifies as user-replaceable in the EU 2027 sense without further redesign. [src9]
