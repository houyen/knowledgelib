## Head-to-Head Comparisons

### EPICKA TA-105 Pro vs Anker Nano Travel Adapter
The TA-105 Pro is a 70W GaN powerhouse; the Anker Nano is a 20W compact for phones and tablets. The Anker is 43% smaller and ~$25 cheaper, but its 2-pin AC won't accept grounded plugs and its USB output throttles when sharing. The EPICKA accepts almost any AC plug, charges a MacBook Air at full speed, and has 200+ country plug coverage. Most travelers carrying a laptop should pay the extra $25. [src1, src9]

**Pick EPICKA TA-105 Pro if:** you need laptop-class USB-C PD, ground-pin AC compatibility, or charge 4+ devices simultaneously.
**Pick Anker Nano if:** you only charge phones/tablets, prize packing space, and don't need a grounded AC outlet.

### TESSAN Voyager 205 vs TESSAN 140W GaN
Same brand, different scale. The 140W is a 5-port single-rail adapter at $71; the Voyager 205 is an 8-port dual-rail flagship at $90-119. The Voyager's killer feature is parallel full-speed charging — the 140W single-rail will split its budget across active ports while the Voyager 205 keeps a 160W rail and a 45W rail independent. If you only need to fast-charge one laptop at a time, the 140W is enough. If you're charging a MacBook Pro + phone + tablet at the same time, only the Voyager 205 won't throttle. [src7, src8, src10]

**Pick TESSAN Voyager 205 if:** you charge 3+ high-draw devices in parallel or want futureproofing for PD 3.1 / multiple laptops.
**Pick TESSAN 140W if:** you charge one laptop at a time and want to save $20-50.

### EPICKA TA-105 Original vs EPICKA TA-105 Pro
Same brand, same family — but the Pro adds GaN, 70W PD, and 3 USB-C ports for ~2.5× the price ($20 vs $50). The Original maxes at standard USB-A speeds (no PD, no QC) and only one USB-C port. If you don't carry a USB-C laptop, the Original is the smarter buy — you're paying for fast charging you'll never use. [src3, src4, src9]

**Pick EPICKA TA-105 Pro if:** you carry any USB-C laptop or want future-proof fast charging.
**Pick EPICKA TA-105 Original if:** budget is the priority and you only charge phones/tablets.

### Ceptics 35W vs EPICKA TA-105 Pro
The Ceptics is a modular swappable-plug kit with surge protection; the EPICKA is a one-piece all-in-one. The Ceptics handles surge spikes on cruise ships and hotel outlets but its modular plugs can be lost or left behind. The EPICKA's integrated slide-out plugs are foolproof but offer no surge protection. Power output is similar (35W PD vs 70W PD), so for laptops the EPICKA is faster. [src2, src4]

**Pick Ceptics 35W if:** you carry surge-sensitive electronics (camera bodies, drone batteries) or travel on cruise ships.
**Pick EPICKA TA-105 Pro if:** you want a one-piece adapter with double the USB-C PD output.

## Important Caveats

- Travel adapters do NOT convert voltage. US/Canada 110-120V outlets differ from 220-240V in Europe/Asia. Ensure your devices support dual voltage (input: 100-240V) before plugging in.
- USB wattage is shared across ports on single-rail adapters. A 70W single-rail adapter splits power when charging multiple devices simultaneously; the Voyager 205's dual independent rails are the exception.
- No universal adapter covers every country. India (Type D) and South Africa (Type M) are commonly excluded.
- The Anker Nano's 2-pin AC outlet does NOT accept grounded 3-prong plugs (no EU Schuko, UK 3-pin, or grounded laptop bricks).
